# Lagos Danfo Racing — Android and iOS research and product design

**Updated:** 18 October 2026  
**Revision:** 18 — minimalist menus and GitHub product handoff, with driving as the main activity  
**Scope:** free-to-play Android and iOS danfo game; exactly two initial modes: open-world Work a Route and segmented Race; free production tools/assets with no new paid dependencies or engine royalties; no inherited micro-game size cap.  
**Status:** sourced research, product specification and implementation handoff. No playable game or device benchmark is included in this handoff.

## Direction and authority

The player acquires a personal danfo, works towards full ownership where a purchase balance is outstanding, and races rival danfos through Lagos. Aggressive overtaking, traffic decisions, bus personality and a recognisable city are central. The user has agreed that Race and Work a Route are separate modes sharing a bus and career. Passenger work, bus-buying and payment arrangements deepen the ownership experience.

The user's latest direction expands the previous Android-only brief to **Android and iOS**, confirms **free entry**, and asks for the fullest practical representation of danfo life and Lagos. This revision supersedes the earlier single-route prototype as the product's intended extent. A prototype is a development instrument, not the size of the finished game.

## 1. What the audience evidence actually says

### Verified survey findings

GeoPoll's 2024 report includes a Nigeria sample of **631**. In its country findings, **46% of Nigerian respondents said origin did not matter if a game was fun**, and **47% welcomed Black characters/heroes**. Across the four-country sample, racing appeared in the top-three genre choices of **34%**. These are different questions and denominators. [S24]

The pooled sample also reports free-game preference, affordability and data costs as obstacles. “Ever bought a game or an in-game item” is not a current payer-conversion rate; “bought a product after seeing a game ad” is not a rewarded-ad completion rate. The methodology's printed total is 2,588, while country counts sum to 2,558; use the country figure above and do not silently reconcile that inconsistency. [S24]

The 2025 study expanded to six markets. Its public landing page still says the report is forthcoming, while December 2025 industry reporting describes a release. Full country tables were not obtained here; secondary pooled findings are corroboration, not a substitute for Nigeria data. [S25]

Carry1st/Newzoo's February 2025 release estimates Nigeria's 2024 games revenue at $300 million. That is a broad market estimate, not revenue available to this game or evidence of a specific danfo-racing audience. [S26]

### Audience segments to design and test separately

These are proposed research segments rather than demographic facts inferred about every Nigerian:

1. **Lagos-familiar players:** recognise roads, voices, distances and everyday details; likely to spot geographic mistakes.
2. **Nigerian players elsewhere:** understand much of the language and humour but may not know a specific Lagos junction.
3. **Racing-focused players:** judge steering, speed, collision fairness, rivals and progression.
4. **Bus enthusiasts and customisers:** value mechanical character, interiors, horn choice, paint and vehicle collection.
5. **Diaspora players:** may value recognisable places and sounds; their spending or retention advantage is unproven.
6. **Players with modest devices or intermittent connectivity:** need the same race outcomes and content access with an appropriate performance profile.

Test across genders, ages, spending histories and device classes. Do not equate the audience with young men on expensive phones, or assume all local players want the same slang, music or difficulty.

## 2. The revised game identity

**Player promise:** Get your danfo, earn through racing or passenger work, clear its purchase balance, make it yours, and build your reputation across Lagos.

The racing should stand on four connected pleasures:
- **Mastery:** judge the gap, turn and braking point.
- **Ownership:** recognise your bus by its sound, handling, appearance and history.
- **Place:** know where you are from the road structure and everyday life.
- **Rivalry:** remember who beat you and want another race.

An ordinary session:
1. Resume in the player's garage or open Race directly.
2. Choose an available race, career event or challenge.
3. See the route, conditions, rivals and expected duration.
4. Race with clear position and finish information, or serve passengers with clear stop and journey progress.
5. Receive the earned event income and career progress.
6. Optionally view a highlight, choose a bonus reward, customise or rematch.

Basic racing does not depend on an ad filling, a payment, a fuel timer or a new online login.

### How the product ties together

**One personal danfo, one Lagos world, one ownership career, two ways to play.** The bus and the player's ambitions connect the game; individual modes supply different immediate pleasures.

The main menu shows the selected bus, naira balance, one chosen next goal/ownership indicator and two dominant play buttons: **Work** (passengers) and **Race**. **My Bus** and a small **Settings** entry are secondary. Account/history and career details are contextual or expandable sections inside My Bus, not extra top-level destinations or modes. The Lagos map is a connected driving space for passenger work; selected competitive races use defined courses within that same setting. Job/course selection occurs through clear menus without requiring a physical visit to a 3D hub.

The proposed full loop:
1. **Choose a goal:** make the next instalment, improve the bus, unlock a district or earn a chosen appearance.
2. **Choose a mode:** Race for competitive driving and prizes, or Work a Route for passenger service and fares.
3. **Complete an event:** each mode retains its own rules, records and clear end point.
4. **Receive the outcome:** base money and career progress are saved automatically; a relevant optional ad reward may add to them.
5. **Advance the same career:** settle a played shift, make an upgrade, claim an unlock or personalise the bus.
6. **Choose the next drive:** replay for mastery, take another job or switch modes with the same bus and balance.

One earned currency, **naira (₦)**, supports fares, race awards, operating costs, vehicle agreements, mechanical development and the collection. It is game currency with no cash-out or real-money purchase. Reputation and personal records express achievement rather than becoming another spendable premium balance. Core district access remains earnable through either preferred mode.

The initial ownership journey has a beginning and an achievable handover. After it, a favourite-bus build, new district rivalry, another vehicle or optional fleet chapter supplies the next ambition. Challenges and authored variation keep familiar roads worth revisiting between major updates.

For example: complete a passenger shift, put earnings towards the next payment, tune the brakes, enter a sprint with that same bus, then return to the garage to see both activities reflected in one career. A player who prefers one mode can keep progressing through it.

All routes, buses and ordinary upgrades belong to the free progression design. Advertising earns revenue around completed play and voluntary reward choices. Bus purchase instalments are paid with game earnings; the product does not ask the player to pay real money.


### Initial release boundary

**Confirmed user decision, 18 October 2026:** Start with exactly two modes: Race and Work a Route. Arcade and Explore are excluded. The broader conversation about endless traffic, discovery activities, playable conductor work and management does not authorise those as initial features.

Keep the shared personal bus, garage upgrades/customisation, earned currency, ownership payments, researched Lagos world and the advertising-funded model. Work a Route requires connected, freely navigable streets within the playable city, rather than isolated pickup/drop-off levels. Race uses defined courses and its own event boundaries within the same geographic foundation. Actual city coverage is a content-planning decision; the twelve-area framework remains a research/content map, not an obligation to finish twelve areas before the starting product can ship.

Race formats below are variations within Race, not additional main modes. The conductor handles passenger work automatically in Work a Route. A standalone conductor mode, fleet-management mode, restoration minigame and hybrid passenger race are outside the initial release. Later expansion requires a separate decision; it is not a committed backlog.

### Proposed race formats

| Format | Player objective | Lagos/danfo contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Point-to-point sprint | Beat rivals to a finish | Learn a coherent route and contest traffic gaps |
| District circuit | Win over multiple laps | Build knowledge of bends, queue edges and alternative lines |
| Time attack | Improve a personal time | Master the bus without rival contact |
| Elimination | Avoid being last at scheduled checkpoints | Sustained pressure without a single early mistake deciding everything |
| Dense field race | Beat a closely matched pack of rival danfos | Contest narrow overtaking space and defend a line |
| Conditions event | Race a selected route in rain, evening or other authored conditions | Familiar roads create new decisions |

Proposed ordinary sprint length is roughly **90–180 seconds**. Longer races and circuits can serve players who want them. This is a test range, not a discovered Nigerian preference.

Variation belongs within the two selected modes: routes, rivals, conditions and passenger jobs. Race formats retain the same driving foundation; the initial release has no Explore or Arcade entry.

### Two modes, one career

**Confirmed user direction, 18 October 2026:** Separate racing from passenger work and connect both through the same personal bus and career. This supersedes revision 4's proposal to make passenger service an ordinary race format.

| Mode | Immediate objective | Earnings | Career contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Race | Beat rival danfos or a personal time | Race awards | Racing reputation, districts, mastery and money |
| Work a Route | Pick up passengers, serve destinations and complete a shift | Completed-journey fares | Route reputation, operating milestones and money |

The player chooses the mode from the garage or main screen. Both contribute to the same earned-currency balance and hire-purchase agreement. Each has its own records and objectives. Ordinary racing remains accessible without first completing a passenger shift; choosing one mode does not require abandoning the other.

### Passenger pickups and drop-offs

**User-directed feature:** Include passenger service as a full play mode. The details below are proposals requiring reference work and playtesting.

#### Work a Route

1. **Choose a job.** Preview the advertised service route, destinations, estimated duration and fare rules. Start with a readable job in a connected district before introducing longer and more demanding work.
2. **Pull in for passengers.** Stop at an eligible roadside boarding area or bus park. The door opens automatically, waiting passengers board and the conductor calls the destination.
3. **Work the open road network.** Choose how to reach the service destinations, judge traffic and decide whether to collect eligible optional passengers when spaces are available. Navigation suggests a path; valid alternative streets remain usable. Other danfos can compete for optional business.
4. **Serve requested drop-offs.** Advance voice and visual cues identify where passengers want to leave. Stop within the appropriate roadside segment; passengers alight and completed-journey fares are credited.
5. **Finish the shift.** Show completed journeys, fares, any disclosed operating expenses and the amount retained. The existing played-shift settlement handles the owner payment.

This mode measures successful service and earnings rather than declaring the first bus to a finish line the winner. Other buses, changing demand and traffic create urgency. A short self-contained shift provides a useful end point and a clear next-job choice.

#### Open-world passenger work and segmented racing

**Confirmed world structure:** Work a Route is open-world driving with ongoing passenger-service goals. The player navigates a connected Lagos road network with junctions, alternative streets, bus parks and roadside boarding areas. A pickup or drop-off completes an interaction within that world, not a separate level that automatically ends the drive.

The accepted job still advertises a service route/destinations, so customers remain compatible with the chosen danfo service rather than becoming arbitrary taxi requests anywhere on the map. Required destinations stay clear; the player chooses viable streets between them. A suggested route is guidance, not a rail or a mandatory sequence of turns. Taking a different street does not automatically fail the job: update navigation towards the remaining required destinations. Boarding remains tied to eligible, readable roadside areas and bus capacity.

Keep the city geographically consistent as the player changes streets and jobs. Completed work can lead into another job in the same world, or a return to the bus park/garage. Save position, direction, bus, current job, passengers and destinations so returning to the app preserves the ongoing drive. Define understandable playable-city boundaries and keep required destinations reachable within installed content.

Open-world continuity and event accounting coexist. A full job/shift retains its disclosed completion condition and a safe, stationary settlement point. Individual pickups, drop-offs, wrong turns, street changes and revisits do not become extra events or ad opportunities. Complete and settle the agreed work, show its results, then continue or choose the next activity. Do not interrupt an active journey or force a reset after each customer.

Race is a discrete event using a defined course, start/grid, checkpoint/branch rules, finish and results. Its active course contains the player and rival danfos, with roadside life outside it. Reuse the city geography/assets while isolating the race population from normal open-world traffic. Explicit mode transitions preserve career state and correctly suspend/complete work state; unfinished customers must not silently become racing passengers or generate fares from the race result.

#### Everyday route rhythm

Use multiple researched roadside boarding positions and requested drop-off segments along each corridor, alongside major stops. Passengers may flag the bus from eligible waiting areas; destination requests can arise between the major terminals. This gives the shift its repeated stop, board, move, request, drop-off rhythm.

The player has a forgiving stopping area around an eligible location rather than needing to hit a single exact point. Passenger positions and walking paths are authored to match the road. Generate demand ahead of the bus with sufficient notice; requests do not appear too late to serve. Already boarded passengers keep their destination through save/resume.

The conductor handles fares, change and destination calls. This remains a driving-and-service mode with its own earnings objective, separate from contact-heavy racing.

#### Approved passenger job decisions and regular customers

The job board shows estimated active duration, the main stop sequence, a useful authored demand cue and disclosed earning rules before a shift starts. Demand cues describe the game's forecast, such as a busy departure point or stronger demand at a later stop; they do not imply a live Lagos data feed. Preserve the accepted job specification and destinations for the current shift.

During driving, show the next required drop-off, available seats and nearby eligible pickup information. Choosing optional walk-up customers uses seats that might otherwise serve later demand. The conductor handles fares and routine communication; the player need not sort a manifest or negotiate each fare. Required journeys stay clear and viable whatever optional business is collected.

Include a small recurring passenger cast with stable identities, appearances, valid route contexts and concise authored stories. Familiar people recognise the bus or conductor on later played shifts. Story steps advance after the relevant journey completes, with saved event IDs to prevent duplicate progression on replay/reload. Ordinary fare and seating rules apply. Greetings yield to service cues, remain optional to hear/read and do not turn every stop into a cutscene. No real-calendar attendance is required.

#### Street population and visible vehicle occupants

**Recommended presentation in response to the user's people/modelling questions:** Race has a clear active course and a populated roadside. Work a Route has normal traffic, waiting customers and visible bus occupants. These are production requirements proposed for this game, not implemented features or measured engagement findings.

| Actor | Race | Work a Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player and rival danfo drivers | Visible seated drivers with hands at the wheel and restrained steering/reaction animation | Visible seated drivers with the same shared character system |
| Player's conductor | Remains aboard for identity and occasional commentary; stays inside during racing | Handles destination calls, door/service cues and fare presentation automatically |
| Fare-paying passengers | No customer load or passenger-service tasks | Visible waiting characters become visible seated passengers and later alight |
| Drivers in ordinary traffic vehicles | No civilian traffic on the active course | Simple seated drivers in nearby visible vehicles; use cheaper representations with distance |
| Roadside people | Walking, waiting, trading or watching outside the racing line | Location-appropriate street activity, with eligible customers in authored waiting areas |

The race should not look like an abandoned city. Place roadside activity along credible pavements, setbacks, market edges and other off-course spaces. Separate background walking paths and spawn rules from the racing course so people do not wander into it. Avoid filling every metre with a crowd. Drivers and the conductor retain their own seats; neither consumes a fare-paying passenger place. The camera should reveal occupancy through suitable windows/door views without requiring a new interior camera mode.

#### Boarding, seats and alighting

Use a short automatic sequence while preserving the player's normal driving camera:

1. **Accept the stopping position.** The bus is stationary inside a forgiving eligible segment, its passenger door faces the waiting area, and the approach to the door is clear. A clear prompt guides correction when the bus is badly aligned.
2. **Open and unload.** Animate the sliding side door appropriate to the selected bus. Passengers for this destination leave first, stepping down and following an authored path away from the doorway.
3. **Reserve and approach.** Reserve a specific free seat before inviting a waiting passenger. The character walks to a queue point and then to the door; a full bus cannot invite more people than its researched passenger capacity.
4. **Enter and sit.** Use a brief step-up/duck animation matched to the door and bus floor, then a short move into the assigned seat and a seated pose. Preserve the character's appearance. The doorway and interior may naturally conceal part of a transition, but people must not visibly pop into seats or pass through solid bodywork.
5. **Complete service and depart.** Update occupancy once for each completed transfer. The conductor handles routine fares/calls. Close the door when the doorway is clear; departure becomes available after service and closure.

For requested drop-offs, give advance cues, then use the same stopping checks and the reverse seat-to-door-to-roadside sequence. Passengers keep their destination and assigned seat between stops. Seat anchors belong to the bus so occupants move with it; seated occupants need restrained idle/lean motion rather than independent walking physics. Do not require a full money-counting animation or a cinematic camera cut for every fare.

Keep transfers brisk, varying idle gestures, calls and clothing rather than making every stop a long scene. Overlap waiting/approach motions only where paths remain clear; never send boarding and alighting characters through one another in the doorway. Exact timings and simultaneous transfer limits require playtesting. Author and validate stop approaches, with a bounded retry for a temporarily blocked path so a passenger cannot leave the bus locked forever. An interrupted transfer retains its state and seat reservation; it does not create a second passenger or a second fare after reload. Individual transfers and stops remain outside ad placement boundaries.

#### Building the stops

Lagos references include both major stations and street-corner boarding. Represent a range of researched roadside waiting places, junction approaches, market edges and bus parks. A modern shelter/sign is appropriate only where location evidence supports it. [S1]

Each reusable stop scene contains:

- **Physical setting:** the relevant kerb, verge or frontage, surface wear, drainage edge, nearby shops/props and any referenced sign or shelter. Use original or cleared assets and fictional commercial graphics.
- **Service segment:** an invisible, forgiving stopping zone with direction and door-side checks. Show restrained UI guidance when needed rather than a large permanent luminous square in the street.
- **People positions:** waiting/queue points, an entrance approach and separate exit-clearance points. Reserve enough clear ground for the bus door; do not route customers through a stall, open drain or solid wall.
- **Stop data:** name, route direction, accepted destinations, passenger demand and drop-off eligibility. The physical stop is reusable; demand, time, weather and roadside activity vary by shift.

Build these from modular ground/frontage/prop pieces and named path/door anchors, then validate each placement against the actual bus geometry. Stops on the same road need not look or behave identically. A bus park supports multiple waiting groups and bays; an ordinary roadside point can be a small waiting patch with no shelter at all.

#### Character production and mobile cost

Create original or explicitly cleared modular characters in Blender: a small set of compatible body rigs, body silhouettes, clothing, hair and carried-item variants with locally reviewed combinations. Share walk, wait, enter, exit, sit, drive and conductor animation sets where their proportions permit. Swapping colours alone is insufficient variety, while unique bespoke people for every street would add little visible value.

Prioritise readable people beside the bus and at the doorway. Use simpler seated figures inside nearby vehicles, cheaper meshes/animation updates at distance and culling when truly hidden. Keep the authoritative passenger count and seat assignments independent of visual detail, so a crowded bus does not become logically empty when its occupants are culled. Establish crowd/occupant budgets on representative phones rather than declaring an untested NPC count safe.

In the proposed Godot stack, navigation supplies paths while game scripts move characters; AnimationTree can blend the authored movement/boarding/seated states. Bus-door alignment, seat reservation, transfers and fare completion still require custom game logic. [S64, S65] Use ordinary character scenes for animated people; do not assume the repeated static-prop MultiMesh technique supplies independent character rigs or seat transitions. [S62]

#### Keep the driving readable

- The conductor handles collection, change and routine dialogue automatically. The player steers, accelerates/brakes and chooses stops.
- Show the next required drop-off and remaining spaces. Capacity follows the selected bus's researched layout.
- Required passengers alight before new passengers fill their seats. Boarding and alighting use predictable short animations, with departure available after service and door closure.
- Already boarded passengers retain their destination. Missing a stop leaves a visible, viable recovery path; an unfinished journey does not award a delivery fare.
- Required tutorial/story pickups are reserved so ambient rivals cannot make an assigned job impossible. Competition for optional walk-up passengers does not remove an essential objective.
- Subtitles and visual destination cues preserve usability with voices muted.
- Waiting passengers use authored pavement and stop positions with readable boarding paths.
- Keep detailed manifests in the preview or pause screen; driving needs only actionable stop and occupancy information.

Exact calls, layouts, passenger behaviour, boarding duration, capacity references and route lengths require Lagos research and playtesting. The 90–180-second sprint proposal does not automatically apply to multi-stop shifts.

#### Connect fares to ownership

Passenger fares and race awards appear as distinct earning sources in the shared career. Successfully completed journeys retain their base fares even if a later journey is unfinished. Fares are earned, non-cashable game currency and are granted once per completed journey.

Save the event ID, current stop, passenger IDs and appearances, destinations, assigned/reserved seats, transfer states, occupancy, completed journeys and settled fares. Boarding, resetting a stop or reloading a save cannot create duplicate people, occupied seats or money.

#### Separate mode rules

Racing and passenger work remain distinct. A race does not require passenger deliveries, and a passenger shift is judged by its own service objectives. Hybrid events are outside the initial release.

#### Event boundaries

A complete passenger shift is one event. Individual stops, boarding actions and fares are not completed races or independent ad opportunities. The proposed commercial treatment for whole shifts is set out in section 8; no ads interrupt driving or passenger service.

## 3. Danfo systems that belong in the game

### Understanding the driver's working life

**User direction, 18 October 2026:** The experience should help players understand the life of a danfo driver to a substantial extent. Hire purchase is a central example, and all game money is denominated in naira (₦). This expands the product's experiential purpose, while preserving Race and Work a Route as its two modes.

Represent economic decisions, skilled teamwork, local knowledge, relationships, setbacks, personal identity and ambitions. Everyday driving belongs in Work a Route; ownership, business accounts, upkeep and personal stories use supporting menus/results, with conductor/passenger activity during ordinary driving and stops. Competitive racing is an authored game interpretation; its prize income is not presented as the ordinary way real drivers finance buses. Do not imply that every driver has the same contract, family, beliefs or working practices.

#### What the sources support

FIJ's 2022 interviews describe owner remittances, fuel costs, paying a conductor, park/roadside collections, restricted take-home earnings and family responsibilities. These are individual historical accounts, not today's universal cost schedule. The conductor, owner, park collector and traffic official are distinct roles. [S2]

Bird's 2022 interviews include hire purchase, independence through work, family support, bus inscriptions and returning lost belongings. These support depicting dignity and service alongside economic pressure. The article's financial amounts and individual claims are historical and should not be copied into the game as current prices or universal facts. [S3]

A 2025 Alimosho study's author abstract identifies fuel use, running costs, operating hours, maintenance knowledge, congestion and enforcement interference as factors affecting profitability in that study area. The abstract was read through TRID's authorised reproduction; direct full-text retrieval failed. It is a geographically bounded study, not proof of conditions on every Lagos route. [S70]

#### Further incorporation proposals

The user has confirmed the working-life objective and naira currency. The following details are researched recommendations, rather than approval to introduce compulsory new deductions, maintenance chores, extra modes or monetization changes.

| Working-life detail | How it can appear in the existing game | What a player would understand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual take-home earnings | A short end-of-shift account showing fares, disclosed operating costs, the appropriate owner payment and retained money | Cash collected is gross revenue; the driver's retained amount comes after obligations |
| Driver–conductor partnership | Persistent named conductor, destination calls, capacity coordination, brief change/fare exchanges and an explained pay arrangement | The conductor supplies skilled labour and is part of the business, not just comic commentary |

#### Economic presentation and scope boundaries

The clearest teaching moment is a compact naira settlement: **passenger fares − applicable fuel/crew/park/upkeep costs − the current contract's owner payment = retained shift earnings**. Show hire-purchase balance reduction separately. Do not charge an ordinary rental remittance and a hire-purchase instalment as though they were automatically the same obligation. Keep fare income and fictional race awards distinct in the ledger.

Any new cost mechanism needs an explicit economy decision, previewed rules and tuning that keeps ordinary free progression satisfying. Existing expenses and owner payments remain applied once, including when a rewarded bonus is claimed. Naira denomination itself changes labels and accounting units, not ad rewards or their eligibility rules. Game fares, prices and costs will be tuned and disclosed as game values rather than advertised as current real-world financial information.

Authenticity can use short contextual scenes and decisions instead of adding full maintenance, household or conductor modes. Compress loading/traffic time without erasing its consequences. Keep normal fare handling automatic and core play immediately accessible; there is no fuel-energy paywall or new ad placement. Historical reports of coercive collections can inform carefully labelled fictional stories without presenting every fee as legitimate tax or every official as corrupt. Any present-day legal/fee details require current primary checking before inclusion.

The daily rhythm is menu job selection, work the route with the conductor and visible passengers, make useful demand/seat decisions, settle the completed shift, then choose a My Bus/Career action, another shift or a race. It requires no separate 3D hub visit or long routine before each race. Real-world absence does not create new arrears, household penalties or lost ownership.

### The player's bus and garage

The latest user direction adds bus acquisition and payment to the career. The proposed default starts with a clearly identified hire-purchase bus under the player's control; full ownership is the early career goal. The garage distinguishes a bus being paid off from an outright-owned bus. This replaces the earlier proposal to begin with outright ownership. The acquisition system below governs the contract, payments and transfer.

The My Bus/garage menu supports:
- Body panels, paint, original slogans, decals, wheels and cabin appearance.
- A set of researched engine/horn identities.
- Clear mechanical upgrades and sidegrades earned through races.
- Before/after inspection, vehicle history and favourite setups.
- Distinctive buses without making expensive variants compulsory for fair competition.
- Presets for players who enjoy racing more than editing.

**Approved bus setups and history:** Provide several viable handling setups with clear trade-offs, including responsive acceleration, steady cornering and forgiving braking where supported by the selected bus/controller. Describe their differences in plain language. Offer a free setup preview on an existing Race route before committing earned game currency. This is part of the garage/Race flow, not a third mode or a new ad offer. Preview state restores the prior setup on exit unless the player confirms the change.

Each bus retains a stable identity and visible history: ownership progress/handover, completed route milestones and selected race/service achievements. Earned original route plates and other cosmetic mementos can appear in the cabin or garage. Record each milestone once and preserve history through paint/setup changes, interruptions and updates. Acquiring a later bus does not erase the first bus's record.

Mechanical progression can exist in career. Competitive time trials and any ranked races should use matched classes or equalised setups. Acquiring a cosmetic appearance must not alter collision size, visibility advantages or race power.

Repair can express care for the bus, but routine mistakes should not impose a real-time wait or a purchase requirement. Restore ordinary race readiness automatically or through an immediate, affordable earned-currency action. A visually battered bus may remain the player's chosen style.

### Bus acquisition, “delivery” and the road to ownership

**User-directed addition, 18 October 2026:** Represent how people obtain danfos and pay for them. Include the distinction between working an owner's bus and paying towards ownership.

The reporting includes a driver who acquired his first bus through hire purchase and later operated several buses, alongside drivers making daily remittances and an owner who acquired a bus through a cooperative. Those are different arrangements, not one universal deal. Historical prices in the interviews are not current market prices. [S2, S3]

#### Distinct arrangements

| Arrangement | What it means in the game | Does paying automatically reduce a purchase balance? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily/weekly delivery to an owner | Work a bus belonging to somebody else and remit an agreed amount | No; a rental/remittance arrangement is not automatically a purchase |
| Hire purchase | Control a specific bus while paying a disclosed agreed total over instalments | Yes; eligible instalments reduce the remaining purchase balance |
| Outright purchase | Buy an available bus with accumulated earned game currency | The price is paid and ownership transfers immediately |
| Cooperative-assisted acquisition | An optional story/contract route reflecting the cooperative example in the research | Only according to the specific disclosed game contract |

This table is a simplified game representation, not a statement of legal terms or financial advice for real transactions.

#### Proposed main career

1. **Inspect the bus and read the owner's offer.** Establish its condition, personality and the deal through a concise menu card and the existing bus preview. The character has already saved the initial game-currency deposit so the player can race promptly.
2. **Take a clear hire-purchase deal.** Show the agreed total, deposit, remaining balance and payment rule. The player knows this is the bus they are working to own.
3. **Choose how to earn.** Race events pay race awards; Work a Route shifts pay completed-journey fares. Both advance the same ownership career. Funding ownership partly through fictional race prizes is a creative adaptation, not a claim about ordinary danfo business.
4. **Pay down the balance.** A transparent part of earnings goes towards instalments at played working-day/shift settlements. The garage shows payments made and balance remaining.
5. **Reach the final payment.** A brief ownership card/message marks the handover. The bus's status changes permanently to Fully owned, and that owner's instalment obligation ends. A separate 3D owner or handover animation is unnecessary.
6. **Choose the next ambition.** Keep and develop the first bus, save for another outright, or take a clearly separate deal for a second bus.

The second-bus path can later let the player hire a fictional driver and experience the owner's side of remittances. It is an optional career extension. It must preserve the difference between rental income and a driver purchasing that vehicle.

#### Make paying off the bus feel worthwhile

- Show a visible ownership milestone after settlements.
- Give the owner and conductor reactions to the player's progress.
- Let the player see and personalise the bus while paying it off.
- Make a well-maintained bus, a decorated bus and a deliberately weathered bus valid expressions.
- Let full ownership improve the character's take-home position because the purchase instalment ends.
- Keep the first bus relevant after it is fully paid for.
- Present a voluntary extra payment as a choice between clearing the balance sooner and another use of earned currency.

The “working day” advances through gameplay. Missing real-world days does not create arrears while the app is closed. There is no real-time interest or surprise increase to the agreed total in the proposed baseline.

A shortfall can produce owner dialogue, an extension or an optional additional earning event. Ordinary progression must retain a free way to continue. Irreversible confiscation and punitive debt spirals are not required for the main career.

#### Settlement display

Use a compact, understandable settlement:
- Earnings from the played shift, with passenger fares and race awards shown separately.
- Clearly disclosed game operating expenses, where this mode uses them.
- Agreed owner payment.
- Personal balance retained.
- Hire-purchase balance still outstanding, if applicable.

A payment for renting an owner's bus does not appear on the purchase-progress bar. Park dues, conductor costs and repairs do not quietly count as bus-purchase instalments either.

All amounts are earned, non-cashable game currency. No real loan, real bus transaction or real-money debt repayment is offered. Optional rewarded earnings can help progression, and the base economy supports ownership through normal play. Revision 6's advertising-funded model supersedes earlier cosmetic-purchase proposals; exact ad pacing remains a testable proposal.

Amounts, instalment size and the number of played shifts to ownership remain economy-tuning decisions. Do not import a 2015 bus price or an interview's repayment duration as the correct 2026 price or enjoyable game duration.

#### Ownership state and acceptance checks

Persist the vehicle ID, contract type, agreed total, deposit, instalment ledger, remaining purchase balance and ownership state.

- A hire-purchase payment reduces the correct bus balance once.
- A daily rental remittance does not create ownership progress.
- A duplicate settlement callback cannot debit or credit twice.
- The final payment is clamped to the remaining balance; any excess stays with the player.
- Full ownership transfers once and permanently removes that contract's future instalments.
- An outright-owned bus is not silently converted back into somebody else's property.
- Quitting, updating or restoring cannot erase recorded instalments.
- A paid-off bus can retain its personalisation, upgrades and history.
- Real-world inactivity does not generate unpaid working-day charges.
- Other optional vehicle contracts cannot seize or re-encumber the player's first fully owned bus.

## 4. Racing behavior and fairness

Recklessness comes from speed, close passes, contested space and forceful decisions. It does not require invisible collisions or unavoidable random failures.

### Traffic and passenger rules by mode

**Recommended initial race rule, following the user's stated preference:** Use only the player and rival danfos on the active racing course. The user explicitly wants normal road cars in passenger work. This is a design recommendation for the first version, not a measured claim about which traffic setup maximises retention.

| Mode | Vehicles on the active driving route | Passenger activity | City presentation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Race | The player and competing danfos; no non-competing cars, trucks, motorcycles or buses | No fare-paying passenger load, pickup/drop-off jobs or pedestrian crossings into the racing line | Researched Lagos roads, landmarks and roadside scenery; background people/activity kept outside the course |
| Work a Route | Normal location-appropriate cars and other road vehicles alongside danfos | Roadside pickups, requested drop-offs, fares and automatic conductor handling | Everyday street activity and traffic appropriate to the selected corridor |

Show seated drivers in the player and rival race buses. Keep the player's conductor aboard for identity/commentary, inside the bus during racing. There is no fare-paying customer load or passenger-service obligation. Background roadside people remain visible outside the active course, with no pedestrian paths or spawners feeding the racing line.

The intended racing pleasure is direct rivalry: identify the bus contesting a gap, push back, defend a line and recover a position. Narrow geometry, field size, rival behaviour and the contact model provide intensity. A road-only personal time attack follows its event rules without civilian traffic.

Use the same environment assets with separate actor manifests per mode. Race setups clear the course of non-competing moving vehicles and parked vehicles that would intrude into the racing line. Background scenery can remain beyond it, with no traffic or pedestrian system feeding actors onto the course. Work a Route enables its normal traffic and passenger schedules.

Switching modes through event handling preserves career money and ownership while keeping work passengers, fares and race results in their correct event state. A racing scene must not inherit an active customer load or civilian traffic spawner from passenger work.

Keep race variation focused on route, rivals, grid/field composition and conditions. No ambient-traffic race is added to the initial scope by this recommendation. Ad placements and spacing are unchanged.


### Contact-heavy racing and tight roads

**Confirmed user direction, 18 October 2026:** Racing should be brutal and physical, including buses bashing and squeezing past each other on tight roads. The following is an authored racing interpretation, not a claim that every Lagos driver or road behaves the same way.

- Rivals contest gaps, hold a line, squeeze alongside, sideswipe and make forceful overtakes. Give them readable aggression and the same contact vulnerabilities as the player.
- Side scrapes produce metal/paint sound, visible body movement, dust and suitable scrape/dent states. A shove affects heading and momentum; a heavy hit creates a stronger, understandable recovery.
- Keep the bus substantial: suspension lean, tyre grip, braking and engine response communicate weight. Contact should support continued racing rather than turn an ordinary scrape into an instant terminal crash.
- Tune repeated contact, low-speed pile-ups and wall scrapes so buses do not stick together, clip through geometry or acquire explosive impulses.
- Use narrow streets, junction pinch points and close rival packs for racing pressure. Market frontage and physical road edges remain readable; non-competing or parked vehicles do not intrude into the racing line. Passenger work can use normal queues, roadside parking and merging traffic.
- Preserve recognisable bridge and arterial proportions. The common experience is contested, limited usable space, with location-specific road widths rather than identical corridors everywhere.
- The camera and collision shapes must agree on whether a gap is usable. Place obstructions with enough lead-in for a meaningful decision, and provide recovery from a blocked position.

Proposed damage presentation uses authored scrape/dent variants, shaders and controlled secondary motion. Full structural destruction and soft-body deformation are not assumed engine features or initial requirements.

This contact model belongs to Race. Work a Route keeps the same bus physics and city detail while directing play towards picking up and delivering passengers.


Proposed invariants:
- The visible bus and usable gap agree with the collision model.
- Traffic becomes visible early enough for the selected speed and device.
- A merge has a readable lead-in.
- Rivals obey the same basic road geometry and collision rules.
- Minor contact has a predictable cost.
- Major crashes recover in a clear, repeatable way.
- A small early mistake does not make every race irretrievable.
- The game never deliberately makes a player lose to sell an item or ad.
- Road surfaces and rival field composition change racing decisions without erasing player control; normal traffic density is a passenger-mode variable.

A horn can affect suitable ambient traffic through an authored response. It should not magically clear every obstacle. Different cosmetic horn choices have the same authored gameplay effect.

A player bus's handling can vary by weight, acceleration, braking, suspension and traction. Actual tuning values require references and playtesting. No quantitative real-world performance was measured here.

### Controls and presentation proposal

Provide a clearly explained assisted control scheme plus alternatives:
- Touch steering with automatic acceleration and a reachable brake.
- Tilt or steering-wheel controls for players who prefer them.
- Optional manual acceleration and gears for experienced players.
- Adjustable sensitivity, left-handed placement and reduced camera movement.
- Controller support where practical.

One-finger play remains an option to investigate, not a restriction on the whole product. The previous brief never established it as a requirement for this game.

Cabin, chase and bonnet/forward views can serve different tastes if they preserve legibility. Visual quality can scale while traffic logic and scoring remain equivalent. The recommended free engine foundation is set out in section 10; final art treatment and measured performance remain unselected.

**Approved handling and onboarding requirements:** Maintain immediate, readable steering under controlled bus weight/body movement. Differentiate tyre feedback, side scrapes, shoves and heavy impacts, with understandable recovery and optional restrained haptics. Tune input response, camera comfort and repeated contact before scaling content. The first playable introduction teaches steering, braking and recovery through driving; explain ownership when the first earned outcome gives it context. Work a Route teaches stopping, door service and destination cues within that mode. Either mode can be entered without completing the other's tutorial. Save sensitivity, control-placement and camera-comfort preferences.

### Constructing Lagos and maximising variety

**Design response, 18 October 2026:** Build reusable, district-specific environments for Race and Work a Route. The two-mode boundary remains unchanged. The proposal below is a production method; no city generator or 3D environment has been implemented.

#### A stable city foundation

For each selected corridor, prepare a route record with road connections, elevations, junction movements, landmark positions, usable road width, traffic paths and eligible passenger-stop areas. Give each route segment an environment profile with allowed assets, placement sockets, density ranges, material palettes and sound zones. Profiles are based on that specific corridor; a district can contain several profiles rather than one visual stereotype.

For example, a researched market frontage can favour contiguous stalls and loading activity; an industrial approach can favour large frontage, trucks and warehouses; another segment can use fenced homes or modern offices. Each choice must fit the selected location's evidence.

Hand-adjust landmark areas and visibly repetitive sequences. Review neighbouring sections together, since a convincing individual block can still repeat conspicuously along a whole route. Camera-facing detail receives more attention than unseen interiors.

Each combination must remain compatible with its route and vehicle. Passengers already onboard keep their destinations, and ongoing events retain their setup through resume.

#### Count meaningful variety

Track distinct road/junction sequences, decision points, visual landmark sequences and service patterns separately from cosmetic combinations. Several paint schemes do not create several distinct routes. No unique-level total is claimed from multiplying the tables above.

Prefer additions that change what the player sees or decides: another recognisable frontage sequence, a different merge, an alternative valid race path, or a passenger job that changes stopping choices. Filter repetitive variants and combinations that close required paths or make service impossible.

#### Keep it suitable for phones

Reuse meshes and materials, vary supported instance data and load nearby route sections. Godot's MultiMesh can reduce draw overhead for repeated meshes, but grouping everything into a single city-wide batch weakens culling. Partition decorative instance batches by street section; collision and passenger navigation remain explicit separate systems. [S62]

Visual profiles reduce texture/detail/effect cost while retaining event geometry and gameplay outcomes. More variety should come chiefly from recombination and authored layout choices rather than loading a completely unique heavy model for every roadside object.

#### Rights-controlled source use

The production plan uses original models/art, owned or appropriately licensed recordings, CC0 reusable assets and licensed map data. Use original fictional business names, advertising graphics and slogans; individually verify any proposed real logo, third-party landmark model, artwork or other protected material before inclusion.

For OSM, retain attribution and keep the derived road-data layer separately versioned. Distribution must meet the applicable ODbL database/share-alike and source-access obligations; treating the scene as a rendered work does not automatically remove underlying database duties. Keep the source extract and modifications available in the form required by the chosen distribution. [S61, S63]

Maintain an asset/source record rather than a blanket claim that everything visible online is reusable. No paid material or new licensing expense is a dependency.

## 6. Progression, retention and sharing

**Objective:** Increase voluntary active play and repeat visits over months. Count enjoyable driving and service, rather than idle time, menu dwell or ad viewing, as play time.

### Why players return

Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski's four-study research associates perceived competence and autonomy with enjoyment; the multiplayer survey also links relatedness with enjoyment and future play. This supports testing mastery, choice and connection as design principles. It does not predict Nigerian retention or establish a causal uplift for these particular features. [S47]

The proposal gives players three visible goals: something achievable during the current event, the next meaningful garage/career milestone, and a longer ambition. The next objective appears with the progress already made, so a new session has a clear purpose.

| Horizon | Proposed reason to continue | Concrete expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current run | Complete a satisfying challenge | Win a position, improve a corner or serve the next requested stop |
| Current session | Achieve a visible small milestone | Make a payment, earn a chosen decoration or finish a route challenge |
| Several sessions | Change the bus or career meaningfully | Complete an upgrade, earn a district introduction or defeat a recurring rival |
| Several weeks | Reach a personal ambition | Fully own the first bus, complete a district championship or acquire another bus |
| Long term | Express identity and keep improving | Maintain a favourite bus, collect different handling classes, pursue mastery and play new authored events |

These horizons are design layers, not promised unlock durations. Economy tuning must establish the actual play time required.

### Ownership is a chapter, not the ending

The proposed career starts with the initial deposit already saved, makes early repayment progress visible, celebrates full ownership, then presents several elective ambitions. The player can restore and personalise the first bus, pursue championships, master new districts, acquire another bus or eventually operate a small fleet.

Fleet ownership is an optional later chapter using played-shift accounting. Avoid turning elapsed wall-clock time into the principal money source, since that could make driving economically irrelevant. The first fully owned bus remains usable and competitive in appropriate classes; new ambitions do not erase it or force another debt contract.

### Depth within each mode

**Race:** Learn routes, braking and overtaking; improve personal records; defeat rivals with distinct visible strengths; explore different handling classes; complete district championships. Offer ordinary AI races and local personal ghosts before treating live multiplayer as necessary.

**Approved personal improvement feedback:** Pair an optional personal ghost with one concise, actionable post-race observation derived from actual sector/checkpoint timings, such as time lost through a particular bend. Separate matched-setup skill comparisons from faster times earned with different mechanical setups. Save route/course version, class, handling configuration, conditions and physics version with the record. Label incompatible records separately or withhold direct comparisons. Coaching can be muted, needs no account/backend and never blocks replay or the earned result.

**Work a Route:** Learn demand and stop sequences; choose jobs of different length; manage available seats and stopping decisions; complete service milestones; encounter short conductor/passenger stories. Route demand, traffic and time of day can change decisions, while existing passenger destinations remain reliable.

Both modes earn common money and shared career access. Their specialist records and reputation stay distinct. Core routes must remain earnable through either mode; testing should check that income per active minute does not make one mode feel like compulsory work funding the other. Equal access does not require identical rewards for every event.

### Make replay change decisions

Build authored variations from district geometry, traffic patterns, time/weather, rival combinations, vehicle class and event objectives. Use compatibility rules: a short passenger job needs viable stops, and a race variant needs readable gaps and fair visibility. Do not count every random combination as independently valuable content.

A district should add a recognisable route graph, a characteristic driving decision and a distinct traffic/sound sequence. New content should alternate between improving familiar routes and introducing genuinely new routes. Keep voice cooldowns and a growing dialogue bank so cultural detail does not become repetition.

Forza Horizon 5's official October 2026 update illustrates a weekly themed playlist with specified rewards. It is an existence example, not proof of retention uplift or a prescription to copy its time limits. [S48]

Proposed ongoing cadence:
- A small rotating board of optional race and route-work challenges.
- A weekly authored theme combining familiar content in useful ways.
- A larger chapter or cosmetic collection only at a cadence production can reliably sustain.
- A permanent archive or later earnable return for important rewards, with progress preserved when a player misses days.

### Session design

Start with quick races around the existing 90–180-second test range and trial short passenger shifts around 4–6 active minutes. These are hypotheses, not findings about Nigerian preferences. Offer longer jobs by choice.

The first session should let the player drive promptly, complete one understandable event and see a meaningful career result. Introduce garage upgrades and ownership information when relevant; fleet management is outside the initial release. Results provide an immediate replay or next-job choice, the reward already saved and one understandable next milestone.

Offline solo play, reliable saves, resumable downloads, stable frame delivery and predictable traffic remain retention requirements. Test longer sessions on modest Android devices and the target iPhones; visual ambition cannot compensate for unreliable driving.

### Expression and social play

Proposed sharing:
- A short overtake/finish highlight.
- A portrait of the player's bus.
- A friend challenge with the same route, class and conditions.
- A pass-and-play time challenge on one device.
- Optional platform leaderboards where available.

Use the native share sheet and preserve control over what is shared. Personal ghosts and local challenges provide competition without requiring an own-account service.

Live cross-platform multiplayer, clubs, public custom-livery sharing and a unified account would require separate service, moderation and privacy decisions. They are potential expansions, not prerequisites for retention or authorisation to collect personal information.

### Measure return before adding more systems

The seven approved additions are included in the product scope. The studies below guide implementation and tuning; priority ranking is not permission to silently omit an approved feature. Validate core handling/service before broad content expansion. Test rival recognition/rematch interest, regular-passenger recognition, route/job decisions, felt setup differences, useful mastery feedback and retained bus history. Include repeated sessions, muted/subtitled play, interruptions and representative Android/iOS devices. Automated correctness checks do not prove enjoyment.

Prioritise the following tests:
1. Can first-time players enjoy and understand each mode before deeper progression appears?
2. Does the ownership goal improve return visits compared with a simpler unlock track?
3. After the first bus is paid off, which next ambition do players choose and keep playing?
4. Does meaningful event variation improve repeat play, rather than merely enlarging the menu?
5. Can race-focused, passenger-focused and mixed-mode players all progress at a satisfying pace?
6. Do sharing and cosmetic expression add value when the driving itself already works?

Measure next-day, week and month return; active driving/service minutes per acquired player; completed events; interruption recovery; and reasons for leaving. Use longitudinal playtests and available store/ad aggregates within the selected privacy architecture. Additional telemetry needs deliberate approval and disclosure; this proposal introduces no new own-account backend.

## 7. Initial scope and detail allocation

The starting product has exactly two modes: segmented Race and open-world Work a Route. Open-world driving belongs to passenger work on the connected city network. Arcade, endless-survival play, a separate standalone Explore mode, discovery collections, standalone conductor/cooperative play, fleet management, restoration minigames and hybrid passenger races are outside this release.

Danfo and Lagos detail still enriches the selected modes:
- Voice and character moments during departures, passenger service and results.
- Route-specific scenery, traffic, sound and conditions.
- Garage inspection, upgrades, paint, horns and bus identity.
- Ownership milestones and short owner/conductor messages in menus, alongside the conductor's existing driving presentation.
- Race variants within Race and different passenger jobs within Work a Route.

The conductor handles fares automatically in the passenger mode. Optional dialogue does not block driving. The city research and cultural inventory remain references, not instructions to turn every observed activity into a separate feature.

## 8. Advertising-funded monetization

**Confirmed user direction, 18 October 2026:** Focus on maximising advertising revenue while sustaining engagement. Direct player payments are outside the product model. This supersedes earlier proposals for cosmetic purchases, ad removal, paid seasons, subscriptions and price testing. Bus purchase agreements use earned game money only.

**Commercial objective:** Maximise cumulative net advertising contribution per acquired player over a long enough horizon to capture return play. Ad density, eCPM and opt-in rate are inputs rather than the final objective.

### How advertising fits the product

The career creates things players want to achieve: another instalment, a chosen upgrade, an appearance, another bus or a collection milestone. Ordinary play already advances those goals. A useful optional reward can accelerate a selected goal or award a disclosed decorative item.

Both modes finish at the same results structure:
1. Record the outcome and save base earnings and progress.
2. Show the earned amount and the player's chosen next milestone.
3. Where available, offer one relevant optional rewarded action.
4. Offer Next, Replay or Garage immediately. If an automatic interstitial is eligible, display it within this results break before starting another event.
5. Carry earnings, payments and unlocks into the shared career.

A displayed rewarded ad suppresses the interstitial at that results screen and resets shared spacing. Declining an optional reward does not revoke earnings or create a new ad trigger; an interstitial is governed by its independently accrued eligibility. Switching modes never creates a fresh ad schedule.

### Proposed rewarded offers

Exact reward quantities remain testable proposals rather than implemented decisions.

| Placement | Offer | Grant and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| race_bonus | Double eligible earned race currency | One additional amount equal to that race's base currency award, once after a completed race |
| shift_bonus | Double eligible completed-journey fares | One additional amount equal to the completed shift's eligible base fares; no reward for unsettled journeys |
| garage_sample | Unlock one selected decorative item | One named permanent livery detail, cabin decoration or other item; preview before opting in |
| cosmetic_trial | Try one selected appearance | The existing disclosed trial for the next three started races; an optional test placement, not a paid-item preview |

The third and fourth placements are alternatives to evaluate, not instructions to show every offer on every visit. Keep one relevant offer per results screen and keep the garage free of automatic ads. A permanent item may be a better fit than a trial in an ad-funded catalogue; test its value and repeat demand before selecting the final set.

Normal play provides a satisfying free progression path and an earnable customisation set. A reward-eligible item has a stated grant and does not require buying anything. There is no player-facing cash shop or premium wallet.

Eligible currency excludes previous ad bonuses, owner-payment transfers, fleet income and unrelated grants. Operating expenses and owner instalments apply once, whether or not a bonus is claimed. A bonus must not double deductions or alter a race result, personal record or vehicle class.

No rewarded rescue is part of ordinary competitive racing. Losing a race remains a driving outcome with an immediate replay path, rather than a prompt to change the result through advertising.

Google's rewarded-ad policy requires clear disclosure, appropriate opt-in, normal use after declining and delivery of the promised reward. Direct monetary rewards are prohibited. All game amounts are non-cashable and non-transferable. [S39]

### Proposed interstitial starting rule

The user has selected the revenue model; exact pacing still needs testing. Retain the preceding numerical baseline rather than silently raising the frequency.

| Rule | Proposed baseline |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible place | A completed qualifying event's results flow, after the result and earnings are visible and saved |
| First automatic ad | No earlier than three completed qualifying events and 180 seconds of active play |
| Later automatic ads | At least two qualifying events since the last displayed full-screen ad and 180 seconds since that ad closed |
| Active play minimum for later eligibility | At least 120 seconds of active play since that full-screen ad |
| Qualifying event | A race reached its real finish or a full passenger shift completed; active time excludes menus, idle time and ads |
| Rewarded interaction | An actually displayed rewarded ad suppresses the interstitial on that debrief and resets the spacing anchor |
| Rapid reset/quit | No automatic ad; no qualifying completion |
| Menus, garage, customisation, pause, launch | No automatic ad |
| Offline/no inventory | Continue immediately; no substitute delay or ad wall |
| Failed display | No completed-impression credit; prevent retry spam and continue |
| Return from background | No stored ad suddenly appears on resume |

Active play includes driving and required service animations, but excludes menus, voluntary idle time and ad viewing. One completed passenger shift is one event. Individual stops, boarding actions, fare transactions and partial legs do not become ad opportunities.

Interstitials belong at understandable results breaks. Do not surprise the player after they have pressed Race or Work a Route to start the next event. Google Play specifically addresses unexpected interstitials during play or at the beginning of a level/content segment. [S40]

There are no banners. No-fill, offline status and consent outcomes preserve normal core play; an unavailable optional reward must never become an ad wall.

### Personalisation and ongoing content

Bus identity remains part of the product even though real-money sales have been removed. Players earn or claim disclosed rewards for paint, slogans, interiors, horns and decorative collections. Cosmetic choice does not change collision, passenger capacity or competitive power.

Offer permanent goals and optional themed challenge collections. A season is a free content programme with earnable rewards; no paid track or subscription is planned. Important rewards can return through an archive or later earning path.

Additional suitable sponsorship can be considered after real reach and advertiser interest exist. It is a later B2B advertising possibility, not forecast revenue or a player payment. No brand has been contacted, and no alcohol or gambling sponsorship is proposed.

### Optimise revenue and engagement together

1. **Driving and return play:** Fix confusing controls, repetition, slow starts and lost progress first.
2. **Reward value:** Compare currency bonuses with known-item rewards. Track later play and total ad contribution as well as opt-in.
3. **Pacing:** Compare the retained results-break baseline with lighter, denser and rewarded-only schedules. Change one material variable at a time with a stable comparison group.
4. **Ad demand:** Evaluate mediation/bidding using realised receipts and display success, while accounting for SDK size, load time and reliability.
5. **Career economy:** Check that optional bonuses remain useful after the first bus is owned, without making ownership trivial or making ordinary progress feel starved.
6. **Content:** Add meaningful route/event variety and customisation goals where repeated play shows demand.

Unity's racing-publisher case documents rewarded revenue alongside broader mediation/acquisition changes. Google's Netmarble case compares self-selected ad opt-ins with non-opt-ins. They justify testing, not assuming causal retention benefits or importing the same results into Nigeria. [S43, S49]

### Revenue measurement

There is no verified eCPM, retention curve or traffic forecast for this game.

For each platform and player geography:

**Ad revenue = rewarded served impressions × rewarded realised eCPM / 1,000 + interstitial served impressions × interstitial realised eCPM / 1,000.**

**Net advertising contribution = ad receipts + contracted net sponsorship receipts, if any − directly attributable delivery and operating costs.**

Report cumulative contribution per acquired player at 30 and 90 days, then longer observed horizons. Include players who leave. Track return rates, active driving/service minutes, completed events, impressions, reward completions, post-ad exits, reliability and currency inflation alongside revenue.

When forecasting from opportunities, include opt-in/request/fill/display probabilities as applicable. When using served impressions, do not apply fill rate again. Country, platform, format and period matter; historical reports are not a current Nigeria revenue guarantee. [S45]

A test needs a defined primary outcome, exposure window, uncertainty, retention guardrail and rollback rule. A fixed seven-day sample or a higher eCPM alone cannot establish the optimum. Additional telemetry requires a deliberate privacy decision; this proposal does not introduce an own-account backend.

### Ad and reward state

- Save base results and completed-journey fares before opening an ad.
- Allow one ad request in flight.
- Grant each eligible reward once using a unique identity.
- No-fill, cancellation or failure leaves base earnings intact.
- Preserve the shared interstitial history across modes, restarts and app backgrounding.
- Consume a race appearance trial only when its recorded entitlement is active and the relevant race starts.
- Replays and delayed/duplicate callbacks cannot duplicate earnings.
- Disable retry spam and avoid consecutive advertiser pods as the default. [S44]
- Respect the eventual audience, platform, consent and SDK configuration throughout.

## 12. Acceptance criteria for the expanded product

These are proposed checks to include in the later build contract.

### Racing
- All ordinary races can start without an ad or purchase.
- The finish, position and reward agree after interruption and resume.
- Race-course actors are limited to the player and competitors; no civilian vehicle or crossing pedestrian enters the racing line.
- Passenger-mode traffic and requests have sufficient visible response space under the selected tuning.
- Vehicle geometry and collision boundaries agree.
- Camera/input changes do not change the available race rewards unfairly.
- AI rivals do not teleport into a winning position.
- A race has a useful restart and recovery path.
- Side scrapes, shoves and heavier impacts have distinct, readable effects without repeated-contact instability.
- Narrow gaps agree with the visible bus width, road edges and camera.
- Aggressive rivals follow the same road geometry and contact rules as the player.

### Separate modes and passenger work
- Race and Work a Route have separate objectives and records, with a shared bus, earned balance and ownership career.
- Both modes remain accessible and support earnable core-route progression.
- A passenger shift is judged by its service and earnings objectives, not by finishing ahead of ambient buses.
- Ambient rivals cannot remove an essential assigned pickup or make a tutorial/story job impossible.
- Boarding requires a stationary bus in a valid stop area, and occupancy cannot exceed event capacity.
- The next required drop-off is clear with voices muted.
- Missing a stop leaves a viable recovery path.
- Routes offer multiple eligible roadside boarding/drop-off areas rather than requiring every interaction at a major terminal.
- A passenger request has enough approach notice, and a valid roadside segment allows a forgiving stop.
- Completed journeys retain base fares if the shift is interrupted later, with each fare granted only once.
- Save/resume preserves passengers, destinations, completed journeys and fare settlement.
- Stops and individual fares do not trigger ads or increment event-completion ad counters.
- Switching modes preserves the shared full-screen-ad history.
- The initial mode selector exposes Race and Work a Route only.
- No Arcade, Explore, standalone conductor or fleet-management flow is required for the initial release.
- Races and passenger shifts retain distinct objectives; neither requires playing the other first.
- Passenger work includes normal location-appropriate traffic.
- Switching event mode does not leak a passenger load, fare job or civilian spawner into Race.
- Work a Route uses a connected driving world; pickups/drop-offs do not automatically end or reload a level.
- Valid alternative streets remain usable, with navigation updating towards remaining required destinations.
- All required service destinations are reachable within the playable/installed road network.

- Menu actions preserve ownership/history and affect the correct bus exactly once.

## 15. Research assessment: what would improve the game most?

**Requested 18 October 2026:** A rigorous assessment of further additions that could make the game excellent. This is an independent desk assessment informed by Stanford's published game-design teaching and other primary research; it is not a Stanford-conducted study or endorsement. **Subsequently approved:** the user instructed that all seven additions be incorporated, together with their supporting handling/onboarding and validation requirements. They are integrated into the operative sections above. The two-mode boundary, free-production constraint and existing advertising rules are unchanged.

### Method and evidence strength

Stanford's CS247G course describes making prototypes, rapid iteration and user research. Apply that approach by stating the intended experience, building the smallest useful comparison and observing players rather than assigning a score to an unplayed concept. [S66]

The MDA framework connects rules and implementation to emergent play and the player's emotional experience. Here the target experiences are substantial bus handling, tactical rivalry, learning Lagos, serving passengers and developing a bus that feels personally owned. It is a design framework, not an empirical formula for commercial success. [S67]

Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski's studies support examining competence, autonomy and intuitive controls; relatedness was also associated with enjoyment and intended future play in their multiplayer sample. This does not establish that fictional regular customers or rival memory will improve this game's retention. Those are our design hypotheses to test. [S47]

Two comparable-product references add concrete existence examples: Wreckfest describes contact racing and upgrade trade-offs; EA documents personal ghosts and optional telemetry in F1 22. Neither source demonstrates retention or ad-revenue uplift for these mechanics. Use their principles without copying assets, content or their broader scope. [S68, S69]

#### Rival memory: behaviour before dialogue

Prototype three original rivals: one protects narrow gaps, one brakes late but leaves recoverable openings, and one waits for opportunities to overtake. Their strengths must be visible in driving, not just claimed in a biography. A compact record can remember the last result and a few relevant encounters, producing an appropriate greeting or reaction in a later event. Rival persistence changes authored reactions and goals; it does not grant hidden speed, teleportation or immunity to contact. Select difficulty before an event and keep its rules stable through that event.

Keep reactions varied and rate-limited. A rival need not retaliate on every contact. Avoid turning unavoidable pack contact into an escalating penalty system. This feature fits Race, with no additional standalone mode, account service or online opponent simulation.

#### Regular passengers: continuity without lengthy scenes

Prototype a small cast with different routes, schedules, appearances and concise personalities. For example, a regular commuter recognises the conductor on a later played shift, or a passenger's short authored story continues when a normal journey is completed. Their requests must remain compatible with the chosen route and its clear stop system. Personalities are authored fictional individuals rather than claims about an ethnic, gender or occupational group.

Do not require attendance on real calendar days, introduce debt for missed visits or force unskippable conversations. A regular passenger's seat, destination and fare use the normal passenger system. This is a local saved-state and dialogue feature; a live generative-AI service is unnecessary. MIT's account supports the social character of danfo travel, but does not demonstrate that recurring NPCs increase retention. [S1]

#### Route choices: reward knowing the place

Keep landmarks and street relationships stable. An alternative path must be an authored, valid part of the selected course, with a readable entry and enough information to make a choice. A branch cannot bypass mandatory race checkpoints or strand a customer's required drop-off. Wider/longer and narrower/shorter are testable examples, not a rule to manufacture everywhere.

During passenger work, use traffic and researched demand patterns to change decisions. During Race, intensity continues to come from rival buses and the course; civilian vehicles, fare jobs and crossing pedestrians stay off the active racing line. Weather variants must preserve readable hazards and adequate response time.

### Additions with a weaker case for the initial release

- Live multiplayer, public livery exchange and clubs: possible future value, but they require services and operating decisions beyond the current local-first release.
- A full walkable city, extra main modes and playable conductor work: outside the agreed initial product and unnecessary to test its central pleasures.
- Detailed maintenance, fuel queues, confiscation or constant breakdowns: authentic domains do not automatically make enjoyable repeated play. Environmental/story representation is already available without compulsory chores.
- Full soft-body destruction, dense individual simulations and elaborate facial animation: no measured evidence here that these are the best use of mobile performance or production effort.
- Streak loss, real-time owner arrears and extra ad triggers at passenger stops: conflict with the existing product decisions and are not recommended.

### Validation needed before calling the experience excellent

Start with one coherent district, a small rival cast, one personal bus and one functioning passenger corridor. This is a validation slice, not a cut to the intended final product. Existing modular content can expand after its driving and service work.

1. **Core replay check:** let players repeat a short race and shift before exposing a large reward catalogue. Record voluntary replay, confusion, control errors and stated reasons. If the feel is weak, address it before expanding the city.
2. **Feature comparisons:** compare a fixed rival with an otherwise equivalent rival that has tactics/memory; generic passengers with recurring characters; and a linear course with a carefully matched branch version. Keep rewards, difficulty and ad rules comparable. Counterbalance order across players and include new players as well as repeat testers.
3. **Learning check:** observe whether repeated attempts improve gap judgement, route knowledge or stop decisions. Ask what changed; a better time caused only by a stronger upgrade does not establish acquired skill.
4. **Recognition and irritation check:** ask Lagos-familiar players to identify places, bus details and character behaviour without leading questions. Ask what feels false, repetitive or annoying. Test subtitle-only and muted play as well.
5. **Return and device check:** run repeated voluntary sessions across days with modest Android devices and target iPhones. Track active play, return, mode preference, temperature/frame stability, save recovery and reasons for leaving. A small qualitative group can reveal faults but cannot estimate national demand or precise population retention.
6. **Commercial check:** retain the current ad policy while assessing these gameplay changes. Longer events can reduce ad opportunities per hour, so examine cumulative net advertising value per acquired player alongside return and enjoyment. No recommendation here changes placements, spacing or reward offers. Any later monetization change is a separate decision.

Use voluntary feedback, local measurements and available platform/ad aggregates within the zero-new-spend and local-first constraints; no new telemetry backend or paid research service is introduced. Agree numerical product targets after baseline playtests. A small sample with enthusiastic comments is not a statistically established 10/10 or a revenue forecast.

**Approved implementation sequence:** validate handling/onboarding; develop rivals and regular passengers; then add route/job decisions and understandable setup/mastery feedback, followed by visible history and its presentation. All seven additions remain included. The immediate goal is a repeatable, memorable drive and shift that players choose again.

## Reference foundation retained from revision 1

The following is sourced background, not a separate competing product specification. Proposed mechanics in this foundation are governed by the updated design above.

#### What a danfo actually is

A danfo is a shared-route commercial minibus, rather than one single manufacturer or universal vehicle specification. Its familiar Lagos identity includes yellow bodywork and dark horizontal striping. Photographs and reporting show converted vans, individual wear and personal inscriptions. Volkswagen-derived buses are prominent references, while reporting also identifies vehicles such as Nissan Urvans. [S3, S5]

Distinguish the fleet:
- **Danfo:** the familiar passenger minibus.
- **Korope:** a smaller minibus category.
- **Keke:** a three-wheeled local passenger vehicle.
- **Okada:** a motorcycle transport category.
- **BRT/formal buses:** a different part of the transport system.

These should have different silhouettes and traffic behavior if represented. MIT's account of Lagos transport describes the relationship between minibuses, smaller vehicles and formal transit; this is a mixed system. [S1]

#### Vehicle research implications

**Documented:** Volkswagen's history says the Transporter introduced in 1979 retained a rear-mounted boxer engine. That manufacturer history is relevant to a T3 reference. It does not establish the engine, condition or conversion of every Lagos bus. [S4]

**Proposed for this game:**
- Establish one recognisable base bus before deciding variants.
- Give the player's vehicle a persistent identity through its body, paint, horn and name.
- Let acceleration, braking, suspension movement and turning convey a working minibus.
- Separate cosmetic damage from mechanical effects.
- Decide deliberately whether minor contact costs speed, damages the bus, or both.
- Keep steering predictable enough that players can judge gaps.

No real acceleration curve, top speed, turning radius, braking distance or rollover threshold was measured in this desk research. Those require a selected vehicle reference and gameplay testing. “Authentic” should not be used to disguise invented physics.

#### What photographs genuinely establish

The reviewed 2018 danfo photograph shows a flat-front minibus, dark bands, a strong window line, metal body panels and visible wear. Its street context includes drainage, uneven road edges and ordinary buildings. [S5]

Those cues provide stronger vehicle identity than adding generic racing decals. Wear can vary: a maintained personal bus, a patched workhorse and a decorated bus are plausible creative variations, not three verified fleet classes.

#### How real danfo operation creates its driving character

Danfo operation follows routes, demand, stops and relationships between people. It is not simply random movement. The mapping work described by MIT found an extensive and changing route network, including major interchange areas such as Oshodi and Obalende. [S1]

FIJ's 2022 reporting describes drivers and conductors working together, loading passengers, making payments around parks and routes, paying owners and meeting daily expenses. The reporting helps explain commercial pressure; its historical naira amounts should not be treated as current prices. [S2]

#### Roles to keep distinct

| Role | Research meaning | Possible relevance to a racer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driver | Controls the vehicle and makes road decisions | Player and rival identity |
| Conductor | Calls destinations, handles boarding and fares | Voice, commentary and social character |
| Passenger | Travels to a destination | Bus-stop setting and reactions |
| Agbero/park collector | A distinct street or park role associated with dues and control | Optional background context |
| Traffic authority | Formal traffic management/enforcement | Street context if later chosen |

A conductor and an agbero should not automatically be treated as the same person. Some casual guides blur that distinction; the reporting describes separate roles. [S2]

#### Driving patterns worth translating into racing

The following are **proposed gameplay abstractions** of the documented traffic and stop environment, not measured statistics about all Lagos drivers:

| Pattern | What the player reads | Racing use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A bus slows near a stop | Reduced speed and a developing obstruction | Choose an overtake early |
| A vehicle emerges from a roadside queue | An approaching merge | Defend or surrender a gap |
| Several vehicles contest limited space | A narrowing route through traffic | Commit, brake or change line |
| A flyover splits from a surface road | Two paths with different constraints | Choose a route |
| A rough patch interrupts a fast section | A visible change in surface | Trade distance against lost speed |
| An open stretch follows congestion | A chance to recover momentum | Overtake before the next bottleneck |

The appealing racing skill is **reading space and preserving momentum**. Random unavoidable collisions would undermine that skill.

Real rush-hour gridlock and enjoyable racing have different pacing needs. Race traffic density should be tuned to create decisions and recovery opportunities. A literal full journey through stationary traffic is a separate simulation idea.

#### Handling proposal

A useful prototype would test:
- Noticeable body movement while the steering response remains readable.
- Braking that matters before an obstruction or turn.
- Recoverable light contact.
- Rivals that contest gaps and make understandable mistakes.
- Different challenge from open roads, junctions and tight approaches.

Long drifts, nitro, jumping, heavy takedowns and spectacular rollovers are possible arcade choices. They are not established by this research as essential danfo traits.

#### 4.1 Ojuelegba: a strong first race reference

The inspected 2019 photograph shows the overhead road structure, supports, minibuses, roadside stalls and mixed activity below. These are specific spatial references rather than a generic “African city” backdrop. [S8]

**Proposed race ingredients:** a readable approach to the flyover, a decision between constrained routes, a short compressed section and room to accelerate afterwards.

Do not infer present-day signs, barriers or lane rules from one old photograph. It is an architectural and street-character reference.

#### 4.2 Oshodi: transport architecture matters

JDB's project account places the interchange along Agege Motor Road and the Apapa–Oshodi Expressway and describes three terminals connected by a sky-link. [S9]

**Proposed race ingredients:** queue edges, vehicles joining the road, terminal approaches, grade separation and a recognisable departure/finish area.

A purely old-style open bus park would miss the major terminal infrastructure. Race routing should use selected approaches rather than turning the entire passenger terminal into a racetrack by accident.

#### 4.3 Third Mainland: water and scale

The crossing should retain its relationship to the lagoon and its mainland/Island approaches. Its long bridge form is different from the conspicuous cable-and-pylon form of the Lekki–Ikoyi bridge. [S10, S13]

**Proposed race ingredients:** sustained overtaking, gradual curves, vehicle queues, an approach bottleneck and a visible finish target. Wind, rain or bridge-surface effects would be designed modifiers; none were measured here.

An arcade version can compress distance while preserving the crossing's identity.

#### 4.4 CMS and Marina: commerce beside water

The inspected Marina reference shows a waterfront road and substantial buildings, including varied building ages and scales. The CMS junction reference identifies the meeting of Broad Street and Odulami Street. [S11, S12]

**Proposed race ingredients:** a turn through a commercial block, a recognisable junction, then a change of openness near the waterfront.

Lagos Island, Victoria Island and Ikoyi should have distinct identities. They are not interchangeable labels for one street scene.

#### 4.5 Lekki and Ikoyi: a second Lagos character

Julius Berger identifies the Admiralty Alexander/Lekki–Ikoyi bridge as connecting Lekki and Ikoyi. The inspected builder photograph confirms its distinctive cable-stayed appearance. [S13]

**Proposed race ingredients:** a landmark reveal, a bridge run and a change in road frontage on either side.

The bridge should not appear next to Oshodi or Ojuelegba without a deliberately fictional transition. A landmark collage is different from a compressed but coherent route.

#### 4.6 Apapa: trucks change the race

The NPA statement concerns truck access to Lagos Port Complex and Tin Can Island ports in Apapa and the electronic call-up system. This provides a grounded reason for a port corridor to have a different vehicle mix. [S17]

**Proposed race ingredients:** long vehicles that block sightlines, slower acceleration, fewer easy passing gaps and rewarding anticipation. Exact truck density and present road condition need route-specific references.

#### Details that make the streets recognisable

#### Architecture and roadside references

Build a reference set for:
- Flyover columns, ramps, pedestrian bridges and roadside barriers.
- Bus parks, loading areas and modern terminals.
- Low-rise commercial frontage, workshops and taller office buildings.
- Shop signs, hand-painted lettering, route boards and local advertising.
- Gutters, culverts, patched asphalt and transitions between road and shoulder.
- Waterfront edges, boats at a distance and the relationship between bridges and buildings.
- Trees, boundary walls and maintained streets as well as busy trading areas.

This is an asset-research checklist, not a claim that every item belongs on every road. Place details according to the specific district and reference.

Afrotype's Danfo project is especially useful for researching bus lettering. Its designer describes field visits, photography and sketches behind a typeface inspired by local bus decoration. [S6]

#### Traffic composition

Suggested traffic categories are other danfos, smaller buses, formal buses, private cars, delivery vehicles and district-appropriate heavier vehicles. Smaller motorcycles or tricycles need location-specific treatment.

LAMATA describes restrictions affecting motorcycles and tricycles in certain areas as part of the rationale for its first/last-mile scheme. Their presence should not be identical on every road. [S14]

Normal traffic direction should follow Nigeria's right-hand traffic convention; any contraflow element should be an explicit race design decision. Lane markings, junction directions and median gaps still need verification against the chosen road. [S23]

#### Present-day transport

LAMATA's Blue Line schedule lists Marina, National Theatre, Iganmu, Alaba and Mile 2. Rail infrastructure can therefore be relevant to a contemporary environment along the appropriate corridor. It should not be scattered decoratively across unrelated districts. [S15]

The 2022 bus photo and 2019 street photo are useful references, but neither alone represents all of Lagos in 2026.

#### People and atmosphere

The environment can convey work, movement, trade, humour and ordinary life. People should belong to places: waiting around stops, using footbridges, standing at shops or working at stalls.

**Proposed race treatment:** pedestrians remain outside the normal race line, with collision behavior decided explicitly if ever introduced. Human road casualties are not needed to communicate reckless overtaking.

This is a game-direction proposal, not a representation that real streets lack pedestrian conflict.

#### Weather, time and road conditions

The Lagos Ministry's indexed 2026 notice and reporting of its briefing describe expected above-normal rainfall and a long rainy season. This is a forecast for that year, not a guarantee of conditions on every day or road. Rain is a grounded environment choice. [S16]

| Condition | Proposed environment treatment | Possible racing effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bright dry day | Clearer distance reading, road and vehicle detail | Baseline handling |
| Overcast day | Softer contrast and a different skyline mood | Mostly atmosphere |
| Rain shower | Water on road, spray and audible rain | Longer braking if deliberately tuned |
| After rain | Puddles and wet patches | Localised line choices |
| Evening / night | Vehicle lights, shopfronts and lit road sections | Different visibility demands |
| Hazy dry-season variant | A route-specific reference must establish the look | Optional reduced-distance reading |

Avoid constant flooding, universal potholes or uniform darkness. Those would flatten different places into one repeated obstacle theme.

Standing water depth, tyre grip, braking penalties, visibility distance and the frequency of damaged road patches remain game parameters. They are not empirical results from this research.

#### Sound and language

Emeka Ogboh's Lagos Soundscapes work, presented by the African Artists' Foundation, is a particularly relevant primary cultural reference. It describes conductor calls as a form of verbal mapping: routes and stops become recognisable through voice and cadence. It also identifies vehicle sounds, horns and commerce as parts of the soundscape. [S7]

**Proposed sound layers:**
1. Player bus: engine response, gear changes if applicable, tyres, suspension and body rattle.
2. Nearby traffic: distinct horns, passing engines and braking.
3. Place ambience: terminal voices, shop activity or a more open bridge environment.
4. Character: occasional conductor or rival reactions.
5. Optional original music.

A single loud loop across every race would lose much of the place-specific value.

#### Phrase research

| Expression | Context | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “O wa o!” | Passenger signals a wish to alight | Cultural context; not automatically a race command |
| “Enter with your change” | Exact-fare request | Terminal ambience or introductory flavour |
| Repeated destination names | Attracting passengers and identifying a route | Place identity |
| “Oya, move!” | Proposed race line | Requires native voice and context review |
| “You wan overtake me?” | Proposed rival banter | Original dialogue, not a field quotation |

The passenger-use account supports the alighting and change context. New race dialogue is labelled as proposed rather than presented as recorded speech. [S18]

Use Nigerian English, Pidgin and locally appropriate Yoruba with natural pronunciation. A Lagos-based reader should review route names, delivery and humour before recording. Gameplay information should remain understandable without recognising every joke.

Existing songs, radio streams, field recordings and videos are research references. Production audio needs its own usable rights or original recording.

The evidence establishes **existing creative and commercial interest in the setting**. It does not establish the size of paying demand for this particular racer.

#### Proposed position for this game

The clearest expression of the user's request is a persistent personal danfo competing against identifiable rival danfos in finite races through recognisable Lagos environments.

Potential distinction would come from racing quality, bus personality, rivals and coherent places. The existence of competitors is useful research; their discovery, retention and revenue cannot be inferred from their themes.
