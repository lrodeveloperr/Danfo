## 1. Vehicle designs: trademark and trade dress in vehicle shapes and grilles, OEM licensing, precedents, and how different an "inspired-by" bus must be

### Takeaway
Licensing a Toyota or VW body is not realistic for a zero-budget game in which battered buses crash in races. Carmakers vet damage and "illegal" use, and Toyota has a record of refusing games. The safe course is a fictional marque in the GTA/BeamNG style: no real badges, names, grilles or exact light signatures. Be most careful with VW's T1/T2 "Bulli" front, because the EUIPO accepted it as an inherently distinctive 3D trade mark in 2021. US case law (Humvee) protects realistic vehicle depictions in expressive games. That defence is US-only, it narrowed after Jack Daniel's (2023), and it does not stop a store takedown.

### Cited Findings
**US precedent on real vehicles in games**
- **[Case] AM General v Activision (S.D.N.Y., No. 17-8644).** Judge George Daniels granted Activision summary judgment on every claim: trademark, trade dress, unfair competition, false designation, false advertising and dilution. The claims concerned Humvees in *Call of Duty*. The court applied the *Rogers v Grimaldi* test. The Humvees had artistic relevance because realistic military vehicles "evoke a sense of realism and lifelikeness." The use was not explicitly misleading under the Polaroid factors, even though AM General's survey showed some confusion. Sources date the decision 31 March 2020; AIPLA says 1 April 2020. — [Finnegan (S)](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/in-legal-warfare-over-humvee-trademarks-the-first-amendment-goes-beyond-the-call-of-duty-in-dismissing-am-generals-claims.html); [AIPLA (S)](https://www.aipla.org/detail/news/2020/04/08/activision-beats-humvee-trademark-claims-over-call-of-duty); [The Drive (S)](https://www.thedrive.com/tech/32872/you-have-a-first-amendment-right-to-humvees-in-call-of-duty-games-judge-rules); [Miller Nash (S)](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/first-amendment-beats-trademark-rights-three-times-in-one-day)
- **[Case] Textron (Bell Helicopter) v Electronic Arts, *Battlefield 3*.** EA sought a declaration of non-infringement and Textron counter-sued. The court refused to dismiss Textron's claim because it was "plausible" consumers would think Bell had supplied expertise. It noted the "particular prominence" of Bell helicopters in the game **and its marketing**. The parties then settled. Exact dates were not in the retrieved text; from background knowledge, about 2012–13 (unverified). — [The Licensing Letter (S)](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/?p=796); [Cardozo AELJ (S)](https://cardozoaelj.com/?p=1136); [IBA, 3-D objects in video games, July 2021 (S)](https://www.ibanet.org/ip-july-2021-3d-object-video-games-ip)
- **[Case] Saber Interactive v Oovee, *Spintires* (W.D. Wash.).** Saber said it held an **exclusive licence to depict a manufacturer's tractor in games**. It claimed Oovee's tractor infringed that manufacturer's trademarks and trade dress. On 6 October 2022 the court dismissed the Lanham Act, Washington Consumer Protection Act and unjust-enrichment claims without prejudice, with leave to amend. No later outcome was found. — [Frankfurt Kurnit (FKKS) (S)](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102hzos/a-tale-of-two-truck-simulator-games-and-the-first-amendment)
- **[Case] Jack Daniel's Properties v VIP Products, US Supreme Court, 8 June 2023, unanimous.** The Court rejected the Ninth Circuit's broad reading of *Rogers*. Commentators read it as removing *Rogers* protection where the defendant uses a mark as a source identifier for its own goods. — [Proskauer (S)](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/in-jack-daniels-case-supreme-court-rejects-ninth-circuits-expansive-view-of-first-amendment-rogers-test)
- **[Practitioner]** "A series of cases have denied First Amendment protection to videogame producers who use avatars of real athletes," so the outcome for real objects is "not preordained." — [FKKS (S)](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102hzos/a-tale-of-two-truck-simulator-games-and-the-first-amendment)

**UK/EU vehicle-shape trade marks**
- **[Case] London Taxi Corporation v Frazer-Nash Research [2017] EWCA Civ 1729.** LTC registered the black-cab shape as EU mark 951871 and UK mark 2440659, based on the Fairway, TXI, TXII and TX4. LTC claimed the Metrocab hybrid taxi infringed. The Court of Appeal upheld the High Court and found the marks invalid on three grounds:
  - The shape lacked inherent distinctiveness.
  - It had not acquired distinctiveness. Consumers recognising the shape did not prove they saw it as a sign of origin.
  - The shape gave substantial value to the goods.

  In an obiter (non-binding) remark, the court said Frazer-Nash would have infringed by dilution had the marks been valid. — [RPC (S)](https://rpc.co.uk/thinking/ip/court-of-appeal-drives-through-london-taxis-hopes-of-enforcing-protection-for-its-shape-marks); [Pinsent Masons Out-Law (S)](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/the-shape-of-a-london-taxi-not-valid-for-trade-mark-protection-rules-court-of-appeal); [DWW (S)](https://www.dww.com/articles/uk-court-of-appeal-affirms-decision-london-black-cabs-lack-distinctive-character); [Addleshaw Goddard (S)](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/globalassets/insights/intellectual-property/shape-trade-marks-50-shades-of-black-cabs.pdf); [Kluwer Trademark Blog (S)](https://trademarkblog.kluweriplaw.com/?p=1880)
- **[Regulator/official] EUIPO First Board of Appeal, November 2021.** The board held that the VW Bus ("Bulli") shape **can be registered as a 3D EU trade mark**, overturning a refusal for lack of distinctiveness. It found the shape inherently distinctive, "also for vehicles." It pointed to the **V-shaped bead on the front where a radiator grille would normally be**, and said the shape was uncommon for vans when filed and in the 1950s. — [MLL News (S)](https://www.mll-news.com/the-shape-of-the-vw-bulli-a-trademark-a-trademark/?lang=en)
- **[Case] German 3D mark for the T1.** VW registered it in 2006 for "motor vehicles" (class 12) and "model cars" (class 28), and used it against a maker of high-priced model cars. The Hamburg Higher Regional Court found for VW. The Federal Court of Justice (BGH, I ZR 23/23, 2 May 2024) set that judgment aside and sent the case back. It said the model cars were aimed at collectors and advertisers, so VW's general-public surveys might not decide the case. A June 2025 IPKat post is titled "final chapter," but I could not open it, so **the final outcome is unconfirmed**. — [BGH judgment via rewis (S)](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/zq5-02-05-2024-i-zr-2323/); [IPKat July 2024 (S, blocked)](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2024/07/small-cars-big-trouble-reloaded.html); [IPKat June 2025 (S, blocked)](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2025/06/small-cars-big-trouble-final-chapter.html)
- **[Regulator/official]** VW Commercial Vehicles obtained trade mark rights in the name "Bulli" in 2007. — [VW Commercial Vehicles press release (S)](https://volkswagen-commercial-vehicles.prezly.com/launched-70-years-ago-in-march-bulli-has-worlds-longest-production-run-for-a-transporter)

### Inferences
- The Rogers/First Amendment defence is US law and helps only after a lawsuit has started. Nigeria, the EU and the UK have no equivalent doctrine. Store review and complaint handling turn on Apple 5.2.1 and Google's policy, not on case law. **A complaint can get the app pulled first and argued later.** For a zero-budget team, "we would win in New York" is not a usable strategy.
- *Textron* and *Jack Daniel's* show that **marketing use raises the risk**. A real-looking VW or Toyota on the store icon or in screenshots, or as the identity of the game itself, is the exposure point. Prominent real vehicles in key art make an "explicitly misleading" or source-identifier finding more likely.
- The London Taxi case suggests a generic van silhouette is hard to monopolise. **Bulli/T1** is the exception: the EUIPO accepted inherent distinctiveness and VW enforces the shape against models. Danfo's historic reference vehicles are T2/T3 Transporters and HiAce/Urvan vans, not T1s. **Avoid the T1/T2 combination of split or bay windscreen, V-shaped front bead or "V" panel, and a round central badge.**
- **Practical design-distance checklist for a fictional bus.** There is no legal bright line; this is practitioner-style judgement.
  1. **Fictional marque and model name**, trademark-searched. No "VW," "Volkswagen," "Kombi," "Bulli," "Transporter," "Toyota," "HiAce," "Nissan" or "Urvan" anywhere in the game, store listing, keywords or patch notes. Generic words like "danfo," "korope," "bus" and "minibus" are fine.
  2. **New badge**: different shape from the VW roundel and Toyota/Nissan emblems, with a different letterform.
  3. **New front face**: change grille pattern and proportions, headlamp shape, indicator placement and bumper. Do not keep a real model's unique face.
  4. **Change proportions**: blend at least two or three real references (T3 boxiness, HiAce cab-over, Urvan roofline) and invent details. The aim is "a Lagos minibus," not "a 1985 T3."
  5. **Distinct light signatures**, since tail-light shapes are commonly registered as designs.
  6. **Test it**: if playtesters name a specific make and model without prompting, change the design again.
- Licensing is a poor fit regardless of budget. Danfo is about crashes, wear, contact-heavy racing and possible depictions of illegal driving, which carmakers routinely veto.

### Gaps
- Final outcome of VW's remitted T1 model-car case. The EUIPO registration number and current status of the Bulli 3D mark could not be checked (EUIPO blocked).
- Whether Toyota or Nissan hold registered designs or 3D marks for HiAce or Urvan bodies. Not searched; budget exhausted.
- No primary GDC or Game Developer article on Toyota licensing terms was retrievable (gamedeveloper.com blocked).
- No verified example of a store removal triggered by a carmaker.

## 2. The danfo livery: can anyone own government-mandated yellow with black stripes?

### Takeaway
The yellow-with-black-stripes scheme is a **legal requirement Lagos State imposes on all commercial buses**. It signals "licensed commercial bus," not one company's goods. That makes it a poor trade mark for anyone, and no claim over it was found. Taxi precedents point the same way: London's cab shape failed as a trade mark, and NYC regulates its "taxi yellow" colour but its brand is the city-designed logo and markings. Use the colours freely. Fictionalise the official seals, permit stickers, the state logo and real registration markings.

### Cited Findings
- **[Regulator/official, via press] 2014.** Lagos Transport Commissioner Kayode Opeifa warned that commercial passenger vehicles "not painted in the state approved yellow colour with black stripes would not be allowed to operate in any motor parks, garages or on the roads." — [Vanguard, May 2014 (S)](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2014/05/lagos-starts-enforcement-commercial-vehicles-re-certification/)
- **[Regulator/official, via press] November 2022.** LASTMA was directed to impound commercial buses not in the approved colours: yellow with black stripes, with two-door mass-transit buses in red. The GM cited the **Lagos State Transport Sector Reform Law 2018**. — [Gazette NGR (S)](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-begins-impoundment-of-unpainted-commercial-vehicles/); [Ripples Nigeria (S)](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-to-impound-unpainted-commercial-vehicles/?amp)
- **[Journalism]** A 2024 ban on danfo and korope buses applied only to the Lekki–Epe axis, from 1 October 2024, not statewide. — [AllAfrica (S)](https://allafrica.com/stories/202407300159.html)
- **[Fan/forum]** Wiktionary describes the typical danfo as "a VW transporter bus, painted in cadmium yellow with two black stripes." — [Wiktionary (S)](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/danfo)
- **[Regulator/official] NYC.** A 1968 City Council bill barred non-medallion cabs from typical cab colours, and the city made yellow the official medallion-cab colour in 1970. A city–operator agreement states the city "has designed, or obtained the design" of original symbols and logos for the medallion taxi exterior markings. A 2005 redesign was pitched as creating "revenue-generating trademarks and copyrights for the City." — [Time (S)](https://time.com/4640097/yellow-taxi-cabs-history/); [NYC TLC MOU (S)](https://www.nyc.gov:443/assets/tlc/downloads/pdf/mou_printer.pdf); [Adland, 2005 (S)](https://www.adland.tv/new-york-taxis-get-new-look); [NYC TLC "Your ride" (S)](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/passengers/your-ride.page)
- **[Case] London Taxi v Frazer-Nash [2017] EWCA Civ 1729.** The iconic, widely recognised cab shape was invalid as a trade mark (see section 1). — [RPC (S)](https://rpc.co.uk/thinking/ip/court-of-appeal-drives-through-london-taxis-hopes-of-enforcing-protection-for-its-shape-marks)

### Inferences
- A colour every licensed operator must use cannot show that goods come from one trader, which is the core requirement for trade mark protection. Neither an operator nor NURTW could plausibly own "danfo yellow + black stripes." Lagos State could own its **logo, crest and the specific designs of official stickers and markings**, as NYC does with its medallion markings. It does not own the colour as such.
- Risk from Lagos State is mostly **political and reputational, not IP**. If anything, the state could object to its emblem appearing, or to its agencies being shown as villains (see section 3).
- **Do:** use yellow with black stripes, and vary stripe width, wear, rust and hand-painted repairs. Optionally include red two-door "mass transit" variants, which matches the 2022 directive.
- **Fictionalise:** route-permit and "hackney" stickers, union and park stickers, state certification decals and any "Lagos State" stencil or seal. Make them generic, with invented emblems and text.

### Gaps
- The text of the Lagos State Transport Sector Reform Law 2018 colour provisions was not retrieved. The origin of the yellow mandate (often attributed to the 1990s Marwa administration) is unverified.
- No Lagos State trademark registrations were checked; no Nigerian trademark database was reachable.

## 3. Official insignia: Lagos State arms and logo, LASTMA, LAMATA, BRT, NURTW/RTEAN, police uniforms and number plates

### Takeaway
Fictionalise every emblem, logo, uniform badge and plate design. The national coat of arms and flag are restricted by statute when used "in connection with" trade. Police uniforms and insignia are guarded under the Police Act 2020. State, agency, transit-operator and union logos are copyrighted artwork and likely registered marks. Apple also bars making a real government or entity the sole target of enemies. Keep **functional roles** (traffic warden, transit bus, union tout, police checkpoint) using **invented names, colours and badges**.

### Cited Findings
- **[Statute] Flags and Coats of Arms Act** (Cap F30 LFN 2004; earlier Cap 148, 1990). Section 6 restricts use of the National Flag, the National Coat of Arms "or any flag or arms closely resembling" them in connection with any trade, business, calling or profession without a licence from the Minister or other lawful authority. A 2017 Daily Trust report said the penalty section (s.10) set a fine of **₦100**, and a House bill proposed **₦100,000**. Whether that bill passed is unknown. — [PLAC Laws of Nigeria (S)](https://www.placng.org/lawsofnigeria/view2.php?sn=188); [WIPO Lex (S)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/222497); [Daily Trust via PressReader, 18 May 2017 (S)](https://www.pressreader.com/nigeria/daily-trust/20170518/282067686866011)
- **[Regulator/official, via press] July 2026.** The National Orientation Agency (NOA) said altering or misrepresenting the flag violates the Act. — [Gazette NGR (S)](https://gazettengr.com/independence-day-noa-urges-nigerians-to-use-correct-national-symbols/)
- **[Regulator/official, via press] Police uniforms.** In 2022 the Nigeria Police Force suspended the Edo Police Community Relations Committee (PCRC) chair because members wore black uniforms with insignia similar to the Force's. It cited s.214(1) of the 1999 Constitution and **s.101 of the Police Act 2020**. The IGP has also ordered action against sale of police uniforms outside approved outlets. Supernumerary (SPY) officers must wear their own distinct uniform. The text of s.101 was not retrieved. — [TheCable (S)](https://thecable.ng/police-suspend-edo-community-relations-committee-chair-over-illegal-use-of-force-uniform); [Gazette NGR (S)](https://gazettengr.com/edo-pcrc-chairperson-suspended-for-wearing-police-uniform-accoutrements/); [TheCable on IGP orders (S)](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=673562)
- **[Regulator/official] Apple 1.1.2 (F, paraphrased by the fetch tool).** In a game, "enemies" cannot solely target a real race, culture, government, corporation or other real entity. Apple 1.1.1 also bars defamatory or mean-spirited content likely to humiliate a targeted group. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- A commercial game showing the **national coat of arms** (on a plate, a police badge or a government building) is arguably use "in connection with" a business. Enforcement looks negligible and the fine trivial, but there is no upside, so leave it out.
- The **Lagos State crest and logo, and the LASTMA, LAMATA, BRT and operator, NURTW and RTEAN logos** are original artistic works protected by copyright, and very likely registered marks (unverified). Copying them is unnecessary. Players recognise these roles from **function, colour family and context**, not from the exact crest.
- **Uniforms.** The Police Act concerns people wearing uniforms, not depictions in a game. Even so, exact police badges, rank insignia and force crests are best avoided: they are official emblems, and showing "the Nigeria Police Force" as an antagonist meets Apple 1.1.2 and defamation risk. **Fictionalise:** a "traffic officer" in an invented uniform colourway and badge, and a generic "checkpoint" without the real force crest or name.
- **Number plates.** Use an invented plate design with no coat of arms, no "Federal Republic of Nigeria" header and no real FRSC layout. Use number formats that cannot match a real vehicle. A nod to the Lagos plate style (colour band, a fictional slogan) is fine.
- **Agency names.** Avoid one-letter-off acronyms of real agencies. They read as a direct reference while forfeiting a clear parody defence, which is untested in Nigeria. Generic descriptive titles ("Traffic Wardens," "City Transit," "Drivers' Union park boys") are safer.

### Gaps
- Whether Nigerian law (the Trade Marks Act Cap T13, or a Lagos State law) separately protects **state** emblems; no source found. Article 6ter of the Paris Convention covers state emblems against *trademark registration*, not depiction in games (background knowledge, unverified).
- No data on whether LASTMA, LAMATA, NURTW or RTEAN logos are registered trademarks.
- Text and penalty of Police Act 2020 s.101; current FRSC plate specification.

## 4. Buildings and landmarks: Nigeria's Copyright Act 2022, panorama exceptions, and precedents for real landmarks in games

### Takeaway
Nigerian copyright protects architectural works as artistic works. The 2022 Act cut the old broad panorama exception down to **s.20(1)(e): including a publicly viewable artistic work in an "audiovisual work or broadcast."** Commentators disagree on its scope, and it is untested whether a video game counts as an "audiovisual work." Bridges may fall outside "architectural work," but their design can still be an artistic work. Real abroad-precedents (Chrysler Building in *Spider-Man*, Empire State dilution, Eiffel Tower lighting) show the risk lies with **privately owned, branded icons, sculptures and light shows**, not ordinary infrastructure. **Use real place names and recognisable but simplified, non-replica geometry for roads, bridges and interchanges. Fictionalise private malls, hotels, stadium sponsor names and public sculptures.**

### Cited Findings
- **[Practitioner]** The Copyright Act puts architectural works under artistic works, covering drawings, plans and models, and buildings that reproduce a substantial part of an original design. *Ifeanyi Okoye v Prompt & Quality Services* is cited for the point that original architectural works qualify. The same commentary says **bridges may be excluded from "architectural works"** because they are not habitable buildings, but an original bridge design could still be protected as an artistic work. — [Mondaq, Architectural works and IP laws in Nigeria (S)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/nigeria/trademark/1708348/architectural-works-and-intellectual-property-laws-in-nigeria)
- **[Statute, via summaries]** **s.20(1)(e) of the Copyright Act 2022** allows including an artistic work that is situated where the public can view it in an **audiovisual work or broadcast**. The repealed 2004 Act more broadly excluded reproduction and distribution of copies of permanently sited artistic works. — [Wikimedia Commons, copyright rules: Nigeria (S)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Nigeria); [NCC official Act PDF (S, blocked)](https://www.copyright.gov.ng/CopyrightAct/CopyrightAct2023FinalPublication1.pdf); [PLAC copy (S, blocked)](https://placng.org/i/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Copyright-Act-2022.pdf)
- **[Practitioner, conflicting]**
  - Free Knowledge Africa says the 2022 Act "revoked" freedom of panorama, so any image of a copyrighted artwork or structure in public now needs permission, and commercial uses still need it. It also mentions non-commercial and educational limits that do **not** appear in the quoted statutory text.
  - Legal Bytes says s.20 lets public sculptures, buildings and monuments appear in films or documentaries without permission.

  — [Free Knowledge Africa, Restoring FoP (S)](https://freeknowledgeafrica.org/restoring-freedom-of-panorama-in-nigerias-copyright-act-a-call-for-balance/); [Free Knowledge Africa, Navigating the 2022 Act (S)](https://freeknowledgeafrica.org/navigating-the-new-nigerian-copyright-act-of-2022/); [Legal Bytes (S)](https://legalbytes.substack.com/p/analysing-the-nigerian-copyright)
- **[Practitioner]** The 2022 Act widened fair dealing and added institutional exceptions, replacing the 2004 Second Schedule. — [Banwo & Ighodalo (S)](https://banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/modifications-to-fair-dealing-and-other-exceptions-under-the-new-copyright-act)
- **[Regulator/official] Lekki–Ikoyi Link Bridge.** Julius Berger designed it with AS+P; it was Nigeria's first cable-stayed structure. No source discusses copyright in it. — [AS+P (S)](https://www.as-p.com/projects/lekki-ikoyi-link-bridge-291); [Structurae (S)](https://structurae.net/en/structures/lekki-ikoyi-link-bridge)
- **[Practitioner] Chrysler Building.** Insomniac negotiated with co-owners SIGNA Group and RFR Holding to include it in *Marvel's Spider-Man* (2018), "but was unable to reach an agreement," so it was left out. — [Goodmans IP blog (S)](https://www.goodmans.ca/insights/post/goodmans-ip-blog/copyright-issues-to-blame-for-chrysler-building-s-absence-in-spider-man-game); [Propmodo (S)](https://propmodo.com/?p=4993)
- **[Case] Empire State Building.** The TTAB sustained ESRT's opposition to a beer logo using the building's image, finding the image a famous mark likely to be diluted; a dissent questioned whether the public links the image to the owner. ESRT licenses the image and in 2024 filed an application covering downloadable video and NFT artwork of the building. **No claim against a video game was found.** — [Carter Ledyard & Milburn case study (S)](https://www.cll.com/casestudy-14); [BFKN (S)](https://www.bfkn.com/newsroom/publications/Cant-Make-It-There-Cant-Make-That-Mark-Says-TTAB); [Justia trademark record (S)](https://trademark.justia.com/984/05/empire-state-98405665.html)
- **[Case/Practitioner] Eiffel Tower.** The tower itself is in the public domain, but SETE treats the **night lighting** as a separate work and requires approval for night images; a Paris appeal court in 1990 treated the lighting as an original creation. Commentators say the lighting's status is not clearly settled, and enforcement targets commercial use. — [Dreyfus (S)](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2015/12/11/nighttime-photos-of-the-eiffel-tower-is-the-lighting-protected-by-copyright/); [Global Legal Post (S)](https://www.globallegalpost.com/blogs/by-day-and-by-night-45-two-different-stories-at-the-eiffel-tower-6410796); [Snopes (S)](https://www.snopes.com/photographs-of-eiffel-tower-at-night/)
- **[Conflict flag]** One search summary said the US Architectural Works Copyright Protection Act (1990) gives owners "broad rights over completed architectural images." That conflicts with 17 U.S.C. §120(a), which allows pictorial representations of buildings visible from public places. That is background knowledge, not verified this session. Whether a 3D game model is a "pictorial representation" is debated.

### Inferences
- **Roads, viaducts, interchanges and generic bridges** (Third Mainland Bridge's long low concrete viaduct, Eko Bridge, Carter Bridge, Ojuelegba flyover) are largely functional engineering. Depict them in **stylised, simplified, non-survey-grade form** with real names. Under the Mondaq view, bridges are not even "architectural works." Distinctive *designed* features, such as a cable-stayed pylon or a branded arch, deserve more abstraction: a generic cable-stayed bridge is fine; a measured replica of the Lekki–Ikoyi pylon is less safe.
- **Oshodi Transport Interchange, stadiums (Teslim Balogun, the National Stadium), malls, hotels and corporate towers** are designed buildings with identifiable owners, and often trademarks or sponsor names. Give them **original architecture that hints at their massing and role**, with fictional names on signage. The geographic name of a district (Oshodi, Yaba, Obalende, Marina, CMS) is a place name, free to use in dialogue, route boards and stop names.
- **Public sculptures, murals and monuments** are where s.20(1)(e) gives the least comfort, since a game may not be an "audiovisual work." Replace them with original sculptures.
- **Light shows and branded lighting** on bridges or towers (e.g., Lekki–Ikoyi bridge lighting): use generic lighting.
- Real place names are geographic facts. The bigger risk is using a landmark or brand name in the **game title or marketing** in a way that implies endorsement.

### Gaps
- **Lawyer question:** Is a video game an "audiovisual work" under the 2022 Act, and does s.20(1)(e) cover permanent 3D depiction inside a game? Exact definitions were not retrievable.
- No Nigerian case on landmarks in games, films or photographs was found.
- Who owns the copyright in specific Lagos designs (Lekki–Ikoyi Bridge, Oshodi Interchange, Eko Atlantic towers) is unknown.

## 5. Text and slogans: short phrases, scripture translations, and real church or business names on buses

### Takeaway
Typical danfo inscriptions ("No Condition Is Permanent," "God's Time Is The Best," "Monkey Dey Work Baboon Dey Chop," route call-outs) are short phrases, proverbs and place names. Copyright generally does not protect phrases this short. Trademark law matters only if a phrase is used as a brand. **Scripture:** the KJV is public domain almost everywhere except the UK, where perpetual Crown rights apply. Modern translations (NIV, ESV, NKJV and others) are copyrighted with quotation allowances, and their conditions vary. Use KJV or your own paraphrase. **Real church, mosque or business names on buses: fictionalise.**

### Cited Findings
- **[Journalism]** Real danfos carry personal inscriptions and slogans. — [BellaNaija, Lagos through the eyes of a danfo driver, Jan 2022 (from earlier project research)](https://www.bellanaija.com/2022/01/bird-lagos-danfo-driver/)
- **[Regulator/official, publisher policies] Bible quotation allowances:**
  - The American Bible Society (CEV, GNT, RVR60) allows up to 500 verses without written permission, provided scripture is under 25% of the work and under 50% of any book, with a copyright notice and non-commercial use.
  - Crossway (ESV) and Thomas Nelson (NKJV) also allow 500 verses with the 25% and half-book limits, excluding commentaries.
  - Holman (CSB) allows 1,000 verses.

  — [American Bible Society (S)](https://bibles.com/pages/american-bible-society-rights-and-permissions); [Crossway ESV permissions (S)](https://www.crossway.org/rights-and-permissions/esv/); [BibleGateway NKJV (S)](https://biblegateway.com/versions/New-King-James-Version-NKJV-Bible)
- **[Fan/forum]** No official NIV (Biblica/Zondervan) policy was retrieved. One forum post claims Zondervan allows quotation under 25% of a work and refuses permission for pre-2011 NIV text. This is unverified. — [Christian Writers forum (S)](https://christianwriters.com/threads/quoting-bible-verses.26590)
- **[Practitioner/low-quality]** The KJV is public domain in the US and most of the world. In the UK the Crown holds perpetual rights, licensed through letters patent (the King's Printer in England, Wales and Northern Ireland; the Scottish Bible Board in Scotland). Modern KJV study editions can carry copyrighted notes. Sources disagree on how strictly UK rights are enforced. — [eBible.org public-domain page (S)](https://ebible.org/publicdomain.htm); [Christianity Knowledge Base (S)](https://christianity.fandom.com/wiki/King_James_Version); [Puritan Board (S)](https://puritanboard.com/threads/is-the-kjv-bible-copyrighted.95840/latest)
- **[Regulator/official] Apple 2.3.9 (F).** Display fictional account information rather than a real person's data in screenshots. More broadly, rights must be secured for all materials shown. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **Short slogans and proverbs are free to use**, both as copyright-unprotectable short phrases and as common sayings. Do not reproduce a long, distinctive passage written by an identifiable person (song lyrics, a poem, a famous preacher's sermon line).
- **Scripture.** Short KJV verses, or the team's own paraphrase, are safe worldwide in practice. The UK Crown right technically covers KJV text in the UK; enforcement against brief verses is not documented. Avoid the NIV (no confirmed policy). Avoid Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa Bible translations and any Quran translation unless their licence status is confirmed. **Arabic Quranic text itself** (the 7th-century source) is public domain. Its treatment on vehicles that crash and get dirty is a **religious-sensitivity** question, not an IP one (see section 12).
- **Church, mosque and ministry names** (e.g., real "Redeemed," "Winners," "Deeper Life," "MFM" branding), **real business names** and **real phone numbers** on bus bodies risk trademark, passing-off and defamation claims, and spam calls to real people. Invent them ("Grace Abundant Assembly," "Mama Tee Provisions"), and make fictional phone numbers impossible formats.
- **Conductor route calls** ("Oshodi! Oshodi! Obalende! CMS! Wole, wole!") are place names and functional speech, so write them freely. The recording issue is consent (see section 8).

### Gaps
- No Nigerian-law source on short-phrase copyright was retrieved. The rule is general copyright principle (inference).
- Copyright status of the Yoruba Bible (Bibeli Mimo, Bible Society of Nigeria), Hausa and Igbo Bibles, and common English Quran translations (Yusuf Ali, Pickthall, Sahih International). Not retrieved; search budget exhausted.

## 6. Brands in the street: fictionalising Nigerian brands, writing parody names, and Apple/Google trademark policy

### Takeaway
Do **not** show MTN, Glo, Airtel, Dangote, Indomie, Peak, Milo, Coca-Cola, Bet9ja, SportyBet or any other real brand without a licence. Apple 5.2.1 bars third-party trademarks "without permission." Google acts on trademark complaints and its impersonation policy. Unlicensed brand use also hands rights-holders an easy takedown. Follow GTA's model: **original parody brands with new names, logos and colour pairings that wink at a category without copying any one company's mark or trade dress.** Betting brands raise a second problem (gambling promotion), so leave them out entirely or replace them with a neutral fictional lottery kiosk.

### Cited Findings
- **[Regulator/official] Apple (F).** 5.2.1: no "protected third-party material such as trademarks… without permission," and no "copycat representations." 2.3.7: no trademarked terms in metadata. 2.3.9: secure rights to all icon, screenshot and preview material. 4.1(c): no other developer's brand in the app icon or name. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Regulator/official, secondary] Google Play.** Its policy bars apps that "use another app or entity's brand, title, logo, or name in a manner that may result in misleading users." Rights-holders report infringements through Google's legal-complaint process, and the remedy is to prove permission or remove the content. — [Kodular forum (S)](https://community.kodular.io/t/app-suspended-from-playstore/29878); [GoodBarber (S)](https://www.goodbarber.com/help/google-rejection-r101/google-rejection-intellectual-property-infringement-a223); [XDA (S)](https://www.xda-developers.com/google-play-store-policies-and-common-mistakes-to-avoid/)
- **[Journalism] GTA's practice.** Fictional brands with fictional logos also appear on fictional billboards and radio ads. A real licence would not cover that range of use. — [Giant Bomb (S)](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Fictional_Car_Brands); [SlashGear (S)](https://www.slashgear.com/1318255/gta-cars-in-real-life/)
- **[Case] Exclusive "depiction in games" licences exist** (Saber v Oovee), so rights-holders treat in-game appearances as licensable. — [FKKS (S)](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102hzos/a-tale-of-two-truck-simulator-games-and-the-first-amendment)
- **[Case] Jack Daniel's (2023).** Parody gets less protection when the parody mark works as a brand for the defendant's own product. — [Proskauer (S)](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/in-jack-daniels-case-supreme-court-rejects-ninth-circuits-expansive-view-of-first-amendment-rogers-test)
- **[Case] Brand-name apps can be ordered off stores** (Delhi HC, "PUMA GUARD"). — [LiveLaw (S)](https://livelaw.in/news-updates/delhi-high-court-puma-guard-trademark-infringement-suit-puma-200115)

### Inferences
- **Why fictionalise even in the background:** Apple's wording is not limited to prominent use. A billboard or kiosk logo is still a third-party trademark in the binary and in screenshots. Telecoms, FMCG and betting companies in Nigeria advertise heavily and police their marks. And a real brand placed next to crashes, robbery jokes or broken-down buses invites a disparagement complaint.
- **Parody-name rules of thumb:**
  1. Change the **name substantially**. No one-letter swaps ("Indomee," "MTM"). Aim for a new coinage that evokes the category ("Noodlo," "SabiNet Telecom," "Cemento Group").
  2. **Do not copy colour plus shape together.** MTN's yellow oval, Glo's green, Airtel's red swirl, Indomie's red-and-yellow packet and Peak's tin are each trade dress; pick different combinations.
  3. **Do not copy slogans or jingles.**
  4. **Keep jokes non-disparaging and non-factual.** No implied claims that a real product is unsafe.
  5. **Keep parody brands out of store icons and key art**, where they look like endorsements.
  6. **Trademark-search the parody names** for live marks in Nigeria, US and EU (free searches on the WIPO Global Brand Database or USPTO), so the parody does not collide with someone else's real brand.
- **Betting:** sports-betting brands are omnipresent on Lagos streets, but a free game rated for general audiences would be advertising gambling. Leave them out, or show a fictional "pools" kiosk without odds or calls to action.

### Gaps
- Google Play's IP and Impersonation policy text was not read first-hand (support.google.com blocked).
- No specific enforcement history by MTN, Glo, Dangote, Indomie (Dufil) or betting brands against games was found. Not searched; budget exhausted.

## 7. People and likeness: governors, celebrities and street personalities

### Takeaway
Nigeria has **no image-rights statute**. Unauthorised commercial use of a recognisable person is still actionable through the constitutional right to privacy, passing off, and, since 2023, the **Nigeria Data Protection Act**: a Lagos High Court held that unconsented commercial use of a photo infringed privacy and the NDPA. US courts have also refused First Amendment cover for games using real athletes' likenesses. **Use only original fictional characters. No lookalikes of the governor, the president, Afrobeats stars, Nollywood actors or famous street figures, and no soundalike voices.**

### Cited Findings
- **[Practitioner]** "There is no comprehensive piece of legislation on image rights in Nigeria." Claimants use the constitutional right to privacy, civil claims for unauthorised use, passing off, copyright and trademark. For public figures, the commercial-exploitation route is often stronger than privacy. — [BusinessDay, celebrity image rights (S)](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/how-celebrity-image-rights-are-protected-under-nigerian-law/); [IBA (S)](https://www.ibanet.org/image-rights-protection-nigerian-law)
- **[Case] Nwabueze v Nigerian Breweries (Lagos High Court).** Per Templars' analysis, unauthorised commercial use of a person's photograph in advertising, without valid consent, "infringes the person's constitutional right to privacy and amounts to unlawful processing of personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023." Templars notes the decision is not binding precedent. — [Templars, Commercial Use of Individuals' Images, Aug 2026 (S)](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2026/08/Commercial-Use-of-Individuals-Images.pdf)
- **[Case]** Actor Richard Mofe-Damijo sued Jumia over unauthorised use of his image; outcome not found. **[Practitioner]** A photographer's copyright is no shield against these claims. — [IBA (S)](https://www.ibanet.org/image-rights-protection-nigerian-law); [Mondaq, Friction of rights (S)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1210880/friction-of-rights-copyrights-in-a-photograph-image-rights-and-data-protection-rights-of-the-photographed-balancing-the-rights)
- **[Practitioner, US]** A series of cases deny First Amendment protection to games using avatars of real athletes. — [FKKS (S)](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102hzos/a-tale-of-two-truck-simulator-games-and-the-first-amendment)
- **[Regulator/official] Apple 1.1.1 (F).** No defamatory or mean-spirited content, especially content likely to humiliate a targeted individual or group; professional political satirists are generally exempt. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Political satire of a sitting governor would put an ads-only game in political territory, with defamation, store review (1.1.1 protects "professional political satirists," which a small studio would struggle to show) and safety concerns for local team members. It adds little to a driving game. **Leave real politicians out**, including campaign posters with real faces. Fictional generic campaign posters for invented candidates and parties are fine.
- **Celebrities and street personalities** (famous conductors, comedians, skit-makers): no lookalikes, no catchphrases closely tied to one person, no voice imitation.
- **Reference-photo hygiene:** characters must not be modelled on identifiable people from photos (see section 9).

### Gaps
- Primary text of the Nwabueze judgment and NDPA provisions on images and voice as personal data were not retrieved.
- No source on Nigerian treatment of *game* (rather than advertising) uses of likeness.

## 8. Music and sound: Nigerian music licensing, sampling, radio, field recording in public, and voice-actor consent

### Takeaway
**Use only original or CC0/CC-BY music, and no real songs.** Nigerian collective management is in flux. MCSN is the only NCC-approved music collective management organisation (CMO). COSON lost its approval in 2018–19 and, per one report, lost a Court of Appeal ruling in July 2026. Parliament has opened an inquiry. Neither offers a cheap blanket "game sync" licence. The 2022 Act adds **performers' rights** (consent to fixation) and **criminally enforced folklore protection**, which matters for traditional songs and chants. **Field recordings:** ambient street sound is not a copyright work, but identifiable voices are personal data under the NDPA. Get written releases from anyone recognisable, edit out radios and loudspeakers playing real songs, and never use recordings of Emeka Ogboh-style soundscape art.

### Cited Findings
- **[Regulator/official, via press]**
  - The NCC suspended COSON's approval in April 2018, and it lapsed in May 2019.
  - The NCC renewed MCSN's approval in December 2020 and reaffirmed it in 2025.
  - A blog reports a three-judge Court of Appeal panel in Lagos dismissed COSON's appeal on 9 July 2026, quoting the NCC: COSON is "not currently approved to operate as a Collective Management Organisation in Nigeria." This is single-sourced.
  - On 10 June 2026 the House of Representatives directed its Committee on Justice to investigate alleged irregularities in copyright licensing.

  — [Interspace Music (S)](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/coson-court-of-appeal-mcsn-royalty-collection-nigeria/); [Guardian Nigeria, MCSN approval (S)](https://guardian.ng/art/onwenu-tee-mac-others-applaud-approval-of-mcsn-as-collective-society/); [IPKat guest post, July 2026 (S, blocked)](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2026/07/guest-post-nigerias-copyright-licensing.html)
- **[Statute, via practitioner summaries] Performers' rights.**
  - These sit in Part VIII (ss.63–73) of the Copyright Act 2022.
  - Performers have exclusive rights to control fixation of their live performances.
  - Consent to broadcasting is presumed to extend to rebroadcast and to fixation for broadcast unless otherwise agreed (s.65).
  - The term is reportedly 50 years (unverified).

  — [Aelex overview (S)](https://aelex.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/AN-OVERVIEW-OF-THE-COPYRIGHT-ACT-2022.pdf); [Mondaq, review of the 2022 Act (S)](https://mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1328732/review-of-the-copyright-act-2022); [Mondaq overview (S)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1332056/an-overview-of-the-copyright-act-2022)
- **[Statute, via practitioner summaries] Expressions of folklore.**
  - These sit in Part IX (ss.74–76).
  - Section 74 protects folklore against reproduction, performance, broadcasting, adaptation and similar uses made **for commercial purposes or outside their traditional or customary context**.
  - One source says consent from the relevant communities and/or the NCC is needed before commercialisation, with **criminal liability** for failure.

  — [Mondaq overview (S)](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/copyright/1332056/an-overview-of-the-copyright-act-2022); [BusinessDay, 7 ways the Act benefits creatives (S)](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/7-ways-the-copyright-act-2022-benefits-creatives/)
- **[Case] Unconsented commercial use of a person's image** breached privacy and the NDPA (*Nwabueze*, persuasive). — [Templars (S)](https://www.templars-law.com/app/uploads/2026/08/Commercial-Use-of-Individuals-Images.pdf)
- **[Licence text] CC BY-SA 4.0 (F).** "Where the Licensed Material is a musical work, performance, or sound recording, Adapted Material is **always** produced where the Licensed Material is synched in timed relation with a moving image." All CC 4.0 licences also state that publicity, privacy and personality rights, and patent and trademark rights, are **not licensed**. — [CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code (SPDX mirror)](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt)
- **[Regulator/official] Apple 5.2.5 (F).** Apple Music and iTunes previews may not be used as a game soundtrack. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Practitioner/artist]** Emeka Ogboh's *Lagos Soundscapes* documents conductor verbal mapping. It is an artwork in its own right, protected by copyright. — [Google Arts & Culture / African Artists' Foundation (from earlier project research)](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/PAXRr0YoJHcxIQ)

### Inferences
- **Commercial Nigerian tracks** need two permissions: the composition (publisher, or MCSN for some uses) and the master (label). Game synchronisation is normally licensed directly, not through CMO blanket licences (general industry practice, not sourced here). Given CMO turmoil and no budget, **do not license real tracks.**
- **In-game radio:** original stations with invented names (not Cool FM, Wazobia FM, Beat FM, Naija FM). Use original or CC0 music, original jingles and fictional DJs. Afrobeats, fuji, juju, highlife and gospel *styles* are not ownable, but **no melodies, hooks, lyrics or soundalikes of specific songs**, and **no samples** of any recording.
- **CC BY-SA music** in a game always counts as an adaptation, which triggers ShareAlike on the adapted work. Prefer CC0 or CC-BY music.
- **Folklore:** avoid traditional songs, chants, oriki (praise poetry), masquerade or ceremonial music, and recognisable traditional melodies, unless a lawyer confirms the s.74 position or the community or NCC consents. Original music *in a traditional idiom* is a grey area under s.74's "adaptation" wording, so keep it clearly original.
- **Field recording in public (Nigeria):** no statute bans recording in public places (none was found), and generic street noise is not a work. However:
  1. Identifiable voices (a conductor's distinctive call, a named hawker) are personal data, so get **written consent** or make them unidentifiable by mixing, layering, pitch-shifting or using crowd walla.
  2. **Radios and shop speakers** in the background carry copyrighted songs, so remove them.
  3. A conductor's stylised chant could arguably be a "performance," so a release also covers performers' rights.
- **Voice actors and contributors:** use a written release covering worldwide, perpetual, all-media use in a commercial ad-supported game, with the right to edit. Add an **express clause on any AI voice synthesis or cloning** (allowed or forbidden), a credit preference, and NDPA-compliant data handling (purpose, retention, withdrawal). Minors need parental consent and are best avoided.

### Gaps
- Primary text of ss.63–76 and the definition of "folklore" were not retrieved. **A lawyer is needed** if any traditional material is used.
- Whether MCSN offers a game or sync licence at all; no source.
- NDPA provisions on voice recordings and exemptions for artistic or journalistic purposes were not retrieved.

## 9. Reference photos and textures: reference vs tracing, CC licence types, and typical Wikimedia Commons danfo licences

### Takeaway
**Looking at photos to learn forms, proportions, wear and colour is fine. Tracing, projecting or texturing directly from someone else's photo copies their expression.** Use photos only as reference, or use the team's own photos. For shipped pixels, the order of preference is **CC0, then CC-BY with credit, then CC-BY-SA only with care.** **Never NC:** an ad-supported game is "directed towards… monetary compensation." Lagos photos on Wikimedia Commons are typically CC BY-SA 4.0, and they also show people and brands that CC does not clear.

### Cited Findings
- **[Licence text] CC BY-NC 4.0 (F).** "NonCommercial means not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation." — [CC BY-NC 4.0 legal code (CC's GitHub)](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[Licence text] CC attribution, s.3(a) (F).** When sharing, including modified versions, retain where supplied:
  - creator identification,
  - the copyright notice,
  - a licence notice,
  - the disclaimer notice,
  - a URI to the material where reasonably practicable.

  State whether the material was modified, and include the licence text or link. This "may be satisfied in any reasonable manner based on the medium," for example a link to a credits page. — [CC BY-NC 4.0 legal code](https://github.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/blob/main/docs/licenses/by-nc/4.0/legalcode.en.html)
- **[Licence text] CC BY-SA 4.0 ShareAlike, s.3(b) (F).** If you share Adapted Material, your Adapter's License must be BY-SA (same or later version) or a compatible licence. You must include its text or link. You "may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, Adapted Material that restrict exercise of the rights granted." Section 2(a)(5)(C) bars imposing technological measures that restrict recipients' rights in the Licensed Material itself. — [CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code (SPDX mirror)](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt)
- **[Licence text] CC 4.0 s.2(b) (F).** Moral rights, publicity, privacy and personality rights are not licensed, and "Patent and trademark rights are not licensed." — [CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt)
- **[Practitioner/Fan] Guides for game developers:**
  - NC assets are "generally unsuitable for commercial game releases, as even free games often generate indirect revenue."
  - A developer forum asked whether ads make a free game commercial and reached no clear answer.
  - Opinions differ on CC BY-SA in closed commercial games.

  — [Wayline (S)](https://www.wayline.io/blog/creative-commons-license-explained-game-developers); [Cinevva (S)](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/game-asset-licenses); [B4X forum (S)](https://www.b4x.com/android/forum/threads/doubt-about-assets-license-terms.82402/); [OpenGameArt (S)](https://opengameart.org/node/147349)

  - Wole Hammond, *The Danfo of Lagos*: CC BY-SA 4.0.

- **[Regulator/official]** Poly Haven and ambientCG describe their downloadable assets as CC0 (earlier project research). — [Poly Haven licence](https://polyhaven.com/license); [ambientCG licence](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/)

### Inferences
- **Reference vs copying.** Copyright protects a photographer's expression (framing, lighting, the specific captured image), not the facts of what a danfo looks like. **Safe:** studying many photos, then modelling and painting original textures. **Risky:** photo-projecting or camera-mapping a CC BY-SA or all-rights-reserved photo onto a mesh, or tracing hand-painted bus art from a single photo. A specific sign-writer's hand-lettering is itself an artistic work.
- **Watch for third-party content inside a CC photo:** bus inscriptions with real church names, telecom ads, faces and plate numbers are not cleared by the photographer's licence (s.2(b)).
- **CC BY-SA in an app-store game:** a texture made from a BY-SA photo is likely Adapted Material that must be released under BY-SA. **App-store DRM (e.g., FairPlay) could arguably be an "Effective Technological Measure"** restricting it. This conflict is unresolved, so **keep BY-SA pixels out of shipped builds**, or have a lawyer confirm a separate-file approach.
- **NC is out.** Ads make the game "directed towards monetary compensation."
- **Provenance:** log every reference and shipped asset in the existing source/licence register: URL, author, licence version, date checked, whether shipped or reference-only, and modifications.

### Gaps
- A broad sample of Commons "Danfo" category licences could not be pulled (blocked). Three Lagos images recorded earlier were all CC BY-SA 4.0.
- No authoritative ruling on whether ad-supported free apps are "commercial" under NC. The wording strongly suggests yes.

## 10. Fonts: OFL vs proprietary, Google Fonts "Danfo", and Afrotype's terms

### Takeaway
**Google Fonts does host a "Danfo" family** (added 15 March 2024). It was designed by Afrotype (Seyi Olusanya, Eyiyemi Adegbite, David Udoh, with Mirko Velimirović) and is inspired by Lagos bus vinyl cut-out lettering. It is under the **SIL Open Font License 1.1, with no Reserved Font Name**. It can be embedded in a commercial ad-supported game if the copyright notice and OFL text are shipped (e.g., on a licences screen), the font is not sold on its own, and Afrotype's endorsement is not implied. Avoid "free for personal use" display fonts; most decorative freebies carry that restriction.

### Cited Findings
- **[Regulator/official] Google Fonts metadata (F).**
  - Family "Danfo"; designers Afrotype, Seyi Olusanya, Eyiyemi Adegbite, David Udoh, Mirko Velimirović.
  - Licence OFL; date added 2024-03-15; category Serif.
  - Subsets latin, latin-ext, vietnamese; variable **ELSH axis 0–100**; source github.com/Afrotype/danfo.

  — [google/fonts METADATA.pb](https://github.com/google/fonts/blob/main/ofl/danfo/METADATA.pb)
- **[Regulator/official] Afrotype repository (F).** The ELSH axis has three main styles at 0, 50 and 100, covering the Google sub-Saharan African character set. The styles "reflect different cut-out shapes inspired by the vinyl cut-out lettering on public buses in Lagos, Nigeria." It is licensed under SIL OFL 1.1. — [Afrotype/danfo on GitHub](https://github.com/Afrotype/danfo)
- **[Licence text] OFL.txt (F).** "Copyright 2023 The Danfo Project Authors (https://github.com/Afrotype/danfo)," with no Reserved Font Name stated. Its conditions:
  1. The font may not be sold by itself.
  2. It may be bundled, redistributed or sold with software, provided each copy includes the copyright notice and licence (separate file, header or metadata).
  3. Reserved names apply only if declared (none here).
  4. Authors' names may not be used to promote modified versions without permission.
  5. The font and any derivatives stay under the OFL, but **documents created with the font are not covered by the licence**.

  — [Afrotype/danfo OFL.txt](https://github.com/Afrotype/danfo/blob/main/OFL.txt)
- **[Practitioner/designer]** Afrotype's Danfo project page describes field visits, photography and sketching behind the bus-inspired lettering (earlier project research). — [Afrotype, Danfo](https://www.afrotype.com/danfo)

### Inferences
- Embedding Danfo, or any OFL font, in a Godot export (inside the PCK or APK) is "bundling with software." Put OFL.txt and the copyright line in the in-game licences or credits screen and the repo licence register. Rendered UI text and baked textures are "documents created using the font" and fall outside the OFL.
- **Naming overlap:** a game called "Danfo" using a font called "Danfo" is legally harmless (no RFN), but do not imply Afrotype endorses the game. The product spec already plans a distinct, trademark-checked store title.
- **Hand-painted bus lettering in textures:** draw original lettering inspired by sign-writers' styles rather than tracing a specific bus or sign-writer.
- **Safe font sources:** Google Fonts (OFL or Apache) and other OFL fonts. Avoid fonts from aggregator sites unless the licence text is in the download and permits commercial embedding.

### Gaps
- **Afrotype's commercial terms for its other (non-Google) fonts** could not be read; afrotype.com was blocked. Do not assume other Afrotype families are OFL.

## 11. OpenStreetMap: ODbL for roads and place names, attribution wording in games, and Produced Work vs Derivative Database

### Takeaway
A game world built from OSM is a **Produced Work** and needs a visible notice that data came from OSM under the ODbL. Any **cleaned or extracted OSM road and name dataset** the team keeps (even inside the build pipeline) is a **Derivative Database**. If a Produced Work made from it is used publicly, the team must **offer that derivative database, or a file or method describing the changes, free of charge**, and must not lock it behind technical measures without a parallel unrestricted copy. Suggested wording: **"Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL) — openstreetmap.org/copyright."** Show it on the map/minimap or loading screen and in credits, and put it in the store description.

### Cited Findings
- **[Licence text] ODbL 1.0 definitions (F).**
  - "Derivative Database": a database based upon the Database, including any "translation, adaptation, arrangement, modification, or any other alteration of the Database or of a Substantial part of the Contents," including extraction.
  - "Produced Work": "a work (such as an image, audiovisual material, text, or sounds) resulting from using the whole or a Substantial part of the Contents… from this Database [or] a Derivative Database."

  — [ODbL 1.0 text (SPDX mirror)](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- **[Licence text] ODbL s.4.3 (F).** If you Publicly Use a Produced Work, you must include a notice "reasonably calculated to make any Person that uses, views, accesses, interacts with, or is otherwise exposed to the Produced Work aware that Content was obtained from the Database… and that it is available under this License." Example notice: "Contains information from DATABASE NAME, which is made available here under the Open Database License (ODbL)," with links, or plain-text URIs where links are impossible. — [ODbL 1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- **[Licence text] ODbL ss.4.4–4.5 (F).**
  - Share-alike applies to any Derivative Database you Publicly Use.
  - **4.4(c):** "A Derivative Database is Publicly Used and so must comply with Section 4.4 if a Produced Work created from the Derivative Database is Publicly Used."
  - **4.5(b):** creating a Produced Work "does not create a Derivative Database."
  - 4.5(c): internal-only use is not public.

  — [ODbL 1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- **[Licence text] ODbL s.4.6 (F).** If you Publicly Use a Derivative Database **or a Produced Work from a Derivative Database**, you must offer recipients, in machine-readable form, the entire Derivative Database or a file of all alterations (or the method of making them, such as an algorithm). It must be free of charge if distributed over the internet. — [ODbL 1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- **[Licence text] ODbL s.4.7 (F).** You may not impose technological measures on the Database, a Derivative Database or a Substantial part of the Contents that restrict licence rights. The exception is "parallel distribution": also making an unrestricted copy available. — [ODbL 1.0](https://github.com/spdx/license-list-data/blob/main/text/ODbL-1.0.txt)
- **[Regulator/official] OSM copyright page (F, from the openstreetmap-website source strings).**
  - "Where you use OpenStreetMap data, you are required to do the following two things: Provide credit to OpenStreetMap by displaying our attribution notice. Make clear that the data is available under the Open Database License."
  - Display rules differ for browsable maps, printed maps and static images.
  - You may link to the copyright page; where links are impossible, include the full URL.
  - For screenshots in books, films or TV, "you do not need to ask for permission."

  — [openstreetmap-website en.yml (copyright page text)](https://github.com/openstreetmap/openstreetmap-website/blob/master/config/locales/en.yml)
- **[Regulator/official] OSMF Attribution Guidelines,** adopted 25 June 2021. "© OpenStreetMap contributors" and "© OpenStreetMap" are acceptable historical forms. The guidelines do **not** explicitly address games; proposals to treat games like films (credits allowed) were discussed but not adopted. — [OSMF Attribution Guidelines (S, blocked)](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines); [OSM talk list, Aug 2019 (S)](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/talk/2019-August/083082.html); [OSM legal-talk, Feb 2015 (S)](https://lists.openstreetmap.org/pipermail/legal-talk/2015-February/008102.html)
- **[Regulator/official]** The OSMF Produced Work guideline distinguishes rendered works from derivative databases, including the underlying-data obligation (earlier project research, checked 18 Oct 2026). — [OSMF Produced Work guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Produced_Work_-_Guideline)

### Inferences
- **The Danfo pipeline:**
  1. Geofabrik Nigeria extract.
  2. Filtered, corrected road graph with names, widths and lanes. This is a **Derivative Database**.
  3. Godot meshes, navmesh and stop names. These are **Produced Works**, which are public once shipped.

  Under 4.4(c) and 4.6, shipping step 3 obliges the team to **publish the step-2 dataset or its transformation scripts** under ODbL, free, e.g., a public repo or release asset. The game itself, its art and code stay proprietary (4.5(b)).
- **Place names from OSM** (stop names, neighbourhoods) are Contents. Names are facts, but a substantial extracted set is a database extraction, so treat it like the road graph.
- **Where to show the notice:**
  - **Show it in-game wherever a map or minimap is shown** (small corner text), which matches "browsable map" practice.
  - Also put it in credits and on the licences screen.
  - Add one line to the store description.

  Credits-only placement is common in films, but the guidelines have not formally blessed it for games.
- **DRM:** if the derivative database ships *inside* the store package (e.g., as a road JSON), the store's DRM arguably engages 4.7. The published parallel copy from the inference above covers that.

### Gaps
- Full OSMF attribution-guideline text for "interactive and other media" was not readable (blocked).
- Whether OSMF has ever commented on a specific game; no source found.

## 12. Cultural sensitivity: consultants, sensitivity readers, stereotypes, crime and religion, and what Nigerians have said about past portrayals

### Takeaway
Nigerian reactions follow a pattern. **In-group, specific, affectionate portrayals** go viral and get praise: Danfo Run in September 2026 and Lagos Life in October 2026. **Outsider depictions** are criticised when they reduce Africa to colonial-era helplessness or Nigerians to criminals; District 9 was banned in Nigeria. The documented industry practice is paid, scoped review: a cultural consultant or sensitivity reader, ideally Lagos-based, giving a severity-ranked issue list with documented decisions. For Danfo, **do not build gameplay around "one chance" robberies or police extortion. Show religion plurally and respectfully, and keep sacred text off objects that get wrecked.**

### Cited Findings
- **[Journalism] Danfo Run / "Lagos Run" (late September 2026).**
  - It is a free browser game by Nigerian-born frontend engineer Opeyemi Adeniran ("Eureka" on X), built in about four to five weeks with AI tools.
  - Reported players: 7,000 in two days, then 27,000; Techloy says 140,000+ in the first week. The figures conflict.
  - Updates added Lagos Island with **Third Mainland Bridge, Makoko, Idumota and Marina**.
  - Earlier danfo games include *Gidi Run* and *Danfo Racer*.
  - Nigerians reacted with excitement at "transforming a recognisable part of Lagos life into a playable digital experience."

  — [Techloy (S)](https://www.techloy.com/lagos-life-nigerian-browser-games/); [EdgeX news (S)](https://pro.edgex.exchange/fr-FR/news/article/lagos-run-danfo-browser-game); [L24 News (S)](https://www.l24news.com/2026/09/nigerian-developer-goes-viral-after.html)
- **[Journalism] Lagos Life (launched 1 October 2026).**
  - It is a browser life simulator with jobs, rent and virtual money; 18+ under its terms; currency not withdrawable.
  - Company-reported players: 1M in three days, and up to 10M by 11 October (unaudited).
  - Reported raise: $500k at a $10M valuation.
  - An exploit led to balance resets, and copycats appeared.

Nigerians used #CaptainAmericaInNigeria for satirical commentary on fuel shortages and corruption, rather than outrage. OkayAfrica's CEO said the film "didn't belittle us." — [NPR via WBEZ (S)](https://www.wbez.org/culture/2016/05/05/captain-america-is-trending-in-nigeria-but-you-might-be-surprised-why); [OkayAfrica (S)](https://www.okayafrica.com/africas-first-marvel-superhero-makes-his-cinematic-debut-in-captain-america-civil-war/1253024)
- **[Journalism] Consultancy practice:**
  - **NARRATIFY** (February 2026, Jennifer Lufau and ex-Ubisoft Mickaël Newton) offers cultural-authenticity consulting and connects studios with African developers, artists and cultural experts.
  - **Panic** hired a short-term sensitivity reader to flag harmful language, misrepresentation and places needing grounding.
  - **Nimdzi** delivered a severity-ranked issue report with fixes, used to train the team.
  - **Englobe's** "geocultural" review stresses documenting the reasoning behind decisions.

  — [Games Industry Africa (S)](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2026/02/11/narratify-launches-consulting-and-events-agency-focused-on-african-video-games/); [Work With Indies, Panic sensitivity reader (S)](https://workwithindies.com/careers/panic-sensitivy-reader); [Nimdzi (S)](https://www.nimdzi.com/testimonials/video-game-localization-ux/); [Game Developer, Englobe Q&A (S)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/q-a-englobe-s-edwards-talks-gaming-s-geocultural-risks-)
- **[Regulator/official] Apple (F).** 1.1.1 bars discriminatory or mean-spirited content targeting groups. 1.1.2 bars enemies that solely target a real culture or government. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **[Journalism]** Earlier project research used a first-person Lagos bus account and flagged it: "do not reproduce its stereotypes or political allegations as general fact." — [Legit.ng, Oct 2022 (from earlier project research)](https://www.legit.ng/editorial/feature/1496889-a-day-life-a-lagos-bus-driver-mainland/)

### Inferences
- **What resonates:** specific places, slang, humour, hustle, the conductor's craft, traffic ("go-slow"), and the bus as a personal canvas. **What causes backlash:** Nigeria reduced to crime, scams, poverty or chaos, especially by outsiders. The team's insider status matters: Lagos Life's satire of rent and hardship is read as self-commentary.
- **"One chance" robberies** (fake commuter buses used to rob passengers) are a real fear, but a passenger-carrying game that makes them a mechanic or joke frames danfo operators as potential criminals. **Omit.**
- **Police or LASTMA extortion:** do not reward bribery as a mechanic and do not portray identifiable agencies as corrupt (Apple 1.1.2 and defamation). If road enforcement exists, keep it a neutral, fictional, rule-based obstacle (fines for blocking a junction, for example).
- **Religion:** show the real Christian and Muslim mix in slogans ("God's Time Is The Best," "Alhamdulillah," "Masha Allah," "Blood of Jesus") **evenly, never mocked**. Keep Quranic Arabic and Bible verses off surfaces that are crashed, soiled or stepped on, unless a Muslim or Christian Lagos reviewer approves; Quranic script treatment is a recognised sensitivity. No parody of real denominations.
- **Language:** Pidgin, Yoruba and Lagos slang should be written or checked by native speakers (the repo already requires a Lagos-based reader). Avoid machine-translated Yoruba; tone marks matter.
- **Zero-budget review process:**
  1. Recruit two to four volunteer Lagos-based reviewers (mixed gender, Christian and Muslim, at least one Yoruba speaker, at least one danfo-commuter), credited in-game.
  2. Give them the script, barks, signage and bus-slogan list, and a capture video of districts.
  3. Collect a severity-ranked issue list (blocker / should-fix / nit) and record decisions in the repo, following Englobe's documentation practice.
  4. Re-review before each new district.

### Gaps
- **No sources were found on Nigerian reaction to GTA's Nigerian characters, a Call of Duty "Lagos" level, Hitman or Forza Horizon depictions of Lagos.** I could not confirm such levels or characters exist as described in the brief. The report should not assert them without a fresh source.
- The Alabi essay and TechCabal piece on Lagos Life (which likely discuss its socio-political satire) could not be opened.
- No published Nigerian-specific game-representation standard was found. NARRATIFY's track record is unproven (it launched in 2026).

## 13. Consolidated rulebook: do / don't / fictionalise, with basis and lawyer flags

### Takeaway
Real **geography, language, sounds of the street and the yellow livery** are free to use. **Brands, emblems, real people, real music and OEM vehicle identities** are not, and must be fictionalised. **Licences** (OSM, CC, OFL) are manageable with a register, attribution and a published OSM-derived dataset. Lawyer time is needed for a handful of questions, listed below.

### Cited Findings
(Each rule draws on the findings above. Basis key: AG = Apple Guidelines (F); CA22 = Nigerian Copyright Act 2022 (S); ODbL (F); CC (F); OFL (F); cases as cited in sections 1–8.)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Inferences
**Where a lawyer is really needed:**
1. Clearing the **final store title** and any logo (Nigeria, US, EU, UK). "Danfo" alone is occupied by Danfo Run and the Afrotype font name, and is arguably generic.
2. Whether **s.20(1)(e) "audiovisual work"** covers a video game, if any distinctive designed building, sculpture or bridge will be closely replicated.
3. **Folklore (ss.74–76)** before using any traditional song, chant, oriki, pattern or ceremonial element.
4. **ShareAlike and app-store DRM** (CC BY-SA, ODbL 4.7) if any BY-SA asset or OSM derivative data ships inside the package.
5. Any **real brand, music, sponsor or licence deal**, and the contracts.
6. Responding to any **takedown notice or demand letter**: do not ignore it, and do not argue Rogers informally.
7. The **VW-like silhouette** if marketing deliberately evokes the classic VW bus. The EU 3D mark and active enforcement history make this a real risk.

**Process rules that cost nothing:**
- Keep the source/licence register current for each asset.
- Run an "IP sweep" checklist before each store submission: screenshots, icon, keywords, billboards, plates, uniforms, audio stems.
- Get a Lagos reviewer's sign-off per district.
- Show the OSM notice on map screens.
- Keep a licences screen listing OFL, CC-BY credits and ODbL.

### Gaps
- Several statute points are from summaries, not the official text, because the hosts were blocked: Copyright Act ss.20, 63–76; Police Act s.101; Lagos Transport Sector Reform Law 2018. Verify them against the NCC and PLAC PDFs.
- No store removal over vehicle trademarks was found, so the store-risk assessment rests on policy text, not precedent.
