## Fleet composition by era: which base vehicles are danfos, and what is the mix in 2026?

### Takeaway
Danfos began in the 1970s as converted VW Type 2 Kombis. The VW T3 (Vanagon/T25), with its rear engine and flat front, became the iconic danfo from the 1980s, and sources still describe it as the "typical" or "king" danfo. Japanese cab-over vans (Nissan Urvan, Toyota HiAce, Mazda E-series) are also documented in the working fleet. **No survey giving the share of each model was found**, so the 2026 VW-versus-Japanese split cannot be stated as a number. The fleet is estimated at 55,000–75,000 minibuses, and in 2025 it still carried about half of motorised journeys.

### Inferences
- The fleet appears to have moved from VW T2 (1970s) to VW T3 (1980s–1990s dominance) and then to a mixed fleet in which ageing T3s run alongside second-hand Japanese cab-overs (Urvan E24/E25, HiAce H100/H200, Mazda E-series). This is inferred from the 2022 "VW Vanagon or Nissan Urvan" description, HiAce's sales leadership and the "Hummer" 18-seater listings. Because the T3 ended European production in 1990/92 and every surviving one is now 34 or more years old, the Japanese share has probably grown, but **no count confirms this**.
- The listed 2009–2010 "Hummer" HiAces fall in the H200 production run (2004–2019). That run dating comes from background knowledge and was not retrieved this session. In Lagos usage, "Hummer bus" therefore most likely means a high-roof H200-shaped HiAce 18-seater, a newer and taller silhouette than the classic danfo.
- For a game set "now", a fleet made only of VWs would look dated, and one made only of HiAces would lose the iconic silhouette. A mixed traffic population fits the evidence best.

## Related categories for traffic variety: korope, molue, BRT/regulated buses, CNG/electric buses, keke, okada

### Takeaway
Korope is the *smaller* yellow-and-black minibus. Since February 2026 it has been pushed off the Lekki-Epe expressway onto feeder routes. Molues (yellow Mercedes lorry-chassis buses) have been phasing out since the 2000s and now survive only on a few corridors. Regulated buses are blue and white. They include BRT high-capacity buses, about 150–170 CNG buses and a small electric (Yutong/OCEL) pilot fleet. Keke (TVS King, Bajaj RE) and okada are banned on highways, bridges and many LGAs, but enforcement is uneven.

## Dimensions and specifications of the 2–3 most representative base vehicles

### Takeaway
The best documented and most representative bases are the **VW T3 Transporter** (rear engine, flat front, about 4.57 m long), the **Toyota HiAce H100** (cab-over, 4.6 m short or 5.25 m long wheelbase) and the **Nissan Urvan E24** (cab-over, about 4.86 m, TD27 diesel). All are about 1.69–1.87 m wide and 1.93–1.96 m tall in standard-roof form. Official spec sheets could not be retrieved, and some figures conflict between databases.

### Inferences
- **Proposed original reference buses**, sized to real proportions but with no manufacturer design:
  1. "Classic" danfo: flat-fronted, rear-engined, about 4.57 m long × 1.85 m wide × 1.93 m tall, wheelbase about 2.46 m, kerb about 1.4 t. T3 proportions; the hero and default player bus.
  2. "Japanese cab-over" danfo: about 4.6–4.9 m × 1.69 m × 1.94–1.96 m, wheelbase 2.33–2.65 m, kerb about 1.5–1.6 t, diesel of about 60–95 hp. Urvan E24/HiAce H100 proportions. Narrower and longer, with the engine under the front seats.
  3. "Hummer" high-roof 18-seater: a long, tall HiAce-H200-proportioned van. **Dimensions were not retrieved**; the H100 long-wheelbase figures (5.25 m L, 2.24 m H, 2.89 m WB) are the nearest sourced proxy.
- **Door side:** Nigeria drives on the right with left-hand-drive vehicles. This is background knowledge (Nigeria switched to right-hand traffic in 1972) and was **not retrieved this session**. LHD vans therefore have the sliding door on the right-hand (kerb) side, and passengers board from the kerb on the bus's right. The driver sits front-left.
- Engine layout: T3 rear-engine and rear-wheel drive is sourced (S4). The HiAce H100 and Urvan E24 being front-mid "cab-over" layouts with the engine under or between the front seats and rear-wheel drive comes from **background knowledge, not verified this session**.

## Conversions, seating layouts and official versus real capacity

### Takeaway
The official and nominal danfo capacity is **14**, which Lagos itself used as the baseline when it cut loading to 8 in 2020. The common working figure is **14–18**, with officials speaking of "conventional 18-sitters". Benches are rearranged and added so that **four sit on each row** where the factory fitted three, and **two passengers share the front passenger seat**. The conductor works at and through the sliding door. Engine swaps, roof racks and bull bars are undocumented in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- Danfos typically seat 14–18 passengers on semi-fixed urban routes. Source: [Wikipedia, Danfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danfo).
- Drivers "rearranged and added extra seats to maximize profit", with four passengers on each seat, and squeeze two passengers into the front passenger seat when LASTMA officials are not looking. Source: [The Autopian](https://theautopian.com/why-nigerias-danfo-bus-drivers-might-be-the-craziest-and-most-skilled-drivers-on-the-planet).
- In 2020 (COVID-19), 14-seaters "used to carry four passengers on a row" and were cut to three per row. Source: [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/transport/article/transporters-yet-to-obey-lagos-guidelines-on-covid-19/). ## Condition, wear and regulatory standards (VIS, roadworthiness, CNG)

### Takeaway
Officials and press consistently describe danfos as old, "rickety" and poorly maintained. Lagos's Vehicle Inspection Service (VIS) targets commercial buses on brakes, wipers, lights, shock absorbers and tyres, and impounds those that fail. Compliance appears low: in one FIJ sample, 3 of 20 drivers held roadworthiness certificates. There is no CNG mandate for informal danfos; the 2026 CNG/EV rule covers LAMATA's regulated fleet.

### Cited Findings

## Mechanical driving character (speed, acceleration, roll, brakes, overload)

### Takeaway
Sourced figures show these are slow, heavy, top-heavy vans. A T3 diesel takes about 20–22 s to reach 100 km/h and tops out at about 110–130 km/h. Japanese diesels make about 77–94 hp (HiAce) or 62 kW, about 83 hp (Urvan TD27), for 1.5–2.0 t empty. A full 18-passenger load is close to or above the Urvan's 1,250 kg payload. No sourced data exists on body roll, brake fade or sound.

### Cited Findings
- VW T3 1.6 TD: 0–100 km/h in 21.5 s, top speed 127 km/h. Source: [auto motor und sport, technical data](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/oldtimer/alltagsklassiker-vw-t3-platz-fuer-alle-im-kasten-bulli/technische-daten/).
- VW T3 1.7 D (57 PS): 0–100 km/h in 20.0 s, top speed 130 km/h. Source: [AutoPlenum](https://www.autoplenum.de/auto/vw/t3/bus-1979/1-7-d-57-ps).
- An early 1984 T3 with the 50 hp 1.6 D and a 4-speed: about 110 km/h top speed and about 22 s to 80 km/h (owner anecdote). Source: [camperforum.nl](https://camperforum.nl/viewtopic.php?p=109123) **[weak]**. One catalogue lists 37.1 s to 100 km/h for the 50 hp diesel. Source: [auto.ae](https://auto.ae/catalog/volkswagen/multivan/1984-1992-t3/mini-van/) **[weak: outlier]**.
- Nissan Urvan E24 TD27: 62 kW, 176 Nm, kerb about 1.5–1.6 t, GVM 2,750 kg, payload 1,250 kg. Source: [carsales](https://www.carsales.com.au/research/nissan/urvan/1992/window/169be5af-ac43-4ac6-aa9e-84c3c54d4c0e/).
- HiAce H100 diesels: 77–94 hp. Kerb up to about 2.0 t (LWB). Source: [fastcarcheck](https://fastcarcheck.uk/specs/make/toyota/hiace/292438).

### Inferences
- **Overload (inference, assumes about 65–70 kg per adult):** 18 passengers plus driver and conductor is about 1.3–1.4 t. That exceeds the Urvan's 1,250 kg rated payload before any luggage. A full danfo is therefore plausibly at or above GVM, which justifies slower acceleration, longer braking and more body roll when full.
- **Power-to-weight (inference):** a 50 hp T3 at about 1.4 t empty plus about 1.3 t load gives about 18–19 hp per tonne, so acceleration when full is far slower than the 0–100 km/h figures quoted for an empty van. A game "full bus" penalty is defensible.
- Short wheelbases (2.33–2.65 m) and heights of about 1.93–1.96 m, raised further by roof loads, suggest high roll sensitivity. This is inference; no rollover or roll data was found.

## The 2025–2026 transition: franchise MoU, diesel phase-out, CNG/EV mandates and the October 2026 street picture

### Takeaway
In early August 2026, Lagos, NURTW and RTEAN signed an MoU that starts moving danfo operators into union cooperatives holding LAMATA bus franchises. It begins on selected Quality Bus Corridors: 8 QBCs in the framework, with reports of 4 corridors in the first phase. It sets **no published deadline for removing danfos statewide**. LAMATA's "no new diesel" rule (effective early 2026, publicised in early October 2026) applies to the *regulated* fleet, not to informal danfos. In October 2026 yellow danfos remain the dominant minibus across most of Lagos. Lekki-Epe is the main exception: korope were removed there in February 2026 and blue regulated buses dominate.

### Cited Findings
- **August 2026 MoU (LAMATA statement, reported about 6 August 2026):** signed by Ministry of Transportation Permanent Secretary Olawale Musa and LAMATA MD Abimbola Akinajo for the state; Samson Ajala Adepeju and Bolaji Kawojue for RTEAN; Mustapha Adekunle Sego and Usman O. Teslim for NURTW. It provides "a common framework for transitioning bus operations" across the state's **eight Quality Bus Corridors** and forms the basis for granting bus operating franchises. Unions adopt cooperative structures, LAMATA keeps regulatory oversight, and digital fare collection is planned. It followed more than 30 months of discussions. Sources: [Nairametrics, 6 Aug 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/06/lagos-moves-to-phase-danfo-into-franchise-bus-system/); [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/lagos-signs-mou-with-nurtw-rtean-to-transition-danfo-operators-into-regulated-bus-franchise-system/); [Guardian, "NURTW, RTEAN unite in historic deal"](https://guardian.ng/?p=2892358); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1172837).
- The first phase covers four corridors, with digital fare collection. Source: [AbokiFX news aggregator](https://abokiforex.app/show-news/1723838-lagos-introduces-digital-fare-payment-danfo-buses-4-quality-bus-corridors) **[weak: aggregator]**. Rollout starts "on selected corridors before expanding". Source: [Nairametrics, 21 Aug 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/21/lagos-danfo-reform-faces-a-major-test-experts-spot-the-cracks/).
- A Kenyan site frames the deal as one to "eradicate" danfos. It cites 229 blue buses on Lekki-Epe and unregulated minivans barred from highways from late 2026. **This is not corroborated by the Nigerian reports.** Source: [Streamline Feed](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/lagos-signs-historic-mou-eradicate-danfo-buses-under-franchise-transit-system).

- **Diesel phase-out (reported 2–4 October 2026):** LAMATA no longer accepts new diesel buses into the regulated system; operators must bring CNG or electric buses. The rule took effect at the start of 2026. Existing diesels will be replaced gradually. It is "not an immediate ban on every diesel-powered bus operating on Lagos roads". ### Inferences
- Roughly 1,000–1,200 regulated buses (including about 170 CNG buses) against 55,000–75,000 danfos means yellow danfos almost certainly **remain the dominant bus on Lagos streets in October 2026**. The MoU is a framework signed two months earlier, the diesel rule exempts informal operators, and no statewide removal date exists. A game set "now" can treat yellow danfos as the default traffic. It should show blue-and-white regulated and BRT buses on corridors, Lekki-Epe in particular, and could hint at the transition through details such as QR/PTCS stickers on regulated buses, cooperative or franchise talk and CNG buses.
- Lagos has announced "yellow bus" removal repeatedly (2017, 2020, 2024, 2026). Ending the game's timeline with danfos gone would contradict the evidence. A "transition underway" framing is accurate.
