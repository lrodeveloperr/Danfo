## 1. The conductor's craft: calls, door-riding, signals, fares, change and hand signs

### Takeaway
Sources document the conductor as a skilled, highly physical caller and fare-handler. The core of the craft is rhythmic shouted calls with clipped stop names, riding the door, constant arm movement directing boarders, and juggling change. In this pass I found **no documented Lagos system of passenger or conductor hand signs for destinations**. I also found no written source describing bus-body banging as a driver signal, or notes folded between the fingers. Both are widely assumed, but here they remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **Calls are compressed and rhythmic.** A Lagos language guide says conductors "condense complex directions into rhythmic shouts". Its example is "Obalende! Obalende! Wole pelu change e!", glossed as "Enter with your change!" ("wọlé" is Yoruba for "enter", with English "change"). The guide is undated. — [Discover Lagos, "The language of Lagos"](https://www.discoverlagos.ng/post/the-language-of-lagos-understanding-pidgin-slang-and-the-local-lexicon)
- **Stop names are clipped by documented phonological processes.** Obiorah (2021, *Jurnal Arbitrer*) finds that danfo conductors apply "vowel deletion, nasal deletion, vowel lengthening, consonant deletion, and word deletion" when pronouncing bus-stop names. The conductor's pronunciation "differs from the normal way that the populace knows", so "new travelers often time miss their bus stops". Conductors' language also "differs when they interact with their different clients". The full paper, with its per-stop examples, could not be opened. — [Obiorah 2021, Jurnal Arbitrer](https://arbitrer.fib.unand.ac.id/index.php/arbitrer/article/view/272)
- **Calls are made while chasing and boarding a moving bus.** A first-person essay describes a conductor chanting stops such as "Jibowu" and "Yaba" while running after a moving bus. His "arms become a blur of motion, effortlessly directing the passengers into the bus". Conductors are pictured "with arms flailing like the wind, and voices screaming their various destinations… like maestros conducting a symphony". The same essay describes a conductor going up to a passenger to ask where they are headed. The essay is undated. — [Michael Kolawole, "The Prehensile and Mind-reading Skills of Lagos's Bus Conductors" (Substack)](https://michaelkolawole.substack.com/p/the-prehensile-and-mind-reading-skills)
- **Door-riding.** Search summaries surfaced the phrases "hanging on the door of the Danfo in the manner characteristic of bus conductors" and conductors "jumping and sticking to the ubiquitous rickety kombi popularly called danfo". The summary did not say which of these results each phrase came from: [A Day in the Life Gist Corner, "Me and My Driver-Conductor Duo"](https://adayinthelifegistcorner.substack.com/p/me-and-my-driver-conductor-duo), [Brittle Paper, "Rumuokoro"](https://brittlepaper.com/2016/09/rumuokoro-sotonye-dan-african-story/), [Guardian Nigeria, "Danfo… The Naked Realities"](https://guardian.ng/life/danfo-the-naked-realities-of-nigerian-automobiles/), [WATHI, "Danfo tales in the big city"](https://wathi.org/initiative-lagos/contributions-initiatives-lagos/danfo-tales-in-the-big-city). The Brittle Paper item is fiction set partly outside Lagos, so treat the phrasing as literary.
- **Change pairing.** A search summary reports a practice it calls "junction-marriage", in which the conductor pairs passengers so they settle balances between themselves after alighting. The source was not isolated (same result set as above). **Flag: unverified term. A Lagos reader must confirm it before use.**
- **Fare collection at parks is slowed by the hunt for small notes.** A Lagos Ikeja report describes drivers holding passengers at the park while they look for lower denominations as fares are gathered. It also says a "loading fare", "an euphemism for a park tax", is incurred at every park. — [Ikeja Record](https://ikejarecord.com/?p=3102)
- **Conductor pay is informal, daily and unstable (2022).** A Nairametrics profile describes a conductor handed ₦3,000 at the end of one workday, with the driver expecting a share of a side fee. It says owners and drivers hire and fire conductors weekly "because of issues like pilfering and outright stealing". — [Nairametrics, "A day in the life of a Lagos bus conductor", 25 June 2022](https://nairametrics.com/2022/06/25/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-lagos-bus-conductor/)
- **Conductor language is multilingual and figurative.** Òjó & Ntekim-Rex (2024, UNILAG) used participant observation and interviews on Lagos Island and the Mainland. They find that "Yorùbá language, Pidgin English, English language, tag-switching and colloquialism" make up conductor speech, with Yorùbá dominant because most conductors are native speakers. Conductors also use personification and metonymy. — [UNILAG repository](https://ir.unilag.edu.ng/items/61c7d3ce-f8d2-4763-876e-7dbdf1a57f68)
- **Hand signs: nothing documented found.** Three searches returned no source describing Lagos destination hand signs by passengers or conductors. The search tool itself concluded: "I can't confirm from these results whether finger signals are a standard practice". — [search result set incl. Kolawole](https://michaelkolawole.substack.com/p/the-prehensile-and-mind-reading-skills), [Obiorah 2021](https://arbitrer.fib.unand.ac.id/index.php/arbitrer/article/view/272)
- A photo-essay titled "#LagosLivesOn: a portrait of a bus conductor in action" exists, from Samuel Okocha's series, but could not be opened. — [Visura / Samuel Okocha](https://visura.co/okocha/news/lagosliveson-a-portrait-of-a-bus-conductor-in-action)

### Inferences
- **Writing calls:** use the sourced pattern of repeated destination, sometimes a second stop, then a fare or change instruction ("Obalende! Obalende! Wọlé pẹ̀lú change!"). Clipped pronunciations are a documented feature, not an error. Writers should get per-stop clipped forms from a Lagos speaker, because I could not read Obiorah's examples.
- **Conductor animation:** these elements are supported: running beside a slowing bus, riding the open door, wide continuous arm sweeps that funnel boarders in, and turning to individual waiting people to ask their destination. Folded-notes-between-fingers and bus-body slapping are plausible but unsourced here. Present them as artistic choices, and validate them with the fieldwork already proposed in the design doc.
- The game's automatic fare handling can show a short change-pairing moment ("you two share change") as flavour. Do this only once a Lagos reader confirms the practice and its name.
- Because hand signs are undocumented, do not invent a "Lagos hand-sign language" UI. An optional, generic "wave to hail" gesture is safer.

### Gaps
- No source found for: knocking or banging on the body as a stop/go signal; conductor verbal signals to the driver (for example a "go" word or a stop word); how notes are held; "no change" dispute scripts; or a documented hand-sign set. The documented minibus hand-sign system I know of from background knowledge is South African, not verified in this pass. Do not transplant it to Lagos.
- Obiorah's per-stop pronunciation table, Adedun's 221 recorded interactions and the UNILAG thesis text were all unreachable. These are the best primary sources for exact calls and should be read when access allows.

## 2. Passenger behaviour: seating, alighting calls, paying, reactions, preachers and hawkers, disputes

### Takeaway
Informal guides document saying "owa" (from "O wa o") to alight, the need for exact change, crowded benches and fare and change friction. The front-row versus middle-bench question appears in guides, but no source confirms a general "front-seat preference". A major 2026 development is the federal **FRSC (Amendment) Bill 2026**. The Senate passed it, and it would fine **preaching, hawking and trading inside commercial vehicles** (₦50,000, six months' imprisonment, or both). As of late July–August 2026 it was awaiting presidential assent amid strong religious opposition.

### Cited Findings
- **Alighting call:** "learn to say 'owa' when you arrive at your stop so the driver doesn't miss it". Conductors and drivers "are not good listeners", especially with first-timers, who are advised to remind the conductor of their destination repeatedly. These are informal guides; the summary did not isolate which one. — [Zikoko, "These 7 Tips Will Help You Survive Lagos Danfo Buses"](https://www.zikoko.com/life/these-7-tips-will-help-you-survive-lagos-danfo-buses/); [Petfem, "Life Lessons from Lagos Danfo Buses (Part Three): The Passengers"](https://medium.com/@petfemspeaks/life-lessons-from-lagos-danfo-buses-part-three-the-passengers-f46a75eff985)
- **Seating:** one guide says "Avoid the front row… It's always tight, hot, and you're completely shut out from everything that's happening inside the bus. The middle bench is the sweet spot." It is not clear whether "front row" means the seat beside the driver or the first bench behind him. — [Zikoko tips (attribution per search summary)](https://www.zikoko.com/life/these-7-tips-will-help-you-survive-lagos-danfo-buses/)
- **Capacity reference:** in a COVID-19 order of 26 March 2020, Lagos cut danfo loading to **8 passengers from the usual 14**. — [BusinessDay via PressReader, 26 March 2020](https://www.pressreader.com/nigeria/business-day-nigeria/20200326/281633897329458). Ambode-era reporting gives the typical danfo as **14–18 seats**. — [Nairametrics, "Behold Ambode's new danfo bus"](https://nairametrics.com/behold-ambodes-new-danfo-bus)
- **Exact money and change disputes:** passengers are told to have exact fare ready; people boarding with big notes cause change arguments. One blog asks, "Are you that passenger that is always with N1000 or N500 note for just a N50 bus fare?" The ₦50 fare dates this to around 2018 or earlier. — [Erico Trips, "5 things you should know about danfo bus", 27 Sept 2018](https://ericotrips.wordpress.com/2018/09/27/5-things-you-should-know-about-danfo-buses/) (attribution per search summary; the result set also included [Sassy Funke](https://sassyfunke.com/the-travails-of-the-danfo-bus-passenger/))
- **Personal-space friction:** sleeping on neighbours' shoulders is a common complaint ("Don't always hit your head on the person in front or beside you"). — same informal guide result set ([Zikoko tips](https://www.zikoko.com/life/these-7-tips-will-help-you-survive-lagos-danfo-buses/))
- **Conductor misconduct described, with a counterweight.** One source says some conductors "hit their passengers, refuse to give them their change (balance), or, in some extreme cases, fight naked". The same source adds that "some calm, gentle, and respectful Danfo drivers and conductors still exist". — result set incl. [Sassy Funke](https://sassyfunke.com/the-travails-of-the-danfo-bus-passenger/) and [Petfem (Medium)](https://medium.com/@petfemspeaks/life-lessons-from-lagos-danfo-buses-part-three-the-passengers-f46a75eff985) (attribution not isolated)
- **Fare hikes cause disputes:** danfo fares "often rise without prior notice, leaving many passengers stranded, with increases mostly in the evening". — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1091857) (date unclear)
- **Preachers:** Zikoko describes in-bus preachers who can be hard to notice "until someone calls for a shout of praise". — [Zikoko, "5 Types Of People You'll Encounter In A Danfo"](https://www.zikoko.com/life/general/5-types-of-people-youll-encounter-in-a-danfo-public-transportation-lagos/)
- **Hawkers and in-bus sellers:** one column notes almost anything can be bought in a bus, "from bleach to alligator pepper". — result set incl. [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/?p=648555) and [Opinion Nigeria](https://www.opinionnigeria.com/open-letter-to-mr-president-his-excellency-bola-ahmed-tinubu-by-oshiahanwa-moses/) (attribution not isolated)
- **2026 legal flashpoint:**
  - **The bill:** the Senate passed the FRSC (Amendment) Bill 2026. It fines anyone convicted of hawking, trading or preaching inside commercial vehicles ₦50,000, imposes six months' imprisonment, or both. Fines in the bill range from ₦50,000 to ₦100,000, with an updated schedule of 52 traffic offences. It was transmitted for assent. — [Ground News aggregation](https://ground.news/article/frsc-act-senate-raises-traffic-fines-by-300); [ThisDay, 19 July 2026](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/19/christian-group-urges-tinubu-to-reject-bill-criminalising-preaching-in-commercial-vehicles/)
  - **Opposition:** the Organisation of African Instituted Churches, the PFN, CAN South-West and the Northern States Christian Elders Forum urged rejection; NOSCEF called it unconstitutional. On 3 August 2026 the PFN said Christians would keep preaching. — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2026/07/19/christian-group-urges-tinubu-to-reject-bill-criminalising-preaching-in-commercial-vehicles/); [Sahara Reporters, 3 Aug 2026](https://saharareporters.com/2026/08/03/nigerian-pentecostal-fellowship-pfn-vows-christians-will-continue-preaching-commercial); [Leadership (CAN)](https://leadership.ng/can-rejects-%e2%82%a650000-fine-for-preaching-in-commercial-buses-urges-tinubu-to-reject-bill/); [AllAfrica, 20 July 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607200612.html)
  - **Other views:** Muslim Rights Concern urged signing. A zonal RCCG pastor backed regulating inconvenient preaching but opposed the fine. Commuter opinion was mixed. — [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/?p=648555); [Sahara Reporters](https://saharareporters.com/2026/08/03/nigerian-pentecostal-fellowship-pfn-vows-christians-will-continue-preaching-commercial)
  - **Status:** no report of assent was found as of the latest indexed coverage (late July–early August 2026).
- **Passenger music moment:** an anecdote has a conductor spotting a passenger's guitar and asking him to play for the bus. — [BellaNaija](https://www.bellanaija.com/?p=1253916) (attribution per search summary)

### Inferences
- **Alighting:** the "owa / O wa o" call, plus repeated reminders to the conductor, is the best-supported passenger-to-crew verbal cue. It fits the game's "player chooses stops" loop: a passenger calls, then the conductor relays to the driver (the player).
- **Seat assignment:** a 14-passenger interior is the defensible baseline (14 per the 2020 order; 14–18 in older reporting). "Shift!" squeezing onto benches is plausible but unsourced here (see Gaps).
- **Preachers and hawkers are now politically charged** (religious liberty versus road safety, with interfaith disagreement). If included, keep them as neutral ambient life. Never make them the butt of a joke, and do not take a side on the bill. Muting or skipping should be possible, as with any in-bus voice.
- Show passengers reacting to fare hikes and change disputes as ordinary friction resolved by the conductor, consistent with the design doc's automatic fare handling.

### Gaps
- **No source found for:**
  - the specific "shift!" squeeze norm or four-to-a-row seating;
  - a documented front-seat preference (the only sourced guide says to avoid the front row);
  - "drop for bus stop" phrasing;
  - whether fares are paid before or after boarding. Background knowledge says park loading often collects before departure and roadside boarders pay en route; this is unverified.
- **No source found on how passengers react to reckless driving.** Reactions such as shouted protests to the driver are commonly described anecdotally, but none were sourced.

## 3. Parks and stops: motor parks, loading order, informal stops, union dues and 2026 governance

### Takeaway
Danfos load at both union-run parks and garages and countless informal roadside points. The state has repeatedly tried, with limited success, to push operators into designated parks. Dues are layered: a state consolidated levy (₦800 per day announced in 2022), union or agbero "tickets", and "loading" fees at parks. In **2026 the governance picture changed**. NURTW's Lagos chapter is operating under chairman Mustapha Sego (February 2026). In **August 2026, Lagos, NURTW and RTEAN signed an MoU for a Bus Industry Transition Programme (BITP)**. It moves danfo operators into franchises on eight Quality Bus Corridors through union cooperatives, with **fully digital fare collection**, rolled out gradually with no fixed dates.

### Cited Findings
- **Oshodi scale:** a source in the result set describes Oshodi as "West Africa's busiest transport interchange, with over 13 parks and 5,600 buses loading per day". The search summary did not isolate the source. Candidates are the MIT Atlas page or the 2019 ThisDay piece. — [MIT Atlas of Popular Transport: Lagos](https://atlasofpopulartransport.mit.edu/lagos); [ThisDay, 24 April 2019](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2019/04/24/buhari-to-inaugurate-70m-oshodi-transport-interchange-today). **Flag: verify the figure and its date.**
- **Oshodi Transport Interchange:** a $70m interchange was due for inauguration on 24 April 2019. The plan was to consolidate parks into multi-storey terminals with loading bays. — [ThisDay, 24 April 2019](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2019/04/24/buhari-to-inaugurate-70m-oshodi-transport-interchange-today)
- **Ojota:** described as a crowded bus-stop with a pedestrian bridge and "a number of inter-state bus-parks". — result set ([MIT Atlas](https://atlasofpopulartransport.mit.edu/lagos), [WATHI](https://wathi.org/initiative-lagos/contributions-initiatives-lagos/danfo-tales-in-the-big-city); attribution not isolated)
- **Informal stops versus designated parks.** In 2012 LASTMA ordered Ikorodu operators who were loading and dropping at the roundabout to use the designated Sabo motor park. Officials said operators had "turned every nook and cranny of Ikorodu into parks". — [Channels TV, 15 June 2012](https://www.channelstv.com/2012/06/15/fashola-issues-ultimatum-to-danfo-drivers-in-ikorodu)
- **Enforcement at hubs (September 2024):** a Lagos taskforce impounded at least 56 commercial vehicles at Obalende and Oworonshoki to remove "bottlenecks caused by recalcitrant commercial bus operators". A bus driver attacked officers. — [Vanguard, Sept 2024](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2024/09/traffic-infraction-danfo-drivers-attack-lagos-taskforce-during-enforcement/)
- **Route and illegal-parking warnings:** the state warned commercial bus drivers against route violations and illegal parking. A statement attributed to Sola Giwa warned korope operators against unauthorised route deviations, under "the full weight of legal sanctions" (page dated 1 April 2025; date display varies). — [Peoples Gazette](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-govt-warns-commercial-bus-drivers-against-route-violations-illegal-parking/)
- **State levy (2022):** Lagos set a consolidated daily levy of **₦800**, replacing about ₦3,000, from 1 February 2022. It covered the local-government levy and motor-park waste clearing. — [Ripples Nigeria](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-pegs-danfo-levies-at-n800-daily-nurtw-reacts/); [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=651700). Collection did not begin on the announced date. This levy is separate from the dues NURTW and RTEAN "forcefully collect daily". — [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/lagos-n800-transport-levy-scheme-fails-to-kick-off-on-february-1/)
- **Union position:** MC Oluomo said the ₦800 levy "will not stop the union from collecting its 'normal statutory levy'". — [ICIR (part 1)](https://www.icirnigeria.org/nurtw-mc-oluomo-dares-lagos-govt-national-body-as-icir-report-stirs-reawakening-part-1/)

Dues remained hefty despite a government takeover of Idumota parks. — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/despite-government-takeover-of-idumota-parks-transporters-still-yoke-under-hefty-dues/)

Whether this was appealed or later superseded was not found.
- **February 2026:** NURTW Lagos chairman **Mustapha Sego** attended the inauguration, on 17 February 2026, of a union-built "President Bola Ahmed Tinubu Modern Garage" at Oyingbo. — [Nigeria Housing Market](https://www.nigeriahousingmarket.com/news/lagos-transport-union-unveils-president-bola-ahmed-tinubu-modern-garage-facility)
- **August 2026 BITP MoU:**
  - Lagos, NURTW and RTEAN signed an MoU bringing danfo operators into the **Bus Industry Transition Programme**. It covers the state's **eight Quality Bus Corridors** and grants **bus operating franchises**. — [Nairametrics, 6 Aug 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/06/lagos-moves-to-phase-danfo-into-franchise-bus-system/); [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/lagos-signs-mou-with-nurtw-rtean-to-transition-danfo-operators-into-regulated-bus-franchise-system/)
  - Unions form cooperatives to become franchise operators, fare collection "will adopt a fully digital payment system", LAMATA keeps regulatory oversight, and implementation is "gradual". — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/lagos-begins-bus-industry-transition-programme-to-modernize-public-transport/)
  - Experts flagged implementation "cracks". — [Nairametrics, 21 Aug 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/08/21/lagos-danfo-reform-faces-a-major-test-experts-spot-the-cracks/)
  - An earlier cashless-danfo startup, GONA, paid unions in cash weekly and still struggled for union buy-in. — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/?p=358925)
- **Earlier phase-out talk:** past phase-out plans ("Lagos set to replace yellow buses with blue ones") did not displace danfos. A WSCIJ/FIJ investigation found danfos, minibuses and tricycles still dominating. — [Ripples Nigeria](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-set-to-replace-yellow-buses-with-blue-ones/); [WSCIJ, "Forever With Danfo"](https://collaborativemedia.wscij.org/?p=4892)

### Inferences
- **Park loading:** for Work mode, a park's "load then go" (wait to fill, then depart) contrasts with picking up along the corridor. This is supported indirectly by the "loading fare" and park-tax reporting and the design doc's FIJ material. The specific "first in, first out" queue is not sourced here (see Gaps).
- **Fees:** give a park or garage stop a fictional "loading ticket" deducted in the settlement. Label it as a park fee, distinct from fictional roadside "tickets". Never present all fees as legitimate tax or all collectors as criminals; that is consistent with the design doc's guidance.
- **2026 world-building:** the BITP and digital-fare move is a timely, neutral backdrop. A fictional "transition" poster at a park, or a conductor's grumble about cards versus cash, would feel current. It should not imply the real programme's outcome, which is unknown.
- Avoid naming real union leaders (MC Oluomo, Sego) or real union names on in-game signage or characters. Use fictional associations. The reporting involves allegations about real people.

### Gaps
- No source found describing the actual queue discipline at Oshodi, Obalende, CMS, Ojota, Iyana-Ipaja or Ikeja Along: who decides the next bus, how long a bus waits, and whether there is a park "loader" or caller distinct from the conductor.
- No 2025–2026 figure for the agbero, union or "owo ticket" amount per day. The latest found is the 2022 "at least ₦3,000" from interviews. The phrase "owo ticket" itself did not surface.
- Current status of the ₦800 levy, and whether the Parks Management Committee still exists after the 2023 ruling, were not found.
- A "Lagos begins impoundment of unpainted commercial vehicles" headline appeared ([Peoples Gazette](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-begins-impoundment-of-unpainted-commercial-vehicles/)), but neither its date nor its content was confirmed.

## 4. Fares and money: 2025–2026 fares, surge conditions and the driver's daily costs

### Takeaway
Official NBS averages put the **Lagos intra-city bus fare at ₦1,405.85 per trip (October 2025)** and **₦1,537.09 per drop (February 2026)**, the highest in Nigeria. Route-level reporting shows ₦500–₦1,000 short and medium hops, roughly doubling at Christmas, during fuel scarcity and in rain (rain adds about ₦1,500 for one Alimosho commuter in July 2026). Petrol was **about ₦1,280–₦1,380 per litre in Lagos in September 2026**. Owner "returns" of **₦3,000–₦5,000 per day** are the most recent figures found, but they are undated and likely 2023–2025, so they are probably low for late 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **NBS averages:**
  - Lagos average intra-city bus fare **₦1,405.85 in October 2025**, the highest of any state, up 16.57% year on year. — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1068093)
  - **February 2026:** Lagos **₦1,537.09 per drop**, the highest; Taraba ₦1,405.36; Abia lowest at ₦699.17. The national average was ₦1,195.75, against ₦943.68 in February 2025. ## 5. Driving behaviour and enforcement: documented patterns, LASTMA and police

### Takeaway
Documented patterns include unsignalled lane changes and aggressive merges, loading and stopping at roundabouts and informal points, BRT-lane use and one-way (contraflow) driving. Enforcement is real and technology-assisted: LASTMA detected about **38,000 violations from April to June 2026**, and one-way driving carries **vehicle forfeiture, with no fine option**, under the 2018 Transport Sector Reform Law. Extortion allegations against officials are reported but contested. Portray the dynamic, not a stereotype of either drivers or officers.

## 7. Language: conductor and passenger phrases in Yoruba, Pidgin and Nigerian English

### Takeaway
The academic base is solid but was only reachable as abstracts. It establishes Yoruba-dominant code-switching with Pidgin and English, figurative devices, and clipped stop names. Only two phrases were confirmed from documentary sources this pass: "Wọlé pẹ̀lú change" ("enter with your change") and "owa" for alighting. Other commonly heard phrases are listed in Gaps as **unverified candidates** for native-speaker review.

### Cited Findings
- **Adedun (2005), "Carry go": A Sociolinguistic Study of the Language of Bus Conductors in Lagos.** Based on 221 recorded interactions plus field notes, it finds conductor language "creative, attitudinal, emotional, group-directed, solidarity [conscious]" (the repository text is partly garbled). The title phrase "carry go" is itself Pidgin, roughly "carry on, go ahead". — [UNILAG repository](https://ir.unilag.edu.ng/handle/123456789/3065)
- **Òjó & Ntekim-Rex (2024):** Yorùbá-dominant mixing with Pidgin and English, tag-switching and colloquialism; uses personification and metonymy; conductors are "one of the major characters that define the lifestyle and multilingual dynamics of Lagos". — [UNILAG repository](https://ir.unilag.edu.ng/items/61c7d3ce-f8d2-4763-876e-7dbdf1a57f68)
- **Obiorah (2021):** clipped stop names produced by deletion and lengthening processes; speech differs by client. Akanmu (2017), on idioms among Lagos conductors, is cited in this paper but was not found. — [Jurnal Arbitrer](https://arbitrer.fib.unand.ac.id/index.php/arbitrer/article/view/272)
- **Environment shapes the speech:** a study covering Agege, Ojota and Oshodi reports conductors' language is "significantly affected by their environment of speech community". The author is not confirmed. — result set incl. [Epoka University repository](https://dspace.epoka.edu.al/handle/1/2192)
- **"Obalende! Obalende! Wole pelu change e!" = "Enter with your change!"** — [Discover Lagos](https://www.discoverlagos.ng/post/the-language-of-lagos-understanding-pidgin-slang-and-the-local-lexicon)
- **"Owa" said by passengers on reaching their stop.** — [Zikoko tips](https://www.zikoko.com/life/these-7-tips-will-help-you-survive-lagos-danfo-buses/) (attribution per search summary)
- **"Balance" is used for change** (in "refuse to give them their change (balance)"). — result set incl. [Sassy Funke](https://sassyfunke.com/the-travails-of-the-danfo-bus-passenger/)
- **"Go-slow"** is the Lagos term for traffic jams. — [The Common](https://thecommononline.org/?p=4912)
- **"Agbero" = ticket tout.** ICIR glosses agberos as "ticket touts". — [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/money-for-the-boys-how-agberos-pocket-billions-of-lagos-transport-revenue/)
- **"Delivery" and "returns"** are the drivers' words for the owner's daily payment ("delivery for new motor is N5,000"). — result set incl. [TheCable](https://thecable.ng/bus-drivers-why-fares-remain-high-despite-decline-in-petrol-price)
- **"Loading fare"** is a euphemism for a park tax. — [Ikeja Record](https://ikejarecord.com/?p=3102)
- **"One chance"** is the name for the robbery-by-fake-bus crime (section 8). — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=801084)

### Inferences
- **Writer guidance:** build crew dialogue with Yoruba as the base layer, then Pidgin and English. Use tag-switches (a Yoruba tag on an English or Pidgin line) and playful personification, for example a conductor talking to or about the bus as a person. All of this is sourced in the studies.
- **Glossary** for a game help screen, all sourced: danfo, agbero, go-slow, delivery/returns, balance (change), loading (park fee), owa.
- **Sensitivity:**
  - "Agbero" carries a negative "street tout" connotation, and the press sometimes pairs it with "street urchins" ([Guardian Nigeria headline](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/lagos-drivers-want-over-pampered-street-urchins-off-roads-before-implementing-n800-levy/)). Avoid applying it as a slur to every park worker.
  - Ethnic jokes and insults that crew trade in real life should not be reproduced. The design doc already bars humour based on ethnicity, faith or poverty.
  - The academic note that conductors have an "often aggressive public manner" (Òjó & Ntekim-Rex) should be balanced with competence and warmth.

## 8. Crime and danger: "one chance", crashes and safety (designer awareness, not gameplay)

### Takeaway
"One chance" is a recurring Lagos crime: commuters are lured into a vehicle posing as a commercial bus, then robbed and sometimes pushed out. Hotspots named by police include Oworonshoki, Alapere, Ogudu and Ketu (2024), and there were arrests in Agege (2025) and on Okota's Ago Palace Way. Fatal crashes involving commercial buses recur, often with trucks on expressways, and Nigeria-wide road deaths rose in 2025. These realities justify the design doc's avoidance of casualties and should keep crime out of gameplay.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Keep "one chance" out of gameplay.** A mechanic where the player's bus lures passengers, or where passengers fear the player, would trivialise a real crime and stigmatise honest operators. If acknowledged at all, a passing NPC line ("I no dey enter any bus wey get only one space") is the ceiling, and it needs local review.
- **Crashes:** keep contact racing vehicle-on-vehicle with no pedestrian or passenger casualties. Real fatal crashes often involve trucks on expressways (Lekki-Epe, Badagry), so a truck-heavy Apapa-style race stage should not stage crashes that resemble specific real tragedies.
- The 98-of-111 commercial-vehicle share in Lagos Q1 2024 is a reminder not to glamorise reckless danfo driving as consequence-free. Race mode can frame danger as sporting risk (damage, time loss) rather than harm to people.
