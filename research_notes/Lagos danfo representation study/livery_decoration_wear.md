## 1. The yellow livery: origin, rule text, consistency, and 2026 changes

### Takeaway
The current written requirement I could trace is enforcement of the **Lagos State Transport Sector Reform Law 2018**. In November 2022, LASTMA (the Lagos State Traffic Management Authority) was told to impound commercial buses not painted in the "approved Lagos State colour of red or yellow with black stripes". Minibuses (danfo, korope) are yellow with black stripes; two-door mass-transit buses are red. I found **no source** for who first mandated yellow or when, so the "1990s military administration" origin is unverified. No source gives stripe widths, positions or a paint code. In 2026 the yellow danfo is still the default, but it is being pushed off the Lekki–Epe corridor by a regulated franchise bus scheme (reported as blue).

### Cited Findings
**Rule and enforcement**
- Nov 2022: LASTMA's general manager said officers were directed to impound "any commercial bus not painted in the approved Lagos State colour of red or yellow with black stripes". Reported as enforcing the Lagos State Transport Sector Reform Law, 2018. Impounded buses also need commercial number plates and hackney permits before release. [SS] — [Ripples Nigeria](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-to-impound-unpainted-commercial-vehicles/?amp); [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-begins-impoundment-of-unpainted-commercial-vehicles/); [Nairametrics, 9 Nov 2022](https://nairametrics.com/2022/11/09/lagos-to-commence-immediate-clampdown-on-unregistered-vehicles/)
- Split by vehicle class: "all minibuses known as 'Danfo', including 'Korope', should be painted in the right colours while mass transit buses with two doors were to be painted with approved red colour by operators". [SS] — [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/?p=139102)
- A 2024 fact-check describes danfo and korope as "usually painted yellow, with black stripes on the sides". [SS] — [Africa Check](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/commercial-bus-ban-lagos-nigeria-affects-lekki-epe-axis-and)

**Origin and description**
- Wikipedia's Danfo article (via search): "The typical danfo is a VW transporter bus, painted in cadmium yellow with two black stripes". Danfo emerged in the 1970s after state-operated transport declined. The first were VW Kombis carrying twelve passengers; later VW Transporter, Toyota Hiace, Nissan and Mitsubishi L300 appeared. Typical capacity is 14–18 seats. [SS] — [Wikipedia: Danfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danfo)
- A Wiktionary usage citation for "danfo": "almost every danfo was painted yellow and on the side of the driver's door, in black, was 'driver and 2 passengers'". [SS] — [Wiktionary: danfo](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/danfo)
- A search aimed at the military-governor/Buba Marwa origin found **no** source linking any administrator to the yellow scheme. [SS] — searches returned [Red Bull, "What is a danfo"](https://www.redbull.com/ng-en/events/red-bull-danfo-rally/red-bull-danfo-rally-what-is-a-danfo) and the sources above, none with an origin claim.

### Inferences
- **Regulated versus vernacular:** the yellow and black-stripe scheme is a state *colour requirement*, not an emblem or logo. A generic yellow body with black bands is a colour convention, not protected insignia, so the game can use it freely. LAMATA, BRI and franchise-operator logos on the new buses are protected marks and must not be copied (LAMATA has a logo; see the [LAMATA site](https://www.lamata-ng.com/?p=11117) [SS]).
- **Stripe specification is open to art direction.** No source found specifies stripe count, width or height. The descriptive sources say "two black stripes" on the sides. Build a small set of stripe variants (two waist-level bands of differing width; one thick band; bands broken by panel repairs) rather than one canonical spec.
- **Shade:** "cadmium yellow" is a descriptive phrase, not a regulated paint code. A palette from warm deep cadmium/egg-yolk to sun-faded pale lemon, with repainted panels in slightly mismatched yellows, would fit the documented repainting and enforcement cycles. This is an inference; no colorimetric source was found.
- **Optional era or corridor variants:** a red two-door "mass-transit" bus, a non-yellow Island-route bus (historical, weakly sourced) and a generic blue franchise bus as background traffic. Keep the player's danfo yellow by default.

### Gaps
- Who first made yellow mandatory, and when, is unanswered. No reachable or indexed source attributed it to a 1990s military administrator; Wikipedia, LASG and gazette texts could not be opened.
- The legal wording of the colour clause (section number, whether stripes are specified, Pantone or paint code) is unknown. The statute text was not accessible.
- Chequer stripes and era-by-era colour variations: no source found.
- The colour of the 229 Lekki–Epe franchise buses rests on one aggregator.

## 2. Inscriptions and slogans: examples, placement, language, and lettering trade

### Takeaway
Danfo slogans are a recognised folk art. Owners choose religious lines, proverbs and Yoruba/Pidgin/English sayings that "tell the character and persona of the owner". The lettering is mainly **hand-cut vinyl** made by bus-stop lettering artists, with some hand painting. Verified examples are fewer than expected: "No King as God", "No condition is permanent", "Ola enu kan, omo Iya", "Suru lere", "In Jesus Christ, fire!", "Blood of Jesus", and "Time Na Money" in a painting. "Mummy's Blessing", "God Dey" and "Lagos no dey carry last" were **not** found in any source.

### Cited Findings
**Documented examples**
- BellaNaija (Bird feature, Jan 2022) lists "No King as God" and "No condition is permanent" as popular lines on "yellow and black-striped bodies of refurbished, second-hand buses". It explains that inscriptions are "carefully chosen; they tell the character and persona of the owner":
  - a swaggering owner might choose "Ola enu kan, omo Iya" (glossed by the article as "One love, my kin");
  - a level-headed one "Suru lere" (glossed as "patience is a virtue").
  - [SS] — [BellaNaija/Bird](https://www.bellanaija.com/2022/01/bird-lagos-danfo-driver/). One of three search summaries did not surface the two English lines, but two did, attributing them to this article.
- The same feature quotes a Mushin driver-owner: "I have 'In Jesus Christ, fire!' and 'Blood of Jesus' written on my bus because I am a Christian and whenever I go out, I want God's protection on me." [SS] — [BellaNaija/Bird](https://www.bellanaija.com/2022/01/bird-lagos-danfo-driver/)
- A painting by Afebuame shows a danfo inscribed "Time Na Money". It is an artwork, not a photographed bus. [SS] — [Art635 Gallery](https://art635.gallery/?p=1470)
- A newsletter summarising Agbiboa describes danfo slogans as the "public transcripts" of Lagos's urban poor that "shape the mood and choice of commuters". It mentions a slogan thanking a relative for the vehicle's down payment and one urging gratitude; exact wording was not shown. [SS] — [Makeshift Mobility newsletter](https://makeshiftmobility.substack.com/p/40-as-i-was-saying)
- Nationally (not Lagos-specific), "Jesus is Lord" signals a devout Christian driver. [SS] — [Rilwan, "Counsellors, Men of God & Other Nigerian Road Users"](https://rilwan.substack.com/p/counsellors-men-of-god-and-other)
- Wiktionary's citation records "driver and 2 passengers" painted in black beside the driver's door. That is a capacity or operating marking rather than a slogan. [SS] — [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/danfo)

**Scholarship and language**
- Daniel E. Agbiboa, "Mobile bodies of meaning: city life and the horizons of possibility", *Journal of Modern African Studies* 55(3), 2017, pp. 371–393. Slogans painted by danfo workers "not only reflect the lived realities of danfo workers" but are "vital means through which these workers get by, define their identity and expand their horizons of possibility". Paywalled; examples not accessed. [SS] — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-modern-african-studies/article/mobile-bodies-of-meaning-city-life-and-the-horizons-of-possibility/EE4EE15CBAD39D1249A74C80FBAE7C12)
- Language mix: conductor speech combines Yorùbá, Pidgin, English, tag-switching and colloquialism. An artist's residency profile says Lagos bus texts and "a mix of slang words as Pidgin, Yorùbá, and English expressions" inform his work. [SS] — [UNILAG repository](https://ir.unilag.edu.ng/handle/123456789/13422); [Embassy of Foreign Artists](https://www.eofa.ch/en/resident/6687/)

**Lettering technique and trade**
- Primary: the Google Fonts article says Danfo results from "a collaboration between Afrotype and Abu, a lettering artist from Ojota Bus Stop" and is "inspired by the cut-out shapes of vinyl sticker lettering on public transportation buses in Lagos". [P] — [google/fonts ofl/danfo ARTICLE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/danfo/article/ARTICLE.en_us.html)
- Primary: the repository README says the three styles "reflect different cut-out shapes inspired by the vinyl cut-out lettering of the public transportation buses in Lagos". [P] — [Afrotype/Danfo README](https://github.com/Afrotype/Danfo)
- Typeroom (2018) describes the buses' "hand-cut sticker lettering". Wikipedia (via search) also notes "hand-painted signs". [SS] — [Typeroom](https://www.typeroom.eu/content/board-danfo-lagos-iconic-bus-has-its-own-font); [Wikipedia: Danfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danfo)
- Danfo art draws on "text and geometric icons often seen on Danfo buses". Michael Umodit (2018) paints "patterns and words" and symbols on an old danfo. [SS] — [Africanews, 8 Sep 2018](https://www.africanews.com/amp/2018/09/08/nigerian-artists-draw-inspiration-from-chaotic-transport-system); [Discover Lagos](https://www.discoverlagos.ng/post/lagos-yellow-buses-danfo-are-iconic-worldwide)

**Regulatory status of decoration (conflicting)**
- An RPUBLC essay states that "the Lagos State Road Traffic Law 2021 prohibits the use of slogans, stickers, painting, and photos on commercial vehicles". A separate search found **no** such statutory provision. Enforcement in practice is undocumented. [SS] — [RPUBLC](https://old.rpublc.com/?p=17169); search for corroboration returned only LASAA permit and union-sticker rules (see §4).

### Inferences
- **Folk, not official:** slogans are personal and religious expression and the strongest "authentically Lagos" cue an original game can generate freely. The safe approach is to write *new* lines in the documented registers:
  - short Christian or Muslim invocations, with Muslim lines included for balance (none was documented in reachable sources);
  - Yoruba proverbs and virtues (patience, destiny, family);
  - Pidgin wit about money, time and hustle;
  - English motivational sayings;
  - gratitude to a parent or sponsor.
  - Native-speaker review of Yoruba lines is needed; the article's glosses look loose (e.g. *Suru lere* reads more literally as "patience is profit").
- **Production technique to imitate:** flat single-colour cut-vinyl letters (usually black or contrast colour on yellow), with slightly irregular, hand-cut spacing and slight skew. Add occasional hand-painted lines and small geometric icons. The Afrotype/Abu provenance confirms cut vinyl as a primary method.
- **Placement** (rear window or band, front header, side panels, driver's door) is undocumented in reachable text. Artists should treat it as a visual-reference task (see the S5 Commons photo already in the repo), not a sourced rule.
- If the 2021 restriction claim is real, slogans would technically be non-compliant but widespread. The game can present them as vernacular without implying legality either way.

### Gaps
- "Mummy's Blessing", "God Dey" and "Lagos no dey carry last" were not found in any source. Do not cite them as documented.
- Islamic inscriptions (e.g. Qur'anic phrases, "Allahu Akbar") are not documented in reachable sources, although Lagos is religiously mixed. This needs verification.
- No source located lettering by area of the bus. No catalogue of slogans was reachable: Agbiboa 2017 and 2023 were paywalled or blocked, and the BellaNaija page itself was blocked.
- The economics and work practices of bus-stop lettering artists (cost, tools, turnaround) were not found.

## 3. Typography: Afrotype's Danfo, Danfo Std, and the Google Fonts family

### Takeaway
**Danfo on Google Fonts is OFL-1.1** (SIL Open Font License), with designers credited as "Afrotype, Seyi Olusanya, Eyiyemi Adegbite, David Udoh, Mirko Velimirović". It was added on 2024-03-15. It is a single variable font with a custom **ELSH axis (0–100)**, classified SERIF/DISPLAY ("tuscan serif"), and its licence declares **no Reserved Font Name**. It can legally be bundled in a commercial or free game if the copyright notice and OFL text ship with it and the font is not sold on its own. The 2018 predecessor **Danfo Std** (Dá Design Studio) is a different release with unknown terms; do not use it.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Legal use in the game:** the Google Fonts/Afrotype OFL build can be embedded in the Godot app and used for in-game decals and UI headings. Ship OFL.txt (with its copyright line) in the app's licences screen. Do not sell the font file separately. Baked livery textures rendered with it are "documents created using the font" and are not themselves bound by OFL. There is no Reserved Font Name, but renaming a modified version (e.g. "Danfo Game Cut") is still good practice. Credit Afrotype and the authors per the OFL; do not imply their endorsement.
- **Authenticity caution:** using one tidy digital font for every slogan would look too uniform. Real lettering is hand-cut, so add per-letter jitter, uneven baselines and mixed letter sizes, and combine with original hand-lettered decals. The three ELSH masters (0/50/100 cut-shape styles) give natural variety.
- **Avoid:** Danfo Std (2018, Dá Design Studio) has unknown licence terms and is superseded. Avoid any "danfo-style" commercial font without a clear licence.
- **OFL alternatives** for supporting lettering, if Danfo is used for hero lines: heavy condensed grotesque or slab display families on Google Fonts. These were not researched here; check each family's OFL file.

### Gaps
- The afrotype.com/danfo minisite (field visits, photography, sketches) and fonts.google.com were blocked. The axis name ELSH ("element shape" in the Google axis registry) was not verified from a reachable page.
- Glyph coverage was not inspected. In particular, it is unknown whether Danfo has lowercase letters or only capitals, and whether it includes the Yoruba diacritics (ẹ, ọ, ṣ, tone marks). SSA coverage suggests the diacritics are present, but this is unverified.
- Danfo Std's licence terms are unknown.

## 4. Official marks: number plates, windscreen documents, union marks, route IDs, and what to fictionalise

### Takeaway
Official marks fall into three groups:
- the **number plate** (white with **red** characters for commercial vehicles, blue for private, green for government; LGA-code + digits + series format; the Lagos slogan reportedly "Centre of Excellence");
- **windscreen documents** (vehicle licence, roadworthiness certificate, hackney permit; LASAA mobile-advert permit stickers for branded vehicles);
- **levy/union tickets** (state consolidated levy tax cards; NURTW/LG daily tickets).

All of these are government or union documents and should be **fictionalised** in the game, using invented designs, codes and union names. Painted route body tags were proposed in 2017, but I found no evidence they became standard.

### Cited Findings
**Number plates**
- Nigerian plates: commercial vehicles use red lettering instead of blue; government plates are green. The 2011 revision moved the letters to the start. [SS] — [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Nigeria)
- Private plates are "a white background with blue letters and numbers"; commercial plates are for vehicles carrying people or goods (taxis, school buses, cargo vans). [SS] — [Zikoko](https://www.zikoko.com/citizen/different-types-of-nigerian-plate-numbers/)
- Format: three letters for the LGA (local government area; e.g. APP = Apapa), three digits, two series letters. The state name is written large at the top with the slogan "CENTRE OF EXCELLENCE" beneath. Source is a plate-generator site whose own disclaimer says its images are not real plates. [SS] — [openvan.camp](https://openvan.camp/en/license-plates/nigeria)
- Lagos is introducing special plates for persons with disabilities (headline only). [SS] — [NAN News](https://nannews.ng/lasg-to-introduce-number-plates-to-identify-people-with-disabilities/)

**Windscreen documents and permits**
- The vehicle licence is an annual document displayed on the windscreen. The roadworthiness certificate comes from computerised inspection. An offences table excerpted from the 2018 law lists "Driving a commercial vehicle without valid hackney permit" and "Non-display of hackney permit". [SS] — [Youverify offences table (third-party copy)](https://backoffice-api.tms.youverify.co/web/offenses); [Collaborative Media/WSCIJ](https://collaborativemedia.wscij.org/?p=4892)
- An older hackney permit fee schedule shows ₦4,475 for 1–21 passengers (historical; not current). [SS] — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=10964)
- LASAA (Lagos State Signage and Advertisement Agency) requires mobile-advert permits for branded vehicles. Unpermitted vehicles are grounded or impounded, and fake permit stickers are a criminal offence. [SS] — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=529967)

**Levy and union tickets**
- Jan 2022: Lagos announced an ₦800 daily consolidated levy for commercial transporters from 1 Feb 2022, with tax cards issued. NURTW said its own "national union ticket" was unaffected. Drivers cited "N200 as a local government ticket and N300 as an NURTW ticket". ICIR reported the scheme's launch slipped. [SS] — [Ripples Nigeria](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-pegs-danfo-levies-at-n800-daily-nurtw-reacts/); [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/lagos-n800-transport-levy-scheme-fails-to-kick-off-on-february-1/); [Radio Nigeria Lagos](https://radionigerialagos.gov.ng/transport-unions-in-lagos-to-pay-n800-naira-to-government-daily/)
- ICIR, via Zikoko: NURTW ticket collectors ("agberos") collect at least ₦3,000 per day from danfo drivers, based on 50 drivers in 21 LCDAs (local council development areas). Single investigation; 2022. [SS] — [Zikoko](https://www.zikoko.com/citizen/we-bet-you-didnt-know-the-nurtw-was-this-powerful-1/)
- Sep 2025: LASTMA warned unions against fixing union stickers on private vehicles in place of valid registration numbers. Vehicles with unauthorised stickers or identifiers face impoundment. [SS] — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=529967)

**Painted IDs and route tags**
- 2017: commuter-protection plates and body tags showing routes were proposed; whether they were implemented is unconfirmed. "Driver and 2 passengers" was painted by the driver's door (historical usage citation). [SS] — [Nairametrics, Aug 2017](https://nairametrics.com/2017/08/11/behold-ambodes-new-danfo-bus/); [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/danfo)

### Inferences

| Element | Status | Game treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 5. Interior: seating, decoration, floor, door, windows

### Takeaway
Reachable sources describe **stripped, re-seated interiors**: factory seats removed for wood or metal benches to reach about 14 passengers, extra people squeezed into the front seat, and exposed seat foam implied as the norm. Sliding doors are unreliable enough that one falling off mid-journey is a recounted anecdote. Sources on dashboard decoration (rosaries, Qur'an verses, toys), curtains, ceilings and sound systems were **not found**.

### Cited Findings
- Many old T3-based danfos have had factory seats stripped and replaced with wood or metal benches; a stripped interior is common. Seats are added to fit about 14 passengers, and extra passengers are squeezed into the front seat. [SS] — [The Autopian](https://theautopian.com/why-nigerias-danfo-bus-drivers-might-be-the-craziest-and-most-skilled-drivers-on-the-planet); [The Autopian comments](https://theautopian.com/why-nigerias-danfo-bus-drivers-might-be-the-craziest-and-most-skilled-drivers-on-the-planet/comment-page-1)
- A Lagos writer describes an unusually tidy danfo with padded, clean seats and "no exposed foam", presented as the exception. [SS] — [Lagos Convo](https://lagosconvo.substack.com/p/the-day-a-danfo-driver-surprised)
- A passenger account: the sliding door fell off while the bus was moving, startling the passenger beside it. [SS] — [WATHI, "Danfo tales in the big city"](https://wathi.org/initiative-lagos/contributions-initiatives-lagos/danfo-tales-in-the-big-city)
- Capacity is typically 14–18 seats. [SS] — [Wikipedia: Danfo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danfo)
- Mar 2024 VIS roadworthiness checks targeted brakes, wipers, lights, shocks and tyres. Fifty commercial buses were impounded at Costain, Ikorodu Road, Ojuelegba, Yaba, Oyingbo, Ikeja and Ojodu-Berger. [SS] — [PM News, 27 Mar 2024](https://pmnewsnigeria.com/2024/03/27/lagos-vis-impounds-50-rickety-commercial-vehicles/); [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/lagos-vis-impounds-50-rickety-commercial-buses/)

### Inferences
- **Interior kit for the look library:** an improvised bench grid with worn vinyl or leatherette over foam (split seams, taped repairs, exposed foam corners); one bus with bare plank seats for a "battered" tier; a tidy reupholstered set for the "fresh" tier. The documented range runs from bare benches to the exceptional clean padded seats.
- Add a front bench shared by the driver and extra passengers, and a conductor position near the sliding door. Conductor placement is not documented here; see the operations research.
- Door as a wear state: rattling, misaligned or rope-tied. This is an inference from the door-failure anecdote and VIS defect lists.
- Dashboard trinkets, prayer items, stickers and speakers are plausible personalisation cosmetics, but **undocumented here**. Get local reviewer confirmation before treating any as typical, and include both Christian and Muslim items if any are used.

### Gaps
- No reachable source on dashboard decoration, curtains, ceiling liners, flooring (rubber mat, plywood, bare metal), handholds, window latches or audio installations. This needs photo reference or field input.

## 6. Exterior details, wear, and personalisation

### Takeaway
The documented exterior identity is a flat-front VW Transporter-type minibus (later Hiace, Nissan and L300 types) in yellow with black bands, often "rickety": squealing brakes, bald tyres, smoky exhausts and corroded bodies, all targets of 2024 VIS crackdowns. Sources were **silent** on horns, chrome, bullbars, mud flaps, roof racks and lights as personalisation, so these remain design hypotheses.

### Cited Findings
- Vehicle base:
  - Wikipedia (T3) lists the "Volkswagen Danfo or Faragon" name in Nigeria; T3 production ran May 1979 – June 2002. [SS] — [Wikipedia: Volkswagen Type 2 (T3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Type_2_(T3))
  - The Autopian links danfo durability to "the unkillable nature of the T3 Volkswagen Transporters that are used as Danfo". [SS] — [The Autopian](https://theautopian.com/why-nigerias-danfo-bus-drivers-might-be-the-craziest-and-most-skilled-drivers-on-the-planet/comment-page-1)
- Condition descriptors: rickety and dilapidated, with squealing brakes, bald tyres and smoky exhausts. [SS] — [Guardian NG, "Danfo… The Naked Realities"](https://guardian.ng/life/danfo-the-naked-realities-of-nigerian-automobiles/); [RPUBLC](https://old.rpublc.com/?p=17169)
- VIS chief Akin-George Fashola called dilapidated vehicles "ticking time bombs", citing worn-out tyres, faulty brakes and "corroded body structures" (undated). In Feb 2024 VIS said its year's focus was commercial transporters, specifically yellow danfo and korope. Some drivers resisted, damaging VIS patrol vans. [SS] — [Ripples Nigeria](https://ripplesnigeria.com/lagos-targets-ticking-time-bombs-as-it-intensifies-crackdown-on-rickety-vehicles); [Channels TV, 19 Feb 2024](https://www.channelstv.com/2024/02/19/vis-vows-crackdown-on-danfo-other-commercial-transporters)
- BusinessDay reported that 60% of vehicles on state roads run with faulty brakes (date unclear). [SS] — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=248267)
- Danfo persist despite roadworthiness rules, partly through union influence and payments to officials, per a WSCIJ (Wole Soyinka Centre for Investigative Journalism) analysis. [SS] — [WSCIJ Collaborative Media, "Forever With Danfo"](https://collaborativemedia.wscij.org/?p=4892)
- T3 general corrosion points: wheel arches and the area around the windscreen. This is generic buyer guidance, not Lagos-specific. [SS] — [Classics World](https://classicsworld.co.uk/classic-car-price-guide/affordable-vw-campers-buyers-guide/)

### Inferences
**Wear-mask library (inference):**
- rust blooms at wheel arches, sills, the windscreen surround and door bottoms (T3 weak points);
- scrape lines at bumper height on the sides from close-quarters traffic;
- repaint patches in off-shade yellow over filler;
- black bands that do not line up across replaced panels;
- peeling or partly removed vinyl letters (ghost outlines);
- sun fade on the roof and bonnet;
- sooty exhaust staining at the rear;
- cracked or missing lens covers.

**Fresh-to-battered spectrum as three art tiers:**
1. **Fresh/restored:** even yellow, crisp bands, new vinyl slogans, intact lights, clean wheels.
2. **Working:** fade, a few dents and patches, one mismatched panel, a worn door.
3. **Battered:** heavy rust, multiple shades, missing trim, cracked glass, smoke.

The repo's existing "maintained / patched / decorated" idea fits this.

**Personalisation cosmetics:** horn type, chrome bumpers or guards, extra lamps, mud flaps with original text, roof rack, wheel trims and slogan sets are plausible game cosmetics. They are **not sourced as documented Lagos practice** in this pass, so validate with Lagos reviewers or photo reference before calling them typical.

### Gaps
- Multi-tone or air horns, chrome bullbars, mud-flap text, roof racks, mirror condition and lighting mods: no reachable source.
- No source on how owners name their buses beyond slogans.

## 7. Representation in art, design, merchandise and games; essential cues and common mistakes

### Takeaway
The danfo is a well-established Lagos art and design icon. Examples include Emeka Ogboh's danfo sound sculpture and a Horizn Studios luggage collaboration in a danfo colourway; "Mr Danfo" (Seidougha Linus Eyimiegha); Michael Umodit's painted danfo; Afebuame's "Time Na Money" painting; and Afrotype's typeface. Several Nigerian games already use it: Danfo Reloaded II (2013), Maliyo's Danfo Racer (early access Feb 2022), ChopUp's 2D Danfo, and a viral "Lagos Run"/"Danfo Run" browser game. No source documents Lagosians' views on what outsiders get wrong; that remains a reviewer task.

### Cited Findings
- **Emeka Ogboh:** a Berlin-based artist inspired by "the iconic yellow VW buses—known as Danfos". He built a danfo that plays recordings of drivers' and passengers' conversations. Horizn Studios × Ogboh made limited-edition luggage including a cabin case in a yellow danfo colourway, plus t-shirts and stickers. [SS] — [visitBerlin](https://www.visitberlin.de/en/node/2287898); [Horizn Studios (FR)](https://horizn-studios.com/fr/pages/emeka-ogboh); [Artforum](https://www.artforum.com/columns/ayodeji-rotinwa-on-emeka-ogboh-243546/)
- **Mr Danfo:** Seidougha Linus Eyimiegha, a multimedia artist, has a Danfo series referencing "the symbolic motifs attached to the Lagos 'Danfo' buses" (ThisDay, 10 Apr 2022). [SS] — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2022/04/10/mr-danfo-and-his-sketchy-impressions-of-lagos-street-stories/); [Art Twenty One](https://arttwentyone.ng/artists/70-seidougha-linus-eyimiegha/biography/)

### Inferences
**Essential cues, synthesised across sources:**
- the right vehicle: a flat-front, boxy, T3-era van silhouette rather than a modern rounded van or a school bus;
- deep warm yellow with black horizontal bands;
- hand-cut lettering with religious or proverbial lines;
- visible wear and repair;
- crowded improvised seating;
- an open or unreliable sliding door;
- Lagos street context.

**Likely outsider mistakes:**
- showroom-clean buses;
- lemon or school-bus yellow with no bands;
- modern Sprinter-type silhouettes;
- generic racing decals in place of slogans;
- English-only text;
- a single Christian register only;
- uniform digital lettering.

These are reasoned from the cited material plus the repo's S5 photo analysis. **No Lagosian-opinion source was found**, so test them with Lagos-familiar reviewers, as the repo's audience-segment plan already proposes.

**Precedent:** earlier Nigerian danfo games exist. The new game's look library should aim to be distinctive (wear tiers, authentic cut-vinyl slogans, interior detail) rather than relying on yellow alone.
